## Definition

A **lapel** is the folded section on the front of a jacket or coat, running from the collar down toward the fastening. It is a defining detail of tailored garments, including blazers, suit jackets, and tuxedo jackets.

The shape, width, angle, and material of a lapel influence the overall effect of a garment. A lapel can make a jacket look relaxed, classic, formal, dramatic, or modern before color and accessories are even considered.

## What does lapel mean in fashion?

In fashion, a lapel is not simply a decorative flap. It frames the upper body, directs attention toward the face and chest, and gives a jacket much of its visual structure.

On a [blazer](/en/glossar/blazer/), the lapel often creates the polished, recognizably tailored look. On a [suit jacket](/en/glossar/anzugjacke/), it contributes to formality and professional presence. On a [tuxedo](/en/glossar/smoking/), the lapel is commonly finished in a contrasting shiny fabric, making it an especially important eveningwear detail.

## The main types of lapels

### Notch lapel

A notch lapel has a visible V-shaped opening where the collar meets the lapel. It is one of the most familiar options on blazers, sports jackets, and suits.

Its effect is usually balanced and versatile. A notch lapel can work well in business settings, for smart casual dressing, and as part of a classic wardrobe.

### Peak lapel

A peak lapel has pointed ends that angle upward toward the shoulders. This creates a more directional, assertive line than a notch lapel.

Peak lapels are often associated with more formal tailoring, double-breasted jackets, and occasionwear. They can make the shoulder area appear more pronounced, so their scale matters within the full outfit.

### Shawl lapel

A shawl lapel, often connected with a [shawl collar](/en/glossar/schalkragen/), has no notch or pointed peak. Instead, it curves continuously from the collar into the front opening.

This soft, flowing shape is especially common on dinner jackets and some formal garments. It can look refined and calm, particularly when made in satin, velvet, or another material with a subtle sheen.

## Lapel versus lapel collar

The term _lapel_ refers to the folded front section itself. A [lapel collar](/en/glossar/reverskragen/) describes the combined collar and lapel construction of a garment.

In everyday language, the two terms are often used interchangeably. For styling, however, it is useful to notice both: the collar frames the neck and face, while the lapel shapes the front of the torso and the jacket's overall line.

## Why lapels matter for style and proportion

Lapels are small in area but high in visual impact. Because they sit close to the face, their shape helps determine whether a jacket feels sharp, soft, restrained, or expressive.

Pay attention to:

- **Lapel width:** A moderate width is often versatile. Narrow or wide lapels make a stronger style statement.
- **Lapel angle:** Upward-pointing peak lapels create more visual energy than a classic notch.
- **Lapel length:** A longer, open lapel line can support a sense of vertical length.
- **Material contrast:** Satin or velvet lapels draw more attention than a lapel made from the same fabric as the jacket.
- **Scale:** The lapel should feel in proportion to the jacket, shoulders, and wearer, rather than looking accidentally oversized or too slight.

These details work together with the garment's [cut](/en/glossar/schnitt/), [fit](/en/glossar/passform/), and [proportion](/en/glossar/proportion/).

## How to choose a lapel in practice

There is no universally best lapel. The right choice depends on the occasion, the jacket's construction, and the impression you want to create.

A notch lapel is a reliable choice for an everyday blazer or a versatile suit. A peak lapel can be effective when you want more formality or a stronger tailored presence. A shawl lapel is often best suited to eveningwear, particularly when the garment has a smooth, elegant finish.

When trying on a jacket, check whether the lapels lie flat and symmetrical. They should not pull away from the chest, buckle at the collar, or sit unevenly. Such issues can indicate that the jacket's fit, shoulder line, or button position is not working well for you.

## Common misconceptions

- **Misconception: A lapel is the same as a collar.**  
  **Better:** The collar sits around the neck, while the lapel is the folded front section below it. Together, they form an important part of a tailored jacket's construction.

- **Misconception: Only formal suits have lapels.**  
  **Better:** Lapels also appear on blazers, coats, leather jackets, blazer dresses, and other tailored garments.

- **Misconception: Wider lapels are always more flattering.**  
  **Better:** Width alone does not determine the result. The best effect comes from a lapel that relates well to the jacket's cut, shoulder width, and overall proportions.

- **Misconception: Lapels are only decorative.**  
  **Better:** They are both structural and visual. Lapels shape the front opening of the garment and strongly influence its style message.

## Quick fitting check for a jacket lapel

When assessing a blazer, suit jacket, or coat, look for the following:

- The collar sits close to the neck without a visible gap.
- Both lapels lie flat and look symmetrical.
- The lapel edges are smooth, without twisting or pulling.
- The lapel width feels balanced with the shoulders and the jacket's silhouette.
- The material and finish suit the occasion, especially for formal eveningwear.
