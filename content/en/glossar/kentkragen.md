## Definition

A **Kent collar** is a classic shirt collar with pointed tips and a moderate spread between them. It is a familiar, versatile collar shape found on many dress shirts and works especially well for businesswear, smart casual outfits, and formal daytime looks.

The Kent collar creates a neat, balanced frame around the face without appearing as wide or pronounced as a [Spread Collar](/en/glossar/haifischkragen/). Because of its restrained proportions, it is often an easy choice when you want a shirt to look polished rather than highly fashion-led.

## What Does a Kent Collar Look Like?

A Kent collar sits flat around the neck and has two pointed collar ends that angle downward toward the chest. The opening between the points is moderate. This gives it a clean, classic line that is neither extremely narrow nor broadly open.

Details vary from shirt to shirt. Some Kent collars have longer points, firmer interlining, or a slightly wider spread. These differences affect whether the collar feels more formal, more relaxed, or more distinctly traditional.

A Kent collar is usually part of a [Shirt Collar](/en/glossar/hemdkragen/) construction and is commonly paired with a front [Button Placket](/en/glossar/knopfleiste/).

## Kent Collar vs Spread Collar

The key difference is the distance between the collar points.

| Collar type                                  | Shape                               | Visual effect                       | Typical use                                         |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Kent collar                                  | Pointed tips with a moderate spread | Classic, contained, balanced        | Business, smart casual, suits, everyday shirts      |
| [Spread collar](/en/glossar/haifischkragen/) | Pointed tips with a wider opening   | More open, broader, more expressive | Business tailoring, wider tie knots, dressier looks |

A Kent collar tends to look quieter and more traditional. A spread collar has more horizontal presence and can feel more contemporary or more formal, depending on the shirt and styling.

## How to Wear a Kent Collar

A Kent collar works with a tie, without a tie, and under tailoring. Its adaptability makes it useful in a well-planned wardrobe.

### With a tie

The moderate collar opening generally suits smaller to medium tie knots. Choose a tie knot that sits cleanly between the collar points without forcing them apart. With a [Suit](/en/glossar/anzug/) or [Sports Jacket](/en/glossar/sakko/), a Kent collar creates a dependable, professional line.

### Open at the neck

Worn without a tie, the Kent collar looks tidy and understated. Leave one or two buttons open according to the occasion and your comfort. The collar should sit evenly rather than collapsing or lifting away from the neck.

### In smart casual outfits

A Kent collar shirt can make jeans, chinos, knitwear, or an unstructured blazer look more intentional. Combine a crisp cotton shirt with [Chinos](/en/glossar/chinos/) and [Loafers](/en/glossar/loafer/) for a relaxed but polished outfit.

## Why Collar Shape Matters

A collar is close to the face, so it strongly influences face framing and the overall message of a shirt. The Kent collar creates clear vertical points at the neckline while maintaining a calm, familiar structure.

The right collar should work with your preferred level of formality, the shape of the garment, and the role the shirt plays in your wardrobe. A high-quality [Fit](/en/glossar/passform/) matters just as much as collar style. If the neck is too tight, too loose, or the collar does not sit smoothly, even a well-chosen collar will look less polished.

## Practical Checks When Buying a Kent Collar Shirt

- Make sure you can comfortably place a finger between your neck and the closed collar.
- Check that both collar points lie flat and look even.
- Wear the shirt with the jacket, knitwear, or tie you would normally pair with it.
- Look at the collar from the front and side. It should frame the face without crowding the neck.
- Consider fabric and construction. A crisp [Cotton](/en/glossar/baumwolle/) shirt usually gives a sharper collar line than a soft, fluid fabric.
- Choose a collar scale that feels proportionate to your face, neck, and overall build.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **A Kent collar is only for formal shirts.**  
  Not necessarily. It is a classic option for formal shirts, but it also works well in smart casual wardrobes when worn open.

- **Every pointed collar is a Kent collar.**  
  Not exactly. Point length, spread, collar height, construction, and fastening details can create noticeably different collar styles.

- **The collar alone determines whether a shirt suits you.**  
  Collar shape matters, but so do shirt fit, fabric, color, neckline styling, and the surrounding outfit.
