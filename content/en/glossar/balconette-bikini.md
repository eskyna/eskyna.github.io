## Definition

A **balconette bikini** is a bikini top inspired by the shape of a [balconette bra](/en/glossar/balconette-bh/). It has a relatively low, open neckline, straps placed farther toward the shoulders, and cups designed to lift and gently shape the bust from underneath.

The result is a defined, softly rounded décolletage with less coverage at the top of the bust than many full-coverage swim tops. Depending on the design, a balconette bikini may include underwires, molded cups, removable pads, side support, or a tie or clasp fastening at the back.

## What Does a Balconette Bikini Look Like?

A balconette bikini top often has a straight or softly curved cup edge that runs across the upper bust. Its shoulder straps are usually wider apart than those on a classic triangle bikini, leaving the neckline and collarbone area more open.

The cup shape creates a more lifted look than a simple fabric triangle. This makes the balconette style a popular option for swimwear that feels feminine and polished while still offering practical support.

## Effect on Shape and Outfit Impact

The main visual effect of a balconette bikini is lift from below. It can create a more centered, rounded bust shape while framing the upper chest and shoulders.

Because the neckline is open and horizontal in feeling, the style can draw attention across the collarbone area. It pairs naturally with high-waisted bikini bottoms, a [sarong](/en/glossar/sarong/), linen trousers, or an open shirt for a refined beach or poolside outfit.

A balconette bikini can look understated in a matte neutral or more statement-making in a vivid color, floral print, or textured fabric. The overall effect depends on the scale of the cups, the strap placement, and how much contrast the swimwear creates against the skin.

## How to Choose a Balconette Bikini Top

Start with fit rather than cup size alone. Swimwear brands use different sizing systems, and stretchy swim fabrics can feel very different from everyday lingerie.

Look for these details:

- **A secure underband:** The band should sit level around the torso and feel firm enough to provide support.
- **Cups that contain the bust:** The cup edge should lie smoothly without cutting in, gaping, or allowing the bust to spill over.
- **Adjustable straps:** Adjustable straps make it easier to refine the fit, but they should not have to carry all the bust support.
- **The right cup depth:** Shallow cups may flatten or compress the bust, while cups that are too deep can wrinkle or gap.
- **Comfort in movement:** Raise your arms, bend, sit, and move naturally. The top should remain in place without pinching or shifting.

If you usually benefit from a bra-sized fit, use the same principles you would when assessing [bra fit](/en/glossar/bh-passform/). Measuring your underbust and bust can also provide a useful starting point. See [How to Measure Bra Size](/en/glossar/bh-groesse-messen/) for the basic process.

## How to Style a Balconette Bikini

A balconette bikini works well when the rest of the beach look supports its more structured, feminine line.

For an easy daytime look, combine a simple balconette top with matching bottoms, a lightweight linen shirt, and flat sandals. For a more polished holiday outfit, choose a solid-color bikini with high-waisted bottoms, a flowing cover-up, and a textured bag.

The style also works well with swimwear separates. A plain balconette top can balance printed bottoms, while a patterned top can become the focal point when paired with a simple solid bottom. Keep the scale and color story intentional so the outfit feels coordinated rather than busy.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **A balconette bikini is the same as a strapless bikini.**  
  Not necessarily. Balconette bikini tops usually have shoulder straps, although some designs may offer removable straps. A [strapless bra or bandeau bra](/en/glossar/traegerloser-bh/) is specifically designed to be worn without visible shoulder straps.

- **Every balconette bikini has underwires.**  
  No. Some styles use underwires for structure, while others create a similar neckline and shape with seams, padding, elastic, or supportive fabric.

- **More padding always means more support.**  
  Padding can affect coverage and shape, but a supportive fit mainly comes from the band, cup construction, and secure placement of the top.

- **A balconette cut suits only one bust shape.**  
  The style can work for many people, but the best version depends on cup depth, wire shape, strap placement, and personal comfort. Trying more than one construction is often more useful than judging the style by name alone.

## Practical Check Before You Buy

- The underband stays level when you lift your arms.
- The cups sit smoothly against the bust without empty space or overflow.
- The center and sides of the top feel stable rather than loose or twisting.
- The straps are comfortable and do not dig into the shoulders.
- You can move, swim, and sit without repeatedly adjusting the cups.
- The neckline gives the coverage and lift you want for the setting.
