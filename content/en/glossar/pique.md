## Definition

**Piqué** is a textured fabric characterized by a raised, often geometric surface. The name may refer to a woven or knitted construction, but in everyday fashion it is most commonly associated with the durable, lightly structured fabric used for polo shirts.

The visible pattern can resemble small waffles, honeycombs, or diamonds. This texture gives piqué more depth and definition than a smooth fabric, affecting both comfort and the overall impression of a garment.

## What does piqué fabric look and feel like?

Piqué has a subtly three-dimensional surface rather than a flat finish. Depending on its construction and fiber content, it can feel crisp and dry, soft and flexible, or slightly firm. Cotton piqué is especially familiar: it has a casual, sporty character but usually appears more polished than a basic cotton T-shirt.

The structure creates a small amount of visual texture, which can make solid colors look more nuanced. It also means that a piqué garment often holds its shape more clearly than lightweight [jersey](/en/glossar/jersey/).

## Piqué fabric in polo shirts and everyday wardrobes

Piqué is a classic choice for polo shirts because it combines ease with a neat surface. A cotton piqué polo is often suitable for relaxed work settings, travel, weekends, and smart casual occasions. The collar and structured fabric give it more presence than a T-shirt, while the knit construction can still allow comfortable movement.

For a polished everyday outfit, combine a well-fitting piqué polo with chinos, tailored trousers, or a simple skirt. Its visual effect depends on the [fit](/en/glossar/passform/), color, collar shape, and condition of the fabric. A stretched-out collar, faded color, or distorted surface can make an otherwise good piece appear less refined.

Piqué can also appear in dresses, skirts, knitwear, and more formal textile applications. In these cases, the key feature remains the same: a raised surface that adds structure without relying on a print.

## Piqué compared with jersey

Both piqué and jersey can be knitted and comfortable, but they create different effects.

| Feature         | Piqué                                   | Jersey                               |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Surface         | Raised, textured, often geometric       | Usually smooth and flat              |
| Visual effect   | Structured, sporty, more defined        | Soft, casual, fluid                  |
| Typical use     | Polo shirts, structured tops, dresses   | T-shirts, tops, dresses, base layers |
| Shape retention | Often more stable, depending on quality | Can be softer and more draped        |
| Comfort         | Can feel airy and substantial           | Often feels softer and stretchier    |

Neither is automatically better. The right choice depends on whether you want a garment to look more relaxed and fluid or clearer and more structured.

## What to check when buying piqué

Not every piqué fabric performs the same way. Look beyond the surface pattern and assess the garment as a whole.

- **Check the fiber content.** Cotton piqué is common, while blends with [elastane](/en/glossar/elasthan/) or synthetic fibers may change stretch, recovery, drying time, and care needs.
- **Assess fabric weight.** Very thin piqué can look less substantial, while heavy piqué feels more structured and may be warmer.
- **Look at the surface closely.** The texture should appear even, without pulled loops, thinning areas, or irregular distortion.
- **Test shape recovery.** Gently stretch the fabric and see whether it returns to its original form.
- **Inspect collar and cuffs.** On polo shirts, these areas receive frequent wear and strongly influence how polished the garment looks.
- **Consider color and texture together.** The raised surface can make color appear softer or more dimensional. This is useful when building a considered [color palette](/en/glossar/farbpalette/).

## Care for piqué garments

Always follow the care label, as the right method depends on the fiber blend and construction. Cotton piqué is often machine washable, but washing it inside out can help reduce abrasion on the textured surface. Avoid overly hot drying when possible, since heat can contribute to shrinkage or affect shape retention.

If the fabric starts to look fuzzy or worn, check whether friction from bags, belts, or repeated washing is affecting the surface. Good [care](/en/glossar/pflege/) supports the texture that makes piqué visually distinctive.

## Common misunderstandings

- **Piqué is only for polo shirts.**  
  Polo shirts are the best-known use, but piqué can also be found in dresses, skirts, tops, and formal textile constructions.

- **All piqué fabrics are breathable.**  
  The textured construction can support airflow, but breathability depends on fiber content, density, fabric weight, and garment fit.

- **Piqué always looks sporty.**  
  It often has a sporty association, yet color, cut, styling, and garment type can make it look minimalist, classic, casual, or refined.

## ESKYNA Key Line

Piqué adds quiet structure to an outfit: more defined than jersey, but still practical enough for everyday wear.

## Advisory View

Choose piqué when you want comfort with a clearer surface and slightly more visual presence. It is especially useful in a wardrobe built around versatile, durable [basics](/en/glossar/basic/) that can move easily between casual and smart casual settings.
