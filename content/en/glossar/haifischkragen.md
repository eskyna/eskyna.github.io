## Definition

A **spread collar** is a [shirt collar](/en/glossar/hemdkragen/) whose points are positioned farther apart than those of a classic point collar. This wider opening creates a more open, horizontal frame at the neck and face.

Spread collars appear on shirts for business, formal, and smart casual settings. Depending on the collar’s construction, point length, and degree of spread, the effect can range from quietly classic to distinctly tailored.

## Spread Collar Explained Simply

Look at the space between the two collar points. If that opening is visibly broad rather than narrow and vertical, the shirt has a spread collar.

The wider angle changes the visual balance of the upper body. It can make the collar area appear more open and present, especially when worn with a jacket. It also provides room for a larger tie knot, which is why spread collars are a common choice for tailored business looks.

A spread collar is not one rigid shape. Some versions are only moderately wider than a [Kent collar](/en/glossar/kentkragen/), while others are cut very wide. For that reason, it is useful to judge the individual shirt rather than rely on the name alone.

## How a Spread Collar Affects an Outfit

A collar is close to the face, so it contributes strongly to [face framing](/en/glossar/gesichtsrahmung/) and overall [appearance](/en/glossar/auftreten/). A spread collar tends to introduce a wider, more horizontal line at the neckline.

Its effect depends on several details:

- **Collar spread:** A moderate spread is versatile, while a very wide spread looks more expressive and formal.
- **Collar structure:** Firm interlining creates a crisp, controlled result. Softer construction feels more relaxed.
- **Shirt fit:** The collar should sit cleanly around the neck without gaping, pulling, or standing away from the shirt.
- **Tie knot:** A fuller knot usually fills the space more convincingly than a very narrow knot.
- **Jacket lapels:** A spread collar often works well with the open V shape created by a [suit jacket](/en/glossar/anzugjacke/) or [blazer](/en/glossar/blazer/).

## When to Wear a Spread Collar

A spread collar is a flexible option for many occasions:

- **Business and meetings:** Pair a crisp spread-collar shirt with a [suit](/en/glossar/anzug/) and tie for a polished, professional look.
- **Smart casual outfits:** Wear it open with chinos, dark jeans, or tailored trousers. The result is often more refined than a casual collarless top.
- **Events and dinners:** In a fine fabric and a clean fit, a spread collar can work well for [cocktail attire](/en/glossar/cocktail-attire/) or an elevated dinner look.
- **Without a tie:** Choose a collar that holds its shape naturally and keep the neckline intentional rather than overly open.

## Spread Collar vs. Point Collar

The main difference is the distance between the collar points.

| Feature                        | Spread Collar                      | Point Collar                        |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Distance between collar points | Wider                              | Narrower                            |
| Overall visual line            | More open and horizontal           | More vertical and narrow            |
| Tie knot                       | Often suits medium to fuller knots | Often suits smaller to medium knots |
| Style impression               | Tailored, open, versatile          | Classic, restrained, traditional    |

Neither collar is inherently better. The right choice depends on the shirt’s purpose, your preferred [style](/en/glossar/stil/), and how the collar works with your face, neck, and jacket.

## Practical Check Before Buying

When trying on a spread-collar shirt, check the following:

- The collar sits smoothly around your neck when buttoned.
- The collar points lie flat rather than curling or lifting.
- There is enough room for a tie knot if you plan to wear one.
- The open collar looks neat when worn without a tie.
- The shirt works with your existing jackets, [ties](/en/glossar/krawatte/), and trousers.
- The fabric and collar construction fit the occasions you actually dress for.

A good collar is not only attractive on a hanger. It should remain comfortable and hold its line while you sit, turn your head, and move through the day.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **A spread collar is only for formal wear.**  
  A structured spread collar can be formal, but many versions work easily in smart casual wardrobes when worn open.

- **Every spread collar needs a Windsor knot.**  
  A fuller knot can suit the wider opening, but the exact collar shape and tie fabric matter more than following one fixed rule.

- **A wider collar always flatters everyone in the same way.**  
  Collar choice is personal. Face proportions, neck length, shoulder line, and desired effect all influence whether a particular spread feels balanced.

## ESKYNA Key Line

A spread collar creates a wider, more open frame at the neck. Choose the degree of spread and structure that supports your face, tie choice, and real wardrobe needs.
