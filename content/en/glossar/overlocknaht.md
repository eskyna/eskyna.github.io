## Definition

An **overlock seam** is a machine-made seam that stitches fabric pieces together while wrapping thread around the raw fabric edge. This helps prevent fraying and creates a flexible, tidy finish on the inside of a garment.

The seam is usually made with an overlock machine, often called a serger. It is especially common in T-shirts, leggings, underwear, knitwear, and other garments made from stretchy fabrics.

## What Does an Overlock Seam Look Like?

Turn a garment inside out and look at the seam allowance. An overlock seam typically has several threads looping over the fabric edge, creating a narrow, enclosed edge rather than a plain line of stitching.

Depending on the machine setup, the seam may look fine and light or more substantial. The visible thread loops should lie evenly along the edge without loose sections, puckering, or fabric caught unevenly in the stitching.

An overlock seam is different from a [flatlock seam](/en/glossar/flatlocknaht/), which is constructed to sit flatter against the body and is often visible as a decorative or functional detail in activewear.

## Why Overlock Seams Matter in Clothing

An overlock seam affects how a garment wears, moves, and holds up over time. It is not only an inside finish. In stretch clothing, it helps the fabric retain flexibility at stress points such as side seams, armholes, and inseams.

For everyday wardrobe decisions, inspect overlock seams when you assess [craftsmanship](/en/glossar/verarbeitung/) and [inner construction](/en/glossar/innenverarbeitung/). A well-made seam should support the fabric rather than pull it out of shape.

Overlock construction is particularly useful when:

- the garment is made from jersey, rib knit, or another stretch fabric
- the item needs to move comfortably with the body
- raw edges need a clean, efficient finish
- the garment has many curved or frequently stressed seams

## Overlock Seam and Fabric Type

Overlock seams work well with knit fabrics because the thread structure can stretch with the material. On a fitted jersey top or leggings, a rigid seam could snap when the fabric stretches. An overlock seam is better suited to this movement.

In woven garments, overlock stitching is also often used to finish individual seam allowances and stop them from fraying. However, a garment may use other finishing methods, such as a French seam, binding, or a felled seam, depending on the fabric, design, and intended level of finish.

The right seam treatment always depends on the garment's [material quality](/en/glossar/materialqualitaet/), weight, stretch, and use.

## How to Check an Overlock Seam Before Buying

A quick inside check can reveal whether the construction is clean and appropriate for the garment.

- **Look for even stitches:** Thread loops should be consistent, with no skipped stitches or visibly loose areas.
- **Gently stretch the seam:** On stretch fabrics, the seam should move with the fabric without threads breaking or becoming overly tight.
- **Check for puckering:** The fabric beside the seam should lie relatively smooth. Strong rippling can indicate incorrect seam tension.
- **Inspect stress points:** Look closely at underarms, crotches, side seams, and waistbands, where seams receive more strain.
- **Consider comfort:** Bulky, rough, or poorly positioned seams may irritate the skin, particularly in close-fitting garments.
- **Assess the whole garment:** A neat overlock seam alone does not guarantee quality. Check the fabric, [fit](/en/glossar/passform/), hems, and other seams as well.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: An overlock seam is only an edge finish.**  
  **Better:** It can finish a raw edge, but it can also join two or more fabric layers in the same process.

- **Misunderstanding: Overlock seams are a sign of poor quality.**  
  **Better:** They are a standard and useful construction method, especially for stretch clothing. Quality depends on execution and whether the seam suits the fabric and garment.

- **Misunderstanding: Every overlock seam should look identical.**  
  **Better:** Thread count, stitch width, fabric type, and the garment's purpose can change the appearance of an overlock seam.

## Overlock Seam vs. Flatlock Seam

An overlock seam encloses the edge of the fabric, which can create a small raised ridge on the inside. A flatlock seam is designed to lie flatter, often with the fabric edges meeting or overlapping rather than being enclosed in the same way.

For close-fitting sportswear, flatlock seams can reduce bulk and friction. For many T-shirts, knit tops, and casual garments, an overlock seam is a practical and widely used choice.

## ESKYNA Key Line

An overlock seam is a functional construction detail that helps clothing stay neat, flexible, and wearable, especially when fabric needs to stretch with the body.

## Advisory View

When evaluating a garment, treat the overlock seam as one useful quality clue rather than a rule on its own. Check whether it matches the fabric and intended movement, then consider it alongside the garment's [seam](/en/glossar/naht/), fit, comfort, and overall finish.
