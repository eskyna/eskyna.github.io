## Definition

**Dry cleaning** is a professional garment-cleaning process that uses liquid solvents rather than water. It is often recommended for delicate fabrics, structured tailoring, lined garments, and pieces with details that may shrink, distort, bleed, or lose their finish in a regular wash.

If a label says **dry clean only**, treat it as an important care instruction. It reflects how the complete garment is made, including its outer fabric, lining, seams, interfacing, buttons, embellishments, and dye.

## What Does Dry Cleaning Mean in Practice?

The term can be misleading because dry-cleaned clothes are not cleaned without liquid. Instead of water and detergent, a professional cleaner uses a suitable solvent and specialized equipment to remove soil while protecting sensitive materials and garment construction.

Dry cleaning is common for pieces such as tailored [suits](/en/glossar/anzug/), structured [blazers](/en/glossar/blazer/), coats, silk garments, delicate eveningwear, and some items made from wool, velvet, or fabrics with complex finishes. However, the fabric name alone does not decide the care method. A washable fiber can still be used in a garment that needs professional cleaning because of its cut, lining, or details.

The most reliable guide is the [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/) inside the garment.

## Why Dry Cleaning Matters When Buying Clothes

Garment care is part of wardrobe planning. A beautiful jacket may suit your style perfectly, but if it requires frequent professional cleaning and that does not fit your routine or budget, it may not be a practical purchase.

Before buying, consider:

- Whether the care label is realistic for your everyday life
- How often you expect to wear the piece
- Whether the garment is likely to be exposed to perspiration, makeup, food, rain, or public transport
- Whether you have access to a trusted professional cleaner
- How care costs influence the item's [cost per wear](/en/glossar/cost-per-wear/)

This does not make dry-clean-only clothing impractical. It simply helps you choose intentionally. A well-made occasion dress or tailored coat may be worth the extra care, while everyday basics may be easier to live with in washable materials.

## Which Garments Often Need Dry Cleaning?

Professional dry cleaning is often appropriate for:

- Tailored jackets, suits, and garments with structured shoulders or internal construction
- Delicate [silk](/en/glossar/seide/), velvet, lace, or embellished pieces
- Lined coats, dresses, and skirts
- Pleated garments when the pleats need to retain their shape
- Garments with leather, suede, or mixed-material details
- Formal pieces worn for special occasions, such as [evening wear](/en/glossar/abendgarderobe/)

Always check the individual label. Two garments made from the same fiber can have very different care requirements.

## Dry Clean Only vs. Hand Wash

“Dry clean only” and “hand wash” are not interchangeable instructions.

A [hand wash](/en/glossar/handwaesche/) label means the manufacturer permits gentle washing in water under specific conditions. A dry-clean-only label indicates that water washing may create avoidable risks, including shrinkage, color transfer, loss of shape, surface changes, or damage to trims and linings.

Trying to wash a dry-clean-only garment at home can be tempting, but it is a personal risk. The result depends on the garment's full construction, not only on what its main fabric appears to be.

## How to Care for Dry-Clean-Only Clothes Between Cleanings

Professional cleaning is not needed after every wear. Thoughtful maintenance can keep garments fresh while reducing unnecessary cleaning.

- Air garments after wearing them before returning them to the wardrobe.
- Use a wide, supportive hanger for jackets, coats, and dresses.
- Address stains promptly. Do not rub aggressively, as this can spread the stain or damage the surface.
- Use a garment brush where appropriate, especially for wool outerwear.
- Consider gentle [steaming](/en/glossar/dampfen/) to release light creases, but keep heat and moisture away from sensitive finishes.
- Store clean garments in breathable conditions rather than sealed plastic for long periods.

For stains, delicate fabrics, or vintage pieces, tell the cleaner what caused the mark and point out any fragile details before treatment.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: Dry cleaning means no liquid is involved.**  
  **Better:** The process uses liquid solvent rather than water.

- **Misunderstanding: Every garment made from silk or wool must be dry cleaned.**  
  **Better:** Some silk and wool garments are washable. The care label and garment construction are what matter.

- **Misunderstanding: Dry cleaning is always better for a garment.**  
  **Better:** It is the right method only when the care label or a professional assessment calls for it. Unnecessary cleaning may add wear over time.

- **Misunderstanding: A garment is high quality because it says dry clean only.**  
  **Better:** Dry-clean-only care can reflect a sensitive material or complex construction, but it is not proof of [material quality](/en/glossar/materialqualitaet/) on its own.

## Practical Care Check Before Purchase

Before taking home a dry-clean-only item, ask yourself:

1. Can I realistically maintain it according to the care label?
2. Will I wear it often enough to justify its care requirements?
3. Does the garment have a lining, pleats, embellishments, or tailoring that need professional attention?
4. Can it be aired, steamed, and stored easily between wears?
5. Does the care requirement fit the role this piece will have in my wardrobe?

## ESKYNA Key Line

Dry cleaning is not a mark of luxury or difficulty. It is a care requirement that should fit the material, construction, and real life of the garment.
