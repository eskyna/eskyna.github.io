## Definition

**Fabric weight**, usually expressed in **GSM**, means the weight of one square metre of textile. GSM stands for grams per square metre.

This measurement helps describe whether a fabric is likely to feel light, airy, substantial, crisp, soft, or structured. It can also offer useful clues about opacity, warmth, drape, and how well a garment may hold its shape.

## What Does GSM Mean in Clothing?

GSM is a material specification, not a style label. A fabric with a low GSM generally uses less material per square metre than one with a high GSM. However, the number alone does not reveal everything about the fabric.

For example, a lightweight cotton shirt can feel crisp because of its tight weave, while a heavier knit can feel soft and flexible because of its looped construction. A fluid viscose dress and a structured wool trouser may have similar fabric weights but behave very differently on the body.

To understand a textile properly, consider GSM alongside its fibre content, construction, surface texture, and [fabric drape](/en/glossar/materialfall/).

## How Fabric Weight Affects an Outfit

Fabric weight influences both comfort and visual impact.

### Light fabric weight

Lower GSM fabrics are often used for warm weather, layering, shirts, blouses, linings, and flowing dresses. They may feel breathable and move easily, but can also be more transparent or prone to showing underwear, seams, and wrinkles.

Lightweight fabrics work especially well when you want softness, movement, and less visual volume. Check [opacity](/en/glossar/opazitaet/) carefully before buying pale, fitted, or unlined pieces.

### Medium fabric weight

Medium-weight fabrics are often versatile for everyday clothing. They can provide enough substance for trousers, denim, knitwear, skirts, shirts, and dresses without feeling overly heavy.

This range often offers a useful balance of comfort, coverage, shape retention, and layering potential. It can be a practical choice for wardrobe essentials that need to work across more than one season.

### Heavy fabric weight

Higher GSM fabrics usually feel more substantial. They can add warmth, structure, and a more defined silhouette to garments such as coats, hoodies, denim, tailored trousers, and dense knitwear.

A heavier fabric may hang cleanly and resist transparency, but it can also feel warmer, bulkier, or less suitable for humid conditions. The garment’s cut and [motion allowance](/en/glossar/bewegungszugabe/) still determine whether it feels comfortable in movement.

## GSM, Fabric Density, and Quality

Fabric weight is often confused with [fabric density](/en/glossar/dichte/). They are related but not identical.

- **GSM** measures the weight of fabric over a defined area.
- **Fabric density** describes how closely fibres or yarns are arranged.
- **Material quality** considers a broader picture, including fibre quality, construction, finish, comfort, and performance.

A high GSM fabric is not automatically dense, durable, or luxurious. A loosely constructed heavy knit may stretch or lose shape, while a lighter, tightly woven fabric can be strong and long-lasting. Assess the material with your hands when possible, and inspect how it recovers after gentle stretching or creasing.

## How to Use GSM When Shopping

GSM is most useful when you compare similar garments. Compare cotton T-shirts with other cotton T-shirts, or sweatshirt fabrics with other sweatshirt fabrics, rather than comparing unrelated materials.

Use it as one buying criterion among several:

1. **Match the weight to the season.** Lighter fabrics may suit heat and layering, while heavier fabrics can be more useful for cold weather.
2. **Match the weight to the garment's purpose.** A white T-shirt may need enough weight for coverage, while a summer blouse may benefit from a lighter, more breathable fabric.
3. **Check the fabric in person if possible.** Feel the surface, hold it to the light, and notice whether it springs back after being lightly squeezed.
4. **Read the fibre content.** Cotton, linen, wool, silk, polyester, and blends can perform very differently at the same GSM.
5. **Consider your desired silhouette.** More substantial materials often create clearer lines, while lighter fabrics usually create more movement and softness.

For a purchase that will earn its place in your wardrobe, connect fabric weight with [material quality](/en/glossar/materialqualitaet/), fit, care requirements, and realistic cost per wear.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: Higher GSM always means better fabric.**  
  **Better:** Higher GSM only means more weight per square metre. Quality depends on fibres, construction, finish, and whether the fabric suits its intended purpose.

- **Misunderstanding: Lower GSM means poor quality.**  
  **Better:** A lightweight fabric can be well made and exactly right for a breathable shirt, sheer layer, or fluid dress.

- **Misunderstanding: GSM tells you exactly how warm a garment will be.**  
  **Better:** Warmth also depends on fibre type, fabric construction, layering, wind exposure, and how closely the garment fits.

- **Misunderstanding: Heavy fabric always drapes stiffly.**  
  **Better:** Drape depends on construction and fibre as well as weight. Some heavier fabrics remain soft and fluid, while lighter materials can be crisp and structured.

## Practical Check

When assessing fabric weight in a shop or at home, ask:

- Does the fabric feel appropriate for the weather and the garment’s purpose?
- Is it opaque enough for the cut, colour, and intended underwear?
- Does it hang in a way that supports the silhouette you want?
- Does it feel comfortable when you sit, bend, and move?
- Does the fabric recover after a light squeeze or stretch?
- Can you combine the garment easily within your existing wardrobe?

## ESKYNA Key Line

Fabric weight in GSM is a useful guide to how a textile may feel and perform, but the best choice comes from considering fibre, construction, fit, and purpose together.
