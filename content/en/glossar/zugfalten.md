## Definition

**Pull folds**, also called **tension folds**, are visible lines or creases that appear when a garment is pulled against the body. They often run horizontally, vertically, or diagonally and lead toward the point where the fabric lacks space, length, or the right shaping.

They are useful clues when assessing [fit](/en/glossar/passform/). The direction of a pull fold can reveal whether the issue lies in the garment's width, length, shoulder position, bust shaping, hip room, or crotch construction.

## Pull Folds Explained Simply

A well-fitting garment does not need to lie completely flat at every moment. Clothing moves with the body, and soft materials naturally develop wrinkles. Pull folds are different because they look tense, return consistently, and make the garment appear as though it is being pulled in a specific direction.

For example, horizontal pull folds across the bust, upper arm, thigh, or hip often suggest that the garment needs more width in that area. Diagonal folds may indicate that the garment does not match the body's shape or proportions. A jacket may pull from the bust toward the armhole, while trousers may develop diagonal lines from the crotch toward the thigh.

The relevant question is not whether there is any wrinkling at all. It is whether the fabric can settle comfortably when you stand, sit, walk, and raise your arms.

## What the Direction of Pull Folds Can Reveal

The direction of tension folds offers a practical starting point for diagnosing fit.

- **Horizontal pull folds:** Often indicate insufficient width around the bust, upper back, arms, hips, thighs, or waist.
- **Vertical pull folds:** Can indicate excess length or width, although their cause depends on the garment and placement.
- **Diagonal pull folds:** Often point toward a shape or proportion mismatch, such as at the bust, shoulder, hip, crotch, or upper back.
- **Pull folds around seams or closures:** May occur when a button placket, zipper, or seam is under tension.
- **Folds at the crotch of trousers:** Can be connected to the rise, crotch shape, hip room, thigh room, or overall trouser cut.

For a more precise assessment, consider the whole [cut](/en/glossar/schnitt/), not only the labelled size. Ready-to-wear sizes are not standardized enough to guarantee the same fit across brands.

## Pull Folds vs Normal Wrinkles

Not all visible lines are a sign that a garment fits poorly.

| Type of line         | Typical cause                                                | What it means                        |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Pull folds           | Tension, lack of room, unsuitable shaping                    | A recurring fit issue may be present |
| Movement wrinkles    | Sitting, walking, bending, raising the arms                  | Normal and usually temporary         |
| Fabric texture       | Natural surface of linen, crêpe, knitwear, or washed fabrics | Part of the material's character     |
| Draping or gathering | Deliberately designed volume or folds                        | An intended design feature           |

Materials with a soft [fabric drape](/en/glossar/materialfall/) can form more visible lines than firm woven fabrics. That alone does not mean the fit is wrong. Look for persistent tension and restricted movement rather than expecting a perfectly smooth surface.

## Common Causes of Pull Folds

Pull folds can result from several fit factors:

- The garment is too narrow in a specific area.
- The garment is too short through the torso, back, sleeve, or rise.
- The shoulder seam does not sit in the right position.
- The bust, hip, or waist shaping does not correspond to the body.
- [Darts](/en/glossar/abnaeher/) or seams are placed incorrectly for the wearer.
- There is too little [motion allowance](/en/glossar/bewegungszugabe/) for sitting, walking, or raising the arms.
- The garment is forced into place by closing buttons, zippers, or belts.

A size up can sometimes solve the issue, but not always. If the larger size creates excess volume elsewhere, a different cut is often the better choice.

## How to Check Pull Folds When Trying On Clothes

Use this quick fitting check before deciding whether to keep a garment:

1. **Stand naturally.** Do not pull the fabric down, hold your shoulders back unnaturally, or pose in front of the mirror.
2. **Look for recurring lines.** Smooth the garment once, then see whether the folds return immediately.
3. **Move through daily actions.** Sit down, walk, reach forward, raise your arms, and bend slightly.
4. **Trace the fold.** Follow its direction to the point where the fabric is pulling most strongly.
5. **Check comfort as well as appearance.** Tightness, restricted movement, gaping closures, or a garment that shifts out of place are important signals.
6. **Compare cuts, not only sizes.** A different silhouette, rise, shoulder construction, or shaping can make a greater difference than changing the size.

## Common Misconceptions

- **“Pull folds simply mean the garment is too small.”**  
  Not necessarily. The overall size may be correct, while the cut is unsuitable for the body's proportions or shape.

- **“Every crease is a fit problem.”**  
  Clothing wrinkles during movement, especially in soft or natural fabrics. Persistent tension is the key distinction.

- **“I should always alter the tight area.”**  
  Alterations can help in some cases, but they cannot always correct an unsuitable cut. Check the garment's balance and construction first.

- **“A loose garment cannot have pull folds.”**  
  Even a loose garment can pull across the shoulders, bust, upper back, hips, or crotch if its proportions are wrong.

## Why Pull Folds Matter

Recognizing pull folds helps you make more informed purchasing decisions. Instead of assuming a garment looks wrong because of your body, you can identify whether it needs a different size, a different cut, or more room for movement.

This makes shopping more efficient, reduces avoidable [bad purchases](/en/glossar/fehlkauf/), and supports a wardrobe built around garments that feel as good as they look.
