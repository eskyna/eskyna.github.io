## Definition

**Interlock** is a type of double-knit fabric, usually made with two sets of needles that connect two knit layers into one stable textile. It commonly has a smooth, even appearance on both sides and feels more substantial than [jersey](/en/glossar/jersey/).

Interlock fabric is often used for T-shirts, polos, dresses, baby clothing, loungewear, and underwear. Depending on its fiber content, it can feel soft and flexible while retaining more shape than a lighter single knit.

## Interlock Fabric Explained Simply

Think of interlock as a more compact, stable relative of jersey. A standard jersey fabric usually has a clearly recognizable right and wrong side and can curl at raw edges. Interlock is constructed differently, creating a denser fabric with a more balanced surface.

That structure often gives garments a neater line. An interlock T-shirt may look less flimsy than a very light jersey T-shirt, while an interlock dress can feel more covered and controlled without becoming as rigid as a woven fabric.

The exact result still depends on the fibers used. Cotton interlock tends to feel matte and soft, while blends with [elastane](/en/glossar/elasthan/) can offer more recovery. Interlock made with synthetic fibers may behave differently again in terms of warmth, moisture management, and surface appearance.

## Key Properties of Interlock Knit

Interlock is valued for a combination of comfort and structure:

- **Smooth on both sides:** The fabric often has an even, clean-looking surface on the front and back.
- **More stable than single jersey:** It is generally thicker and less prone to twisting or rolling at the edges.
- **Soft stretch:** Interlock usually stretches, especially across the width, but is often less fluid than lightweight jersey.
- **Good coverage:** Its denser construction can make it more opaque than a comparable lightweight knit, although this depends on color, weight, and fit.
- **Comfortable against the skin:** The smooth surface makes it a common choice for close-to-body garments.
- **Shape and drape:** Interlock has more body than thin jersey. Its [fabric drape](/en/glossar/materialfall/) is usually soft but relatively controlled.

## Interlock vs. Jersey

Interlock and jersey are both knitted fabrics, but they do not behave in the same way.

| Feature        | Interlock                                | Jersey                                         |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Construction   | Double knit                              | Usually single knit                            |
| Surface        | Similar, smooth appearance on both sides | Front and back usually look different          |
| Weight         | Often medium weight and more substantial | Can range from very light to heavier qualities |
| Stability      | Generally more stable                    | Often softer and more fluid                    |
| Raw edges      | Less likely to curl                      | Often curls at cut edges                       |
| Typical effect | Clean, covered, structured softness      | Relaxed, flexible, often more casual           |

Neither is automatically better. A flowing jersey may be ideal for a relaxed top, while interlock can be the stronger choice when you want a smoother line, greater coverage, or a more polished everyday basic.

## Why Interlock Matters in Clothing

Fabric construction changes how a garment looks and feels on the body. Interlock can make simple pieces appear more intentional because the material holds a cleaner surface and often has less visual transparency than a very thin knit.

This matters especially for wardrobe basics. A well-chosen interlock T-shirt, long-sleeve top, or casual dress can be highly versatile because it combines comfort with a more composed finish. For a functional wardrobe, this can improve [versatility](/en/glossar/kombinierbarkeit/) and support a clear [wardrobe logic](/en/glossar/garderobenlogik/).

Interlock is not, however, a quality guarantee by itself. A fabric can be called interlock and still vary significantly in yarn quality, density, finishing, seams, and durability.

## How to Assess an Interlock Garment

When shopping for interlock clothing, look beyond the label.

- Check the **fiber content** and consider what suits your routine, climate, and care preferences.
- Feel whether the fabric is pleasantly soft or overly thin, stiff, or loosely made.
- Hold it up to the light to assess [opacity](/en/glossar/opazitaet/), particularly with pale colors and fitted garments.
- Gently stretch the fabric and see whether it returns to shape without looking strained.
- Examine shoulder seams, hems, and neckline recovery, as these areas affect long-term wear.
- Try the garment while sitting, reaching, and moving. Good [fit](/en/glossar/passform/) includes freedom of movement as well as a convincing silhouette.
- Consider fabric weight. A heavier interlock may create a more polished line, while a lighter one may suit layering and warm indoor settings.

## Styling Interlock for Everyday Wear

Interlock works particularly well when you want softness without a slouchy effect. Its balanced surface can make casual garments feel more refined.

A few practical combinations include:

- An interlock T-shirt with straight-leg jeans and loafers for a clean everyday look.
- A fitted interlock top under a blazer, where a smooth surface keeps the outfit visually calm.
- An interlock dress with boots or sneakers for comfortable, low-maintenance dressing.
- An interlock long-sleeve top as a base layer under knitwear, jackets, or an overshirt.

Because the fabric is often visually simple, color, neckline, and silhouette become more noticeable. Use a shade that supports your [color palette](/en/glossar/farbpalette/) and select a cut that suits the role the garment should play in your wardrobe.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **“Interlock is the same as jersey.”**  
  Both are knitted fabrics, but interlock is a double-knit construction and is typically more stable and substantial than single jersey.

- **“Interlock always means thick fabric.”**  
  Interlock is often denser than lightweight jersey, but actual thickness depends on yarn, fiber, and fabric weight.

- **“Interlock is always opaque.”**  
  A dense interlock can provide good coverage, but light colors, thin qualities, and close fits may still reveal what is underneath.

- **“Interlock automatically means high quality.”**  
  Construction matters, but so do fiber quality, finishing, seam workmanship, and how the garment performs after wear and washing.

## ESKYNA Takeaway

Interlock is a smooth, stable double-knit fabric that often gives everyday clothing more coverage, shape, and visual calm than single jersey. It is a useful choice when comfort should still look polished.

## FAQ

### Is interlock a knit or woven fabric?

Interlock is a knit fabric. More precisely, it is a double-knit construction that connects two knit layers into one textile.

### Is interlock warmer than jersey?

Interlock is often warmer than a lightweight single jersey because it is commonly denser and more substantial. Actual warmth depends on fabric weight and fiber content, especially whether it contains cotton, wool, or synthetic fibers.

### Does interlock stretch?

Yes, interlock generally has natural knit stretch, often most noticeably across the width. The amount of stretch and recovery varies with the construction and whether elastane is included.

### Is interlock good for T-shirts?

Yes. Interlock is often used for T-shirts because it feels soft, has a smooth surface, and can create a more substantial, less sheer result than very thin jersey.

### Does interlock shrink?

It can shrink, especially when made from natural fibers such as cotton. Follow the [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/) and avoid assuming that all interlock fabrics react the same way to washing and drying.
