## Definition

**Elastane** is a highly elastic synthetic fiber used in clothing to create stretch, flexibility, and improved shape retention. It is rarely used alone. Instead, elastane is blended with other fibers, such as [cotton](/en/glossar/baumwolle/), [viscose](/en/glossar/viskose/), [polyamide](/en/glossar/polyamid/), or [polyester](/en/glossar/polyester/).

You may also see elastane described as **spandex**. Lycra is a brand name for elastane fiber.

## Elastane explained simply

Elastane helps clothing move with the body. It can make slim jeans easier to sit in, help a fitted dress recover its shape after wear, or give a waistband more flexibility.

A garment with elastane does not automatically feel tight or sporty. Its effect depends on the amount used, the other fibers in the blend, the fabric construction, and the [fit](/en/glossar/passform/) of the garment. A crisp cotton shirt with a little elastane behaves very differently from a soft stretch jersey dress or performance leggings.

In practical terms, elastane often supports comfort where the body moves most: at the knees, seat, waist, elbows, or bust. This can make close fitting pieces more wearable in everyday life.

## What elastane does in fabric

Elastane has two important functions:

- **Stretch:** The fabric can extend when you sit, bend, reach, or walk.
- **Recovery:** The fabric can return closer to its original shape after stretching.

This combination is especially useful in body skimming cuts, denim, underwear, hosiery, swimwear, and activewear. It can also improve the comfort of tailored trousers, shirts, and fitted blazers when used carefully.

However, stretch is not the same as good fit. A poorly cut garment may still pull, twist, or create [pull folds](/en/glossar/zugfalten/) even when it contains elastane. The right size, construction, and movement allowance remain essential.

## Why elastane matters when buying clothes

Understanding elastane helps you assess a garment beyond its appearance on the hanger. When comparing similar pieces, check how the fabric feels, how it springs back after gentle stretching, and whether it supports the intended use.

For example:

- Jeans with elastane may feel more comfortable for long days, but can lose their shape if the fabric is very stretchy or the fit is too tight.
- A fitted work dress may benefit from a small amount of elastane for sitting and moving comfortably.
- Stretch knitwear can follow the body closely, so material weight and [fabric drape](/en/glossar/materialfall/) become especially important.
- In sportswear, elastane supports freedom of movement, but breathability and the overall fiber blend matter too.

The best choice is not always the garment with the highest elastane percentage. Consider the complete [material profile](/en/glossar/materialprofil/), the cut, your daily routine, and how often you realistically plan to wear it.

## Elastane and garment quality

Elastane can improve comfort and performance, but it is not a guarantee of quality. A high quality stretch garment should feel balanced rather than overly thin, stiff, or quickly misshapen.

When assessing elastane clothing, look for:

- fabric that stretches evenly and returns smoothly
- a comfortable fit without strain at seams or closures
- enough fabric weight for the garment’s purpose
- stable seams in areas that stretch often
- a care label that suits your laundry routine

The base fiber is equally important. Elastane blended with cotton, wool, or viscose will have a different handfeel, breathability, surface, and drape from elastane blended with synthetic fibers. Learn more about [material quality](/en/glossar/materialqualitaet/) and [fiber blends](/en/glossar/faser-mix/) before treating any fabric label as a shortcut to quality.

## How to care for elastane

Elastic fibers can be affected by excessive heat and harsh treatment. Always follow the [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/), especially for swimwear, lingerie, sportswear, and close fitting garments.

As a general rule, gentle washing and moderate temperatures help preserve stretch and recovery. Very hot washing, high dryer heat, and intense ironing can reduce elasticity over time. Care matters because a garment may still look intact while its stretch performance has already weakened.

## Common misconceptions

**Misconception: Elastane always makes clothing tight.**  
**Better:** Elastane adds stretch, but tightness comes from the garment’s cut, size, and intended fit. A relaxed trouser can contain elastane, just as a rigid fabric can be cut very close to the body.

**Misconception: More elastane always means better comfort.**  
**Better:** The ideal percentage depends on the garment. Too much stretch can change the feel, drape, and long term shape of a fabric.

**Misconception: Elastane is only relevant in sportswear.**  
**Better:** It is also common in denim, hosiery, underwear, trousers, fitted dresses, shirts, and many everyday wardrobe pieces.

**Misconception: Stretch replaces a good fit.**  
**Better:** Stretch can support movement, but a garment still needs suitable proportions, seam placement, and sufficient room in the right areas.

## Practical shopping check

Before buying an elastane garment, ask:

- Does the fabric stretch where I need freedom of movement?
- Does it recover smoothly after a gentle stretch test?
- Does the garment still sit well when I walk, sit, and raise my arms?
- Is the fabric weight appropriate for the cut and level of coverage?
- Can I care for it according to the label without difficulty?
- Does the piece work with the rest of my wardrobe, not only in the fitting room?

## ESKYNA note

Elastane is most valuable when it quietly supports comfort, movement, and shape. Choose it as part of a thoughtful fabric blend and a well fitting garment, not as a substitute for good construction.
