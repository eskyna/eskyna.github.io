## Definition

**Aubergine** is a deep purple color, often described as an eggplant shade. In fashion, it usually sits between purple, burgundy, and dark brown, although the exact effect depends on its red, blue, brown, or black undertones.

Because it is dark and complex, aubergine can function like a refined neutral while still bringing more color and depth to an outfit than black, navy, or grey.

## What does aubergine mean in fashion?

Aubergine is not one fixed color. One aubergine dress may look like a cool blue-purple, while another may appear like a rich, red-toned plum. This is why the color can feel elegant, sensual, creative, calm, or dramatic depending on the specific nuance and material.

Compared with brighter purple, aubergine generally feels more restrained and grown-up. It has visual depth without demanding attention in the same way as a vivid [fuchsia](/en/glossar/fuchsia/) or a clear violet. It is often a strong option for people who want a dark color but find black too severe.

## Aubergine undertone and color effect

The undertone determines whether an aubergine shade harmonizes with your coloring and existing wardrobe.

- **Cool aubergine** has a visible blue or violet base. It can look crisp and sophisticated with silver, charcoal, navy, and cool pinks.
- **Warm aubergine** contains more red, wine, or brown. It pairs naturally with camel, chocolate brown, gold, olive, and warm nude tones.
- **Muted aubergine** has a softened, greyed quality. It creates a calm, understated effect and works well within a palette of [muted colors](/en/glossar/gedaempfte-farben/).
- **Clear aubergine** appears more saturated and purple. It creates stronger contrast and can become the focal point of an outfit.

Aubergine also changes noticeably under different lighting. A shade that seems almost black indoors may reveal a pronounced purple cast in daylight. This is especially relevant when combining separate items, as [metamerism](/en/glossar/metamerie/) can make colors look harmonious in one light source and mismatched in another.

## How to style aubergine

Aubergine can be used as a complete base color, a tailored statement, or a smaller accent. It is particularly useful in pieces where depth adds character, such as coats, knitwear, trousers, bags, shoes, lipstick, or evening fabrics.

### Aubergine with neutrals

For an easy, polished outfit, pair aubergine with one calm neutral:

- **Aubergine and cream:** soft, elegant, and lighter than a black-and-white contrast
- **Aubergine and grey:** modern and composed, especially with cooler purple tones
- **Aubergine and camel:** warm, rich, and classic when the aubergine contains red or brown
- **Aubergine and navy:** deep and quiet, with less harshness than black
- **Aubergine and black:** dramatic and sleek, though the difference between the colors should remain visible in daylight

Use [cream](/en/glossar/creme/) or [taupe](/en/glossar/taupe/) when you want to lighten an aubergine outfit without making the contrast too sharp.

### Aubergine with other colors

Aubergine can also create sophisticated color combinations:

- Pair it with [burgundy](/en/glossar/bordeaux/) for a tonal, wine-colored look with depth.
- Add dusty rose, mauve, or soft pink for a gentle, romantic contrast.
- Combine it with forest green, petrol, or navy for a dark, layered palette.
- Use gold for warmth and a more festive finish, or silver for a cooler, cleaner effect.
- Choose a controlled accent color rather than adding many competing shades. Aubergine already carries visual weight.

For a tonal outfit, combine several values of purple, plum, and berry. This approach can feel harmonious when the colors share a similar temperature and [saturation](/en/glossar/saettigung/).

## Which materials suit aubergine?

Material strongly shapes how aubergine is perceived.

- **Matte wool, cotton, and knitwear** make aubergine appear quiet, soft, and wearable for everyday outfits.
- **Velvet** intensifies its depth and gives the color an eveningwear or festive quality.
- **Satin and silk** bring out red, blue, or purple reflections and can make aubergine look more luminous.
- **Leather or faux leather** gives it a sharper, more directional presence.
- **Linen** can make an aubergine shade feel more relaxed and less formal, especially in warm weather.

Before buying, assess both the color and the [sheen level](/en/glossar/glanzgrad/). A glossy aubergine blouse can look much more vivid than the same shade in a matte sweater.

## Does aubergine suit everyone?

Aubergine can work for many people, but not every version will be equally flattering. The key is whether the shade's temperature, depth, and clarity support your natural coloring.

A cool, blue-based aubergine may be easier for people who suit cool, high-contrast colors. A warmer, red-brown aubergine may feel more harmonious on people who wear warm, earthy, or softened tones well. If dark colors overwhelm your face, wear aubergine farther from the face, such as in trousers, a skirt, shoes, or a bag.

For the clearest result, compare several shades near your face in daylight. Notice whether your skin looks more even, your eyes clearer, and your features more defined. This is a practical application of [color temperature](/en/glossar/farbtemperatur/) and [color consultation](/en/glossar/farbberatung/).

## Common misunderstandings

- **Aubergine is simply dark purple.**  
  Aubergine belongs to the purple family, but it often includes noticeable red, brown, or black influence. Those nuances change how it combines with other colors.

- **Aubergine always looks formal.**  
  The effect comes from the garment, fabric, and styling. An aubergine velvet dress is more formal than an aubergine cotton T-shirt or relaxed cardigan.

- **Aubergine must be paired with black.**  
  Black can work, but cream, grey, camel, navy, berry tones, and metallic accents often reveal the color more clearly.

## Practical check before buying aubergine

- Look at the garment in daylight as well as indoor light.
- Hold it near your face and compare it with a cooler and a warmer purple.
- Check whether it works with at least three pieces you already own.
- Consider the material, because shine can make the shade look brighter or darker.
- Decide whether you want aubergine to act as a neutral base or a visible color accent.

## ESKYNA Key Line

Aubergine is a versatile deep purple that can replace black with more warmth, color, and character, provided its undertone works with your coloring.

## Advisory view

Aubergine is especially useful when you want an outfit to feel refined without looking predictable. Start with one versatile piece, such as a knit, blouse, tailored trouser, or accessory, then build combinations around the actual warmth and depth of that shade. The goal is not to wear aubergine because it is universally flattering, but to find the aubergine that makes your own coloring look clear and balanced.
