## Definition

A **tankini** is a two piece swimsuit made up of bikini bottoms and a longer swim top. The top typically reaches the waist, hips, or upper thigh, creating more torso coverage than a conventional bikini while retaining the practical flexibility of separate pieces.

Tankini tops come in many shapes, including fitted, loose, ruched, halterneck, camisole, and peplum styles. They can be paired with matching or contrasting bottoms, from brief cuts to a [high waist bikini](/en/glossar/high-waist-bikini/).

## What Does a Tankini Look Like?

A tankini combines the appearance of a swimsuit with the convenience of a two piece. The longer top creates a continuous line over the midsection, but unlike a one piece swimsuit, the top and bottoms can be put on, removed, and sized separately.

The overall effect depends on the cut:

- A fitted tankini creates a smooth, streamlined silhouette.
- A ruched or draped style adds softness and can make the torso feel less exposed.
- A flared or loose top creates more movement and a relaxed beach look.
- A halterneck tankini emphasizes the shoulders and upper back.
- A structured top with cups or an underband can offer more defined bust support.

## Why Choose a Tankini?

A tankini is useful for people who want coverage without committing to a one piece swimsuit. It offers an adaptable middle ground between a bikini and a more covered swim style.

It can be a practical choice for swimming, sun protection, changing, and bathroom breaks because the pieces are separate. It also makes it easier to combine different top and bottom sizes, which can improve comfort and fit.

For styling, a tankini can feel sporty, classic, feminine, or retro depending on its color, print, neckline, and details. A simple solid color looks clean and understated, while prints, contrast piping, or textured fabrics create more visual emphasis.

## Fit and Support

The best tankini fit should feel secure without pressing, rolling, or riding up. Check the bust area first, especially if the top includes molded cups, an underwire, or an internal support layer.

Look for:

- A supportive underband that sits firmly but comfortably around the body
- Cups that contain the bust without gaping, flattening, or cutting in
- Straps that stay in place without digging into the shoulders
- A hem that remains comfortable when sitting, walking, and swimming
- Bottoms that match your preferred level of coverage and stay secure in water

If you prefer an open shoulder line, a [bandeau bikini](/en/glossar/bandeau-bikini/) may be another option. For adjustable coverage and a lighter look, consider a [triangle bikini](/en/glossar/triangel-bikini/).

## How to Style a Tankini

A tankini works naturally for beach days, poolside time, holidays, and active water activities. Add a lightweight shirt, kaftan, sarong, or shorts when moving between the beach and other plans.

For a coordinated look, repeat one color from the tankini in your sandals, sunglasses, beach bag, or cover up. If the tankini has a bold print, keep the remaining accessories simple so the swimwear remains the focal point.

A higher cut bottom can create a longer visual leg line, while shorts style bottoms can create a more sporty effect. As with all swimwear, comfort and secure movement matter more than following a fixed silhouette rule.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **A tankini is just a long bikini top.**  
  A tankini is a complete two piece swimsuit category. Its defining feature is the combination of separate bottoms with a longer top.

- **Tankinis always offer full coverage.**  
  Coverage varies widely. Some styles end at the waist, while others cover more of the hips. Necklines, arm openings, back designs, and bottom cuts also affect how covered the swimsuit feels.

- **A tankini cannot look polished or fashion focused.**  
  The effect depends on cut, fabric, color, and fit. A clean neckline, supportive construction, and well chosen accessories can make a tankini look refined and intentional.

- **More fabric automatically means more support.**  
  Coverage and support are different. A longer top does not necessarily provide bust support unless it has suitable internal construction, cups, straps, or an underband.

## Practical Check Before Buying

- Move your arms, bend, sit, and raise your shoulders to see whether the top stays in place.
- Check whether the bust area feels supported without pressure or empty space in the cups.
- Make sure the hem does not constantly roll upward or float excessively in water.
- Consider whether you prefer adjustable straps, removable cups, or a built in bra structure.
- Choose bottoms that feel secure and match the coverage you want.
- Check the fabric’s opacity when wet, especially in light colors.

## ESKYNA Key Line

A tankini offers the coverage of a longer swim top with the flexibility and easy fit options of a two piece swimsuit.

## Advisory View

Choose a tankini based on how you want to move, swim, and feel at the beach or pool. The most useful style is one that stays secure, supports the bust as needed, and lets you enjoy the day without adjusting it constantly.
