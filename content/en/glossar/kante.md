## Definition

In fashion, an **edge** is the boundary of a fabric piece or garment detail. Common examples include the edge of a [hem](/en/glossar/saum/), neckline, sleeve opening, pocket, lapel, or button placket.

Edges can be left visible, folded under, bound, stitched, piped, or otherwise finished. How an edge is handled affects both the practical performance of the garment and its visual impression.

## Edge in clothing, explained simply

An edge is where a garment ends or where one part of its construction becomes visible. On a T-shirt, this may be the narrow finish around the neckline. On tailored trousers, it is the finished bottom edge at the hem. On a jacket, it includes the edges of the lapels, pockets, and front opening.

A carefully finished edge can make clothing look clean and intentional. A rough, curling, twisted, or fraying edge may affect comfort and make a garment appear less stable, unless that effect is deliberately part of the design.

The edge does not need to be decorative to matter. Even a subtle finish influences how the material sits, moves, and holds its shape. This is why edges are useful details to inspect when assessing [craftsmanship](/en/glossar/verarbeitung/) and [material quality](/en/glossar/materialqualitaet/).

## Common types of garment edges

### Turned and stitched edges

The fabric is folded to the inside and secured with stitching. This is a common finish for hems, sleeve openings, and lighter woven fabrics. It creates a tidy, restrained result.

### Bound edges

A separate strip of fabric or binding encloses the cut edge. Bound edges can add stability, cover raw fabric edges, or create a visible contrast in color or texture.

### Piped edges

[Piping](/en/glossar/paspel/) is a narrow raised trim inserted along an edge or seam. It outlines a shape and can give garments, bags, or upholstery-inspired pieces a more defined, decorative finish.

### Raw edges

A raw edge is not turned under or fully enclosed. Depending on the fabric, it may fray, curl, or remain relatively stable. Raw edges are sometimes used intentionally to create a relaxed, distressed, or deconstructed look.

## Why edge finish matters

Edge finishing is a small construction detail with a noticeable effect. It can influence:

- **Durability:** Finished edges help protect fabrics that fray easily.
- **Comfort:** Smooth neckline, armhole, and waistband edges are less likely to rub or feel bulky.
- **Shape:** A stable edge can help a neckline, pocket, or hem keep its intended line.
- **Visual quality:** Clean edges contribute to a polished appearance, especially in tailoring and occasionwear.
- **Style message:** A sharp piped edge feels different from a soft rolled edge or a deliberately raw finish.

When shopping, inspect the garment at high-use areas such as hems, sleeve ends, necklines, pockets, and closures. These details often reveal more about everyday wear than the overall design alone.

## Edge, hem, and seam: what is the difference?

These terms are connected but not interchangeable:

- An **edge** is the boundary of a fabric piece or garment section.
- A **[hem](/en/glossar/saum/)** is a finished edge, usually at the bottom of a garment or sleeve.
- A **[seam](/en/glossar/naht/)** joins two or more fabric pieces together.
- A **[hem finish](/en/glossar/saumverarbeitung/)** describes the specific technique used to secure and complete a hem.

For example, the bottom edge of a skirt is first an edge. Once it is folded and stitched, it becomes a hem.

## Practical quality check

When examining garment edges, look for the following:

- The edge lies flat without twisting, rippling, or pulling.
- Stitching is even and suited to the fabric weight.
- The finish feels comfortable against the skin where relevant.
- Corners, pocket edges, and openings are reinforced where they receive stress.
- Decorative trims, bindings, or piping sit smoothly and do not distort the garment.

A simple check is to hold the garment up at eye level and look along the hem or opening. Unevenness becomes easier to see from this angle.

## Common misconceptions

**Misconception: Every edge should be invisible.**  
**Better:** Some edges are designed to disappear into the garment, while others are meant to frame a shape or become a visible accent.

**Misconception: A raw edge always means poor quality.**  
**Better:** Raw edges can be purposeful. What matters is whether the finish suits the fabric, garment function, and intended look.

**Misconception: Edges are only technical details.**  
**Better:** They are technical and visual. The way an edge is finished shapes neatness, texture, structure, and perceived quality.

## ESKYNA key line

Edges are small construction details, but they strongly influence how refined, comfortable, and durable a garment feels.
