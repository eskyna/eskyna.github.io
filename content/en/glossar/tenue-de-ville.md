## Definition

**Tenue de Ville**, sometimes written as **Tenu De Ville**, is a French dress code for polished daytime attire. It describes an elegant city look that feels appropriate for professional, social, and formal daytime settings without becoming eveningwear.

In practical terms, Tenue de Ville sits above casual dressing. The goal is to look considered, neat, and refined through good fit, quality materials, restrained styling, and attention to the occasion.

## What does Tenue de Ville mean?

The French phrase _tenue de ville_ translates roughly as “town attire” or “city dress.” It refers to clothing suitable for being out in an urban setting, such as a daytime reception, luncheon, ceremony, business-related event, or formal social invitation.

Unlike a highly specific [Dress Code](/en/glossar/dresscode/), Tenue de Ville leaves some room for interpretation. A suitable outfit should communicate respect for the setting while still fitting your personal style, the weather, and the local context.

A refined dress and blazer, a trouser suit, a skirt with a blouse, or tailored trousers with a smart jacket can all fit the dress code. For menswear, a suit or sports jacket with tailored trousers, a shirt, and polished shoes is often appropriate. The exact level of formality depends on the invitation.

## Tenue de Ville outfit ideas

A Tenue de Ville outfit should look intentional from head to toe. It does not need to be severe or overly formal, but it should be cleaner and more elevated than an everyday casual look.

### For a polished feminine look

- A midi dress with a tailored [Blazer](/en/glossar/blazer/) and closed shoes or refined sandals
- Wide-leg tailored trousers with a blouse, fine knitwear, and a structured bag
- A skirt suit or coordinated jacket-and-skirt ensemble for a formal daytime event
- A [Pants suit](/en/glossar/hosenanzug/) in navy, gray, beige, or another controlled color, styled with understated jewelry

### For a polished masculine look

- A well-fitting suit in navy, gray, charcoal, or a subtle check
- A sports jacket with tailored trousers, shirt, and leather shoes
- A fine-gauge knit with tailored trousers and a jacket for a slightly less formal invitation
- A tie when the invitation, venue, or professional context calls for greater formality

Avoid clothing that reads as overtly sporty, beachwear, distressed, or nightclub-focused. Hoodies, gym shoes, visibly worn denim, and highly casual graphic tops rarely match Tenue de Ville.

## Tenue de Ville compared with other dress codes

Tenue de Ville is often confused with businesswear and semi-formal attire. The categories overlap, but their purpose is different.

| Dress code                                      | Main focus                                                         | Typical level of formality         |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| Tenue de Ville                                  | Elegant daytime city dressing for social or professional occasions | Polished and adaptable             |
| [Business Formal](/en/glossar/business-formal/) | Professional authority and conservative workplace standards        | Structured and formal              |
| [Semi Formal](/en/glossar/semi-formal/)         | A dressed-up look for events, often later in the day               | Elegant, sometimes more festive    |
| [Cocktail Attire](/en/glossar/cocktail-attire/) | Social event dressing with a celebratory character                 | More evening-oriented and dressy   |
| [Smart Elegant](/en/glossar/smart-elegant/)     | A modern, refined balance of smart and relaxed pieces              | Flexible, depending on the setting |

For a daytime wedding, gallery opening, business lunch, or city reception, Tenue de Ville often means elegant restraint. For an evening celebration, [Cocktail Attire](/en/glossar/cocktail-attire/) or another stated dress code may be more relevant.

## How to get Tenue de Ville right

### Prioritize fit and condition

A beautifully fitting, well-maintained outfit will usually look more appropriate than a more expensive but poorly fitting one. Check sleeve length, trouser hem, shoulder fit, and whether the garment allows you to sit and move comfortably. Good [Fit](/en/glossar/passform/) makes an outfit feel calm and credible.

### Choose refined fabrics and finishes

Wool, silk, cotton poplin, fine knitwear, linen blends, and smooth crepe can create a polished daytime effect. Clothing does not need to be plain, but loud logos, heavily distressed finishes, and overly casual jersey can weaken the impression.

### Keep accessories deliberate

A structured bag, simple jewelry, a watch, a leather belt, or polished shoes can finish the look. Accessories should support the outfit rather than compete with it. Consider the overall [Balance](/en/glossar/balance/) between color, texture, and detail.

### Read the invitation closely

The setting matters. A Tenue de Ville request for a business lunch may call for a more tailored, restrained outfit than the same request for a garden event or daytime celebration. When in doubt, choose the more polished option and adjust the formality through accessories.

## Common misconceptions

**Misconception: Tenue de Ville means any outfit worn in the city.**  
**Better:** The term refers to polished city attire, not simply casual clothes for running errands. The look should suit a socially or professionally significant daytime occasion.

**Misconception: Tenue de Ville always requires a dark suit.**  
**Better:** A suit can be appropriate, but the dress code is more flexible than strict business formalwear. Dresses, tailored separates, fine knitwear, and refined colors can also work well.

**Misconception: It is the same as eveningwear.**  
**Better:** Tenue de Ville is primarily a daytime code. It should be elegant, but it usually needs less glamour, shine, and dramatic formality than [Evening Wear](/en/glossar/abendgarderobe/).

**Misconception: Personal style has no place in Tenue de Ville.**  
**Better:** Personal style can be visible through color, cut, jewelry, or a distinctive accessory. Keep the overall message appropriate, polished, and easy to read.

## Quick Tenue de Ville check

Before leaving, ask yourself:

- Does this look polished enough for a daytime city event?
- Are the fit, fabric, and shoes in good condition?
- Does the outfit match the invitation, venue, and time of day?
- Is there a clear balance between professional polish and personal expression?
- Would I still feel appropriately dressed if the event were slightly more formal than expected?

Tenue de Ville is not about looking rigid. It is about showing consideration through an outfit that feels refined, appropriate, and true to your personal style.
