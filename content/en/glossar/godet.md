## Definition

A **godet** is a wedge-shaped piece of fabric inserted into a seam to create extra width, flare, and movement at a specific point in a garment. Godets are most common in skirts and dresses, where they allow the upper silhouette to remain relatively close to the body while the hem opens as the wearer walks.

A godet may be triangular, curved, or cut in a wider flared shape. It can match the garment fabric for a subtle result or use contrast in color, texture, or transparency to become a visible design feature.

## How a Godet Works

A godet is sewn into an opened section of a seam. Above its starting point, the garment stays narrow. Below it, the inserted panel creates additional circumference and movement.

This makes godets useful when a garment needs more room at the hem but should not become bulky around the waist or hips. Several evenly placed godets can create a soft, flowing shape that moves beautifully without relying on a fully gathered or circular skirt.

The effect depends on:

- **Placement:** A lower insertion creates a restrained flare, while a higher insertion adds more visible volume.
- **Shape:** A narrow triangle gives a modest opening. A curved or wider godet creates greater swing.
- **Fabric:** Lightweight fabrics produce fluid motion, while firmer fabrics create a more sculptural line.
- **Number of inserts:** One godet can create a focal point. Multiple godets distribute width around the garment.

## Godet Skirts and Dresses

A godet skirt or godet dress is often chosen for its balance of structure and movement. The garment can follow the body through the upper section, then open toward the hem in a controlled way.

Compared with an [A-Line](/en/glossar/a-linie/) silhouette, which gradually widens from the upper body or waist, a godet silhouette gains width only at selected seam points. This can make the flare feel more directional and animated.

Godets are also different from [pleats](/en/glossar/falten/). Pleats use folded fabric to create fullness, while a godet adds a separate panel. A [flounce](/en/glossar/volant/), by contrast, is an attached strip or circular section that creates a rippling edge or decorative fullness.

## Why Godets Matter in Fit and Proportion

Godets influence both movement and visual proportion. Because they add width lower on the garment, they can keep the upper body line clear while giving the hem more presence.

They are particularly helpful when you want:

- A fitted or slim upper silhouette with freedom of movement below
- A skirt that opens when walking but looks controlled while standing
- More hem width without gathering fabric at the waist
- A garment with a subtle or dramatic sense of motion
- A design detail that draws the eye toward the lower part of an outfit

The placement of a godet also affects [eye direction](/en/glossar/blickfuehrung/). Contrasting inserts or visible seams naturally attract attention, while matching inserts create movement without becoming the main focus.

## What to Look for When Buying or Trying On a Godet Garment

A well made godet should support movement without pulling or distorting the surrounding seams.

- Check that repeated godets begin at the same height and are placed symmetrically.
- Look closely at the point where each insert begins. It should lie smooth, without puckering or tension.
- Walk, sit, and turn in the garment. A godet should open naturally rather than pull apart awkwardly.
- Consider the [fabric drape](/en/glossar/materialfall/). Soft fabrics fall fluidly, while crisp fabrics hold a more pronounced shape.
- Check the hemline. It should hang evenly unless an intentionally asymmetrical design is part of the garment.

## Common Misconceptions

- **A godet is a pleat.**  
  A godet is a separate insert sewn into a seam. A pleat is formed by folding fabric.

- **Godets always make a garment look wide.**  
  Godets add width only below their insertion point. The waist, hip, or thigh can remain slim depending on the cut.

- **Godets are only decorative.**  
  They can be decorative, but they also improve ease of movement and create practical hem width.

- **Every flared skirt has godets.**  
  A skirt may flare through its overall cut, [gathering](/en/glossar/raffung/), pleats, or panels. Godets are one specific construction method.

## ESKYNA Note

> Godets preserve a clear line where a garment should sit close, then add movement exactly where the body and the silhouette need more room.

## Advisory View

When choosing a godet skirt or dress, focus on the relationship between fit, insertion height, and fabric. A lower godet is often understated and elegant, while higher or contrasting inserts create a more expressive, dynamic result. The best choice is one that supports your movement and fits the intended visual impact of the outfit.
