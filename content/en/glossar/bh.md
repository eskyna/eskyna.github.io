## Definition

A **bra**, short for brassiere, is an undergarment worn on the chest to support, cover, shape, lift, or center the bust. Most bras are built from an underbust band, two cups, adjustable straps, and a back fastening. Some styles also use underwires, padding, side panels, or shaping seams.

A bra is not only about appearance. A well-chosen style can make movement more comfortable and help clothing sit more smoothly. Its ideal fit and level of support are personal, and can change with the outfit, activity, and individual comfort needs.

## How a Bra Works

The underbust band is the foundation of a bra. It wraps around the ribcage and should provide most of the garment's support. The cups hold the breast tissue, while straps help keep the cups in their intended position and can fine-tune the fit.

Different bra constructions create different effects:

- A smooth [T-Shirt Bra](/en/glossar/t-shirt-bh/) is designed to disappear under fitted knits and lightweight tops.
- A [Balconette Bra](/en/glossar/balconette-bh/) creates a more open upper cup line and can suit square or wider necklines.
- A [Plunge Bra](/en/glossar/plunge-bh/) has a lower center section for deep V necklines.
- A [Bralette](/en/glossar/bralette/) is usually softer and less structured, with a natural, relaxed feel.
- A [Push-up Bra](/en/glossar/push-up-bh/) uses padding or cup construction to lift and visually center the bust.

The right bra does not need to create a particular shape. It should support the shape, coverage, and feeling you want for the day.

## Why Bra Fit Matters

A bra that fits well can improve comfort throughout the day and make clothing look more intentional. When the band is too loose, it may ride up at the back and shift support to the straps. When cups are too small, breast tissue can spill over the top or sides. Cups that are too large may wrinkle or gap.

Good [Bra Fit](/en/glossar/bh-passform/) can also affect the line of an outfit. A smooth bra works well under close-fitting jersey, while a seamed or lace style may be better suited to thicker fabrics, layered looks, or clothing where texture is welcome.

For formalwear, open backs, strapless designs, or very low necklines, the undergarment should be chosen alongside the outfit. Options such as a [Strapless Bra and Bandeau Bra](/en/glossar/traegerloser-bh/), [Adhesive Bra and Backless Bra](/en/glossar/klebe-bh/), [Boob Tape](/en/glossar/boob-tape/), or [Fashion Tape](/en/glossar/fashion-tape/) can help create the needed coverage and security.

## How to Choose a Bra

Start with the purpose rather than the label or visual style.

### For everyday wear

Choose a bra with a comfortable, stable band and cup shape that works under the clothes you wear most often. Smooth cups and moderate coverage are practical for many wardrobes, especially under T-shirts, blouses, and fine knitwear.

### For support and movement

For active days, prioritize a secure band, stable straps, and a construction that limits unwanted movement. The best choice depends on the activity and how much support feels comfortable.

### For neckline and outfit styling

Match the bra to the garment's neckline, fabric, and level of coverage. A deep V top may call for a plunge style, while a strapless dress needs support from a strapless construction rather than tightened removable straps.

### For a softer feel

Wireless bras, soft cups, and bralettes can be a comfortable choice when you prefer less structure. Support may still come from a wide band, firmer fabric, or thoughtful seaming.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: The straps should carry most of the support.**  
  **Better:** The band should do most of the supporting work. Straps should sit comfortably without digging into the shoulders.

- **Misunderstanding: A tighter band is always a better fit.**  
  **Better:** A band should feel secure but should not restrict breathing, cause pain, or leave persistent deep marks.

- **Misunderstanding: One bra size fits every brand and style.**  
  **Better:** Sizing can vary by brand, cup construction, fabric stretch, and style. Try on different sizes and assess the fit instead of relying only on the label.

- **Misunderstanding: Underwire bras are automatically uncomfortable.**  
  **Better:** Discomfort often comes from a poor fit or a wire that sits on breast tissue. A correctly placed underwire should surround the breast rather than press into it.

- **Misunderstanding: A bra must visibly lift the bust to be supportive.**  
  **Better:** Support can look natural. The right result depends on personal preference, breast shape, and the clothing worn over it.

## Practical Bra Fit Check

Use these points when trying on a bra:

- The underbust band sits level around the body and does not ride up at the back.
- The cups hold the breast tissue without spilling, pinching, wrinkling, or gaping.
- Underwires, if present, sit around the breast tissue and lie flat against the ribcage.
- The center section between the cups rests comfortably against the body when the style is designed to do so.
- Straps stay in place without slipping, digging in, or carrying all the support.
- You can raise your arms, sit, bend, and breathe comfortably without the bra shifting.
- The bra remains comfortable under the clothing you plan to wear with it.

For a more detailed sizing process, see [How to Measure Bra Size](/en/glossar/bh-groesse-messen/).

## Care and Wear

Rotate bras rather than wearing the same one every day. This gives elastic materials time to recover between wears. Follow the garment's [Care Label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/), fasten hooks before washing, and avoid excessive heat when possible, as heat can weaken elastic fibers.

A bra that has stretched out, lost its shape, developed damaged wires, or no longer feels supportive may no longer serve its purpose. Regular fit checks are useful because body measurements, comfort preferences, and wardrobe needs can change.

## ESKYNA Key Line

A bra should support your body and your outfit without becoming a distraction. The best bra is the one that feels secure, comfortable, and right for the clothes and life you actually wear.
