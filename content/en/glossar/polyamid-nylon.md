## Definition

**Polyamide**, often called **nylon**, is a synthetic fiber used in clothing for its strength, smooth surface, low weight, and relatively quick drying performance. It is common in hosiery, lingerie, sportswear, swimwear, outerwear linings, and close-fitting garments.

In fashion labels, polyamide may appear as “nylon” or “PA.” It is frequently blended with [elastane](/en/glossar/elasthan/) to create flexible fabrics that follow the body while returning to shape after movement.

## What Does Polyamide Fabric Feel Like?

Polyamide fabric can feel smooth, sleek, and light against the skin. Fine polyamide yarns are often used for sheer tights, lightweight lingerie, and technical activewear because they can create a refined surface without adding much bulk.

Its final feel varies widely. A dense matte polyamide jersey can feel supportive and sporty, while a fine polyamide mesh may be airy and semi-sheer. Polyamide can also be made into [microfiber](/en/glossar/mikrofaser/), creating an especially soft, fine, and smooth fabric surface.

## Why Polyamide Is Used in Clothing

Polyamide is a practical choice when a garment needs to be durable, lightweight, and comfortable in motion.

### Strength and abrasion resistance

The fiber has good resistance to wear caused by rubbing. This matters in garments that experience frequent friction, such as tights, leggings, underwear, sports bras, swimwear, and linings. For long-term performance, however, the fabric weight, yarn quality, and garment construction remain just as important as the fiber itself.

### Quick drying performance

Polyamide absorbs relatively little moisture compared with many natural fibers. This helps fabric dry faster after washing, sweating, or swimming. It is therefore common in performance clothing and beachwear, although quick drying does not necessarily mean the garment will feel cool in every climate.

### Smooth fit and body-conscious silhouettes

Fine polyamide fabrics can sit closely against the body with little bulk. Combined with elastane, they are often used for shapewear, seamless underwear, leggings, and swimwear because the fabric can support a clean, smooth line under clothing.

### Lightweight comfort

Polyamide can provide strength without the weight of many heavier fabrics. That makes it useful for lightweight jackets, wind-resistant layers, packable clothing, and delicate-looking hosiery.

## Polyamide in Swimwear, Lingerie, and Activewear

Polyamide is particularly useful for garments that need to move with the body.

- **Swimwear:** A polyamide and elastane blend can create a smooth, supportive fit with a soft handfeel.
- **Lingerie and hosiery:** Fine filaments allow for lightweight, sheer, or delicate-looking fabrics with good durability.
- **Activewear:** Polyamide is often used for leggings, sports bras, and fitted tops where mobility and abrasion resistance matter.
- **Shapewear:** Dense blends can provide controlled support while remaining relatively thin under dresses or trousers.

For close-fitting pieces, also consider [stretch](/en/glossar/stretch/), opacity, seam placement, and whether the garment stays comfortable when sitting, walking, and moving.

## Polyamide vs. Polyester

Both polyamide and [polyester](/en/glossar/polyester/) are widely used synthetic fibers, but they can create different results in clothing.

| Feature     | Polyamide / Nylon                         | Polyester                                               |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Feel        | Often smooth, soft, and sleek             | Can range from crisp to soft, depending on construction |
| Strength    | Strong and abrasion resistant             | Strong and durable                                      |
| Common uses | Hosiery, lingerie, swimwear, activewear   | Sportswear, fleece, outerwear, everyday clothing        |
| Drying      | Relatively quick drying                   | Relatively quick drying                                 |
| Stretch     | Often blended with elastane for close fit | Often blended with elastane when stretch is needed      |

The label alone cannot determine quality. A well-made polyester garment may perform better than a poorly made polyamide one, and the reverse is equally true. Check the full fiber content, fabric density, seams, fit, and care instructions before buying.

## How to Wear and Use Polyamide Garments

Polyamide works best when its performance supports the purpose of the garment.

- Choose polyamide and elastane blends for swimwear, fitted activewear, or smooth base layers.
- Look for enough opacity in leggings and swimwear, especially when the fabric stretches over the body.
- Use a breathable outer layer when wearing dense synthetic fabrics in warm weather.
- Check whether a close-fitting garment has comfortable [motion allowance](/en/glossar/bewegungszugabe/) rather than relying only on stretch.
- For long-term wardrobe use, choose pieces with a fit and function you will genuinely wear often.

## Care for Polyamide and Nylon

Always follow the garment’s [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/), as blends and finishes can change the recommended method.

In general, polyamide benefits from gentle washing, moderate temperatures, and avoiding unnecessary heat. Turn delicate items inside out, use a laundry bag for lingerie or hosiery, and avoid rough surfaces that may snag fine fabrics. Swimwear should be rinsed after use to remove salt, chlorine, sunscreen, and body care products.

High heat can damage synthetic fibers or reduce the recovery of elastane blends. When in doubt, air dry rather than using a hot dryer.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: Polyamide is always stretchy.**  
  **Better:** Polyamide has flexibility, but significant stretch usually comes from elastane or a stretchy knit construction.

- **Misunderstanding: All nylon fabrics feel the same.**  
  **Better:** Polyamide can be sheer, firm, brushed, mesh-like, smooth, matte, or glossy depending on the yarn, fabric construction, and finish.

- **Misunderstanding: Synthetic automatically means poor quality.**  
  **Better:** Fiber type is only one part of quality. Construction, weight, finish, fit, and care strongly affect how useful a garment will be.

- **Misunderstanding: Quick drying means no care is needed.**  
  **Better:** Polyamide dries relatively quickly, but chlorine, heat, friction, and harsh washing can still shorten a garment’s useful life.

## Practical Check Before Buying Polyamide Clothing

- Read the full fiber composition, especially the percentage of elastane in fitted garments.
- Stretch the fabric gently and check whether it returns smoothly to shape.
- Hold lighter fabrics against the light to assess [opacity](/en/glossar/opazitaet/).
- Check high-friction areas such as inner thighs, underarms, seams, and straps.
- Try the garment sitting and moving, not only standing still.
- Review the care instructions before buying, particularly for swimwear, shapewear, and delicate lingerie.

Polyamide is a strong, smooth, quick-drying synthetic fiber that is especially useful when clothing needs durability, a close fit, and freedom of movement.
