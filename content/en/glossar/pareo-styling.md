## Definition

**Pareo styling** is the practice of wrapping, folding, tying, or draping a lightweight rectangular cloth as a beach cover up. A pareo can be worn around the waist as a skirt, across the bust as a simple dress, over the shoulders, or tied around the head.

The technique turns one flexible piece of fabric into several beachwear options. It is useful when you want more coverage over swimwear without adding a structured garment to your beach bag.

## What Does Pareo Styling Look Like?

A pareo is usually made from a light fabric that moves easily and dries relatively quickly. Depending on its size and material, it can sit close to the body or fall in soft folds.

The simplest version is a wrap skirt tied at one hip. This creates an asymmetrical line and leaves the legs visible. A longer or wider cloth can also be wrapped higher on the body to make a strapless or halter style cover up.

Pareo styling is closely related to wearing a [sarong](/en/glossar/sarong/), although the words are often used interchangeably in everyday fashion. The key idea is not one fixed garment shape, but the ability to create different silhouettes through wrapping.

## Effect on a Beach Outfit

A pareo adds movement, color, and a more finished appearance to swimwear. It can soften the directness of a bikini, add coverage between the beach and a café, or bring a print into an otherwise simple look.

The placement of the wrap changes the visual effect:

- **Tied at the hip:** Creates an easy, asymmetrical skirt shape and draws the eye toward the knot.
- **Tied at the natural waist:** Defines the waist and gives the look more structure.
- **Wrapped higher on the torso:** Creates a dress-like effect with more coverage.
- **Draped over the shoulders:** Adds a relaxed layer and offers light protection from sun or breeze.
- **Used as a headscarf:** Brings color near the face and can protect hair from wind.

A pareo can also support a clear color story. Match one shade from your swimwear print, sandals, or bag, or use the fabric as an intentional [accent color](/en/glossar/akzentfarbe/).

## How to Wear a Pareo

### As a Side-Tied Skirt

1. Hold the pareo behind your hips with the long edge horizontal.
2. Bring both ends forward and overlap them.
3. Tie the ends at one hip in a secure double knot.
4. Adjust the fabric so the overlap lies flat and the hem falls evenly.

This is an easy choice for the pool, beach, or a casual holiday lunch. A lower tie creates a relaxed look, while a higher waist placement feels more polished.

### As a Front-Tied Skirt

Wrap the cloth around the waist and bring both ends to the front. Tie them in a knot or soft bow. This method creates a central focal point and works well when the pareo has a decorative border or placed print.

### As a Strapless Cover Up

Hold the pareo behind your back, bring the upper corners across the bust, and knot them at the front or side. This works best with a larger, opaque fabric and swimwear that feels secure underneath.

Check the fit while walking, sitting, and lifting your arms. A beach wrap should feel easy to wear, not require constant adjustment.

### As a Halter Style Dress

Hold the pareo behind your body, bring the two upper corners to the front, cross them over the bust, and tie them behind the neck. The lower section should overlap comfortably around the hips and legs.

This style creates a longer vertical line and suits a relaxed holiday look. It can be paired with flat [sandals](/en/glossar/sandale/), a straw bag, and simple jewelry.

### As a Shoulder Wrap

Fold the pareo into a narrower rectangle and place it over the shoulders. Let it hang open or secure the ends loosely at the front. This option is useful when moving from bright sun into air conditioning or when you want a little extra coverage over a swimsuit or summer dress.

## Choosing the Right Pareo

Look at the fabric, dimensions, and print before deciding how you will wear it.

- **Size:** A larger pareo gives more options for dress and shoulder-wrap styles. A smaller one is often easiest as a skirt, scarf, or bag detail.
- **Opacity:** Sheer fabrics feel light and airy but may offer less coverage away from the beach. Check the fabric in daylight.
- **Drape:** Soft, fluid fabrics create elegant folds. Crisp fabrics hold shape but can form bulkier knots.
- **Print placement:** Borders can look especially effective along the hem or at the edge of a wrap skirt.
- **Color:** Choose a shade that works with your swimwear, or use contrast for a more expressive beach look.

For a polished holiday wardrobe, a pareo can work alongside the relaxed pieces associated with [resort casual](/en/glossar/resort-casual/). For a more elevated event by the water, consider the expectations of [beach formal](/en/glossar/beach-formal/).

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: A pareo is only for wearing over a bikini.**  
  **Better:** A pareo is most often used over swimwear, but it can also function as a shoulder wrap, headscarf, temporary skirt, or bag accessory.

- **Misunderstanding: Every pareo can be tied into every style.**  
  **Better:** The available styling options depend on the fabric size, stretch, weight, and opacity. A short or narrow cloth may not provide enough coverage for a dress wrap.

- **Misunderstanding: A loose knot is always more elegant.**  
  **Better:** A soft-looking knot still needs to be secure. Test it in motion so the wrap stays in place without repeated adjustments.

- **Misunderstanding: A pareo replaces occasionwear.**  
  **Better:** A pareo is ideal for beach and pool settings, but may not be suitable for restaurants, hotels, or events with a more formal dress code.

## Practical Check Before You Leave

- Can you walk, sit, and bend comfortably without the wrap opening too far?
- Does the knot feel secure without pulling on the fabric?
- Is the pareo opaque enough for where you plan to wear it?
- Does its color or print connect with your swimwear, shoes, or bag?
- Is the hem positioned where you want it on the leg?
- Have you packed a small pin or hair tie if you need extra security?

## ESKYNA Key Line

Pareo styling turns a simple beach cloth into a flexible cover up that adds coverage, color, and intention to swimwear.

## Advisory View

A pareo is most useful when it suits your actual holiday routine. Choose one that feels good against the skin, works with at least two swimwear options, and can be tied confidently in a few reliable ways. Like [scarf styling](/en/glossar/scarf-styling-tuch-styling/), the best results come from keeping the technique simple and making the fabric feel like a natural part of the outfit.
