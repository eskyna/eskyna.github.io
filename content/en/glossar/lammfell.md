## Definition

**Shearling** is a natural material made from sheepskin with the wool left attached to the hide. The fleece appears on one side, while the reverse is finished as leather or suede. It is used for coats, jackets, vests, boots, gloves, and decorative trims.

In everyday fashion language, shearling may also be called lambskin with wool. What matters visually is the contrast between a soft, dense wool surface and a more structured leather side.

## What does shearling look and feel like?

Shearling has a tactile, substantial character. The wool side can look fluffy, curly, smooth, or closely shorn, depending on its processing. The leather side is often suede-like, though it may also have a smoother finish.

This combination gives shearling a distinctive balance of softness and structure. A shearling-lined jacket can feel protective and rugged, while a refined shearling coat in a clean cut can look quiet, luxurious, and timeless. Its visible surface strongly influences an outfit's [texture](/en/glossar/struktur/) and overall level of visual weight.

## Shearling versus wool, leather, and faux shearling

Shearling is not simply wool and it is not simply leather.

- **[Wool](/en/glossar/wolle/)** is a fiber that can be spun or felted into textile fabrics. It is not attached to a hide.
- **[Leather](/en/glossar/leder/)** is animal hide processed for durability and flexibility, usually without the wool fleece visible.
- **Shearling** keeps both elements together: wool on one side, leather or suede on the other.
- **Faux shearling** is an imitation, often designed to resemble wool fleece on a textile backing. It may be a practical option for a specific budget, use case, or preference, but it does not have the same material structure as real shearling.

When shopping, read the fiber and material label rather than relying on appearance alone. Terms such as “sherpa,” “teddy,” or “faux fur” may describe a similar look without indicating genuine shearling.

## Why shearling matters in an outfit

Shearling is visually present. Its dense surface adds volume, warmth, and texture, especially around collars, cuffs, lapels, and footwear. A large shearling collar brings attention toward the face, while a full shearling coat becomes the dominant element of the look.

Because the material already has depth and character, it often works best with simpler companions: straight-leg jeans, tailored trousers, fine knitwear, or a clear knit dress. A calm color palette can let the surface stand out without making the outfit feel overloaded.

Consider the relationship between the garment's volume and your overall [proportion](/en/glossar/proportion/). A cropped shearling jacket can create a strong waist focus, while a longer coat creates a more enclosing, substantial silhouette.

## How to assess shearling quality

Good [material quality](/en/glossar/materialqualitaet/) is not defined by softness alone. Look at the entire piece.

- Check whether the wool surface looks even and securely attached.
- Feel whether the leather side is supple rather than unusually stiff or brittle.
- Examine seams, edges, closures, and lining for careful [craftsmanship](/en/glossar/verarbeitung/).
- Notice the weight. A heavy piece can feel luxurious, but it should still be practical for how you intend to wear it.
- Consider the cut and the ease of movement, especially at the shoulders, sleeves, and neckline.
- Confirm whether the color, finish, and texture suit the rest of your wardrobe.

A shearling piece can be an [investment piece](/en/glossar/investitionsteil/) when it fits your climate, lifestyle, and personal style. If it is too heavy, too delicate for your routine, or difficult to combine, its value in your wardrobe may remain limited.

## Care for shearling

Shearling requires different care from a wool coat or a standard leather jacket. Check the garment's care label before using any product or cleaning method.

Avoid prolonged exposure to rain and direct heat. If the piece becomes damp, let it dry naturally away from radiators, heaters, and strong sunlight. Do not machine wash it unless the manufacturer explicitly permits this. For stains, significant soiling, or long-term storage concerns, seek a specialist cleaner experienced with shearling, leather, or suede.

Thoughtful [care](/en/glossar/pflege/) protects the material's softness, shape, and finish, and supports its long-term wearability.

## Common misconceptions

**Misconception: Shearling is just a fluffy lining.**  
**Better:** Genuine shearling is a sheepskin material in which wool remains attached to the leather hide. A fluffy lining can also be synthetic or made from a separate textile.

**Misconception: All shearling looks bulky.**  
**Better:** The visual effect depends on wool length, thickness, color, garment cut, and placement. A narrow trim reads very differently from a full shearling coat.

**Misconception: Shearling and faux shearling are interchangeable.**  
**Better:** They can create a similar aesthetic, but their construction, touch, care needs, durability, and material composition are different.

## Practical styling check

Before buying or wearing shearling, ask:

- Is the wool surface the focal point, or is it only a small accent?
- Does the piece add more volume around the shoulders, torso, or feet than you want?
- Can you combine its color and texture with at least three existing outfits?
- Is its weight and care requirement realistic for your daily life?
- Does it support the desired balance between ruggedness, softness, elegance, or warmth?

## ESKYNA key line

Shearling combines the softness of wool with the structure of leather, making it a material with strong warmth, texture, and visual presence.
