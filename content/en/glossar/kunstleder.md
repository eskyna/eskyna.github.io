## Definition

**Faux leather** is a leather-look material made without animal hide. It is commonly constructed from a textile backing and a synthetic surface coating or film, often designed to imitate the grain, sheen, texture, or weight of leather.

You may also see it described as synthetic leather, imitation leather, artificial leather, or leather-look material. These labels are not always technically identical, but they usually refer to materials intended to create a leather-like visual effect.

## Faux leather explained simply

Faux leather can be smooth and polished, soft and matte, glossy, embossed, quilted, or deliberately textured. Its effect depends less on the name alone and more on the specific material construction and finish.

A supple faux leather shirt can read modern and refined, especially when paired with soft [knitwear](/en/glossar/strick/) or tailored trousers. A firmer faux leather biker jacket has more visual structure and often creates a stronger, more urban impression. Glossy finishes can look bold and expressive, while matte surfaces tend to feel calmer and easier to integrate into an everyday wardrobe.

Because faux leather does not behave exactly like [leather](/en/glossar/leder/), it is useful to assess it on its own terms. Some versions are flexible and comfortable; others feel firm, less breathable, or prone to creasing. The quality of the backing, surface, seams, and fit matters as much as the visual finish.

## What is faux leather made of?

Many faux leather materials combine a woven or knitted textile base with a polymer coating. Polyurethane and PVC are common materials, although composition differs by product.

This construction means that faux leather can vary substantially:

- **Surface:** Smooth, grained, patent-like, suede-look, embossed, or textured
- **Handle:** Soft and flexible, crisp and structured, or heavy and dense
- **Drape:** Fluid in lighter versions, sculptural in thicker versions
- **Comfort:** Dependent on thickness, backing, fit, lining, and temperature
- **Durability:** Dependent on material quality, use, storage, and care

The label provides the most reliable information about the exact composition and care requirements.

## Why faux leather matters in an outfit

Faux leather introduces visible [texture](/en/glossar/struktur/) and material contrast. Even in black, brown, or another neutral shade, it can make an outfit feel more defined because its surface catches light differently from cotton, denim, wool, or jersey.

It is particularly useful when you want to add structure without relying on a bright color or large print. A faux leather skirt can sharpen a simple sweater, while faux leather trousers can bring edge to a classic shirt. The key is balance: a structured material often works best with softer, matte, or more relaxed pieces elsewhere in the look.

Consider the garment's [cut](/en/glossar/schnitt/) as well. A loose faux leather trouser usually feels more contemporary and easier to wear than a very stiff, overly close-fitting style. A garment should allow comfortable movement rather than creating tension, noise, or visible pulling.

## How to recognize good faux leather

There is no single visual test for quality, but these details provide useful clues when shopping:

- **Examine the surface:** It should look even and appropriate to the garment. Extremely plastic-looking shine may not suit every style goal.
- **Feel the material:** Check whether it feels excessively stiff, thin, sticky, or fragile for its intended use.
- **Look at the backing:** If visible, it should appear secure and suitable for the weight of the outer surface.
- **Check seams and edges:** Clean stitching and carefully finished edges support a more polished result. See [craftsmanship](/en/glossar/verarbeitung/) for what to inspect.
- **Move in it:** Sit, bend your arms, and walk. Good [fit](/en/glossar/passform/) matters more than an impressive surface on the hanger.
- **Read the care label:** Care instructions reveal practical limits that should suit your lifestyle.

A faux leather piece does not need to imitate real leather perfectly to work well. It needs to look intentional, feel comfortable enough for its purpose, and fit your wardrobe realistically.

## Styling faux leather

### For an understated everyday look

Choose one faux leather item and combine it with quieter materials. For example, pair faux leather trousers with a fine knit, a plain T-shirt, and loafers. A matte finish and restrained accessories make the result feel considered rather than costume-like.

### For a polished outfit

A faux leather pencil skirt or straight-leg trouser can work with a crisp blouse, a blazer, and simple pumps. Keep the silhouette clear and avoid adding too many competing glossy elements. This approach uses [material quality](/en/glossar/materialqualitaet/) and texture as part of the outfit's overall message.

### For a stronger fashion statement

Use faux leather as a focal point through a trenchcoat, biker jacket, corset-inspired top, or bold boot. Let it remain the main textural feature, then ground the rest of the look with simpler colors and shapes. A deliberate [focal point](/en/glossar/fokuspunkt/) keeps the outfit visually clear.

## Faux leather care

Care depends on the specific material, so the garment label always comes first. In general, faux leather can be sensitive to high heat, harsh cleaning agents, friction, and long periods of pressure or folding.

For everyday care:

- Wipe light surface marks gently if the care label allows it.
- Avoid placing faux leather directly against strong heat sources.
- Hang structured garments where possible to reduce deep creases.
- Do not assume that care methods suitable for real leather are suitable for faux leather.
- Consider whether the required care fits your actual routine before buying.

For more guidance, see [Care](/en/glossar/pflege/) and [Care Label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/).

## Common misconceptions

**Misconception: Faux leather is always poor quality.**  
**Better:** Faux leather ranges widely in construction, finish, comfort, and durability. Evaluate the individual garment rather than judging the category alone.

**Misconception: Faux leather and real leather behave the same way.**  
**Better:** They can look similar, but their surface, breathability, aging, care needs, and response to heat or wear may differ.

**Misconception: Glossy faux leather always looks cheap.**  
**Better:** Gloss can be an intentional style choice. The effect depends on the finish, the garment's cut, styling, and the occasion.

**Misconception: Faux leather is automatically a sustainable choice.**  
**Better:** Whether a material is a responsible choice depends on its composition, production, longevity, care, and how long it is actually worn. Avoid making assumptions based on one label alone.

## Practical check before buying

- Does the surface and sheen suit your personal style and the occasions you dress for?
- Does the garment feel comfortable when you sit, walk, and move your arms?
- Are the seams, edges, lining, and closure neatly finished?
- Can you name at least three pieces already in your wardrobe that work with it?
- Are you willing and able to follow the care instructions?
- Does it offer enough [versatility](/en/glossar/kombinierbarkeit/) to justify its place in your wardrobe?

## ESKYNA key line

Faux leather works best when its texture, fit, and finish support the outfit's purpose. Choose the piece for how it wears in real life, not only for how it looks in the mirror.
