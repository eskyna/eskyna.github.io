## Definition

**Swimwear** is clothing designed for swimming, sunbathing, and spending time by the water. The term includes bikinis, one-piece swimsuits, tankinis, swim trunks, swim shorts, and more specialized styles with shaping or support features.

Unlike ordinary clothing, swimwear needs to work when wet. It should stay in place, allow comfortable movement, provide the coverage you want, and feel suitable for the setting, whether that is a public pool, holiday beach, spa, or water sport.

## Common Swimwear Styles

Swimwear comes in many cuts, each offering a different balance of coverage, support, adjustability, and visual effect.

- **Bikini:** A two-piece swimsuit with a separate top and bottom. Bikini tops range from simple tie styles to more structured options such as an [underwire bikini](/en/glossar/buegel-bikini/) or [balconette bikini](/en/glossar/balconette-bikini/).
- **One-piece swimsuit:** A single garment that covers the torso. It may be minimal, sporty, highly supportive, or detailed with cut-outs and open-back designs.
- **Tankini:** A two-piece style with a longer top that covers more of the torso while retaining the flexibility of separate bottoms. Read more about the [tankini](/en/glossar/tankini/).
- **Monokini:** A one-piece swimsuit with pronounced cut-outs, often at the waist or sides. A [monokini](/en/glossar/monokini/) creates a more graphic, body-aware look.
- **Triangle bikini:** A classic bikini style with triangular cups, often adjustable through ties or sliding cup placement. See the [triangle bikini](/en/glossar/triangel-bikini/) for details.
- **High-waist bikini:** A bikini with bottoms that sit at or above the natural waist, offering more coverage through the midsection. Learn more about the [high-waist bikini](/en/glossar/high-waist-bikini/).
- **Swim trunks and swim shorts:** Lower-body swimwear designed for a practical, relaxed fit, often chosen for swimming, beach activities, and poolside wear.

## How Swimwear Affects Fit and Confidence

The right swimwear can make a day at the water feel much easier because it supports movement and matches your personal comfort level. The most flattering option is not one fixed style. It is the piece that feels secure, fits your body well, and suits how you actually plan to wear it.

Details such as cup construction, strap placement, leg height, waistband position, and fabric density change both comfort and appearance. For example, a [high-leg bikini cut](/en/glossar/high-leg-beinausschnitt/) creates a more open hip line, while a high-waist bottom can offer a more covered, retro-inspired silhouette.

For bikini tops, support matters especially when swimming or being active. Wider straps, adjustable bands, underwires, and well-fitting cups can provide a more stable fit. If you need guidance, the principles of [bra fit](/en/glossar/bh-passform/) are also useful when assessing structured swim tops.

## How to Choose Swimwear

Start with the occasion and activity. A bikini for quiet sunbathing may have different requirements from swimwear for laps, water sports, a family pool day, or a beach holiday.

Consider these practical points:

- **Movement:** Make sure the piece stays comfortable when walking, bending, sitting, swimming, and raising your arms.
- **Support:** Choose the level of bust support that feels right for you. Structured cups, underwires, adjustable straps, and secure closures can make a noticeable difference.
- **Coverage:** Decide where you want more or less coverage, including the bust, stomach, hips, seat, and leg line.
- **Fabric opacity:** Check whether pale or lightweight fabric remains sufficiently opaque when wet.
- **Color:** Swimwear is worn close to the face and against bare skin, so color can strongly affect your overall freshness. [Bikini color typology](/en/glossar/bikini-farbtypologie/) can help when choosing shades that work with your undertone.
- **Practicality:** Consider drying time, lining, ease of care, and whether straps, ties, or decorative hardware remain comfortable throughout the day.

A [bikini proportion check](/en/glossar/bikini-proportionen-check/) can be useful when comparing different top shapes, bottom cuts, colors, and pattern scales.

## Swimwear and Beachwear

Swimwear is the foundation of a waterside outfit, while beachwear includes the layers and accessories worn around it. A linen shirt, sarong, kaftan, sandals, sun hat, or light dress can add coverage and make it easier to move from the beach to a café or hotel terrace.

Thoughtful [beachwear layering](/en/glossar/beachwear-layering/) helps create a practical transition without hiding the swimwear completely. A simple cover-up can also protect the skin from direct sun and make an outfit feel more appropriate away from the water.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: Swimwear should fit like ordinary underwear.**  
  **Better:** Swimwear needs to stay secure during movement and when wet. A fit that seems fine in a changing room may shift, loosen, or become uncomfortable in use.

- **Misunderstanding: More compression always means better support.**  
  **Better:** Excessive compression can restrict movement and create digging or discomfort. Good support comes from balanced construction, secure bands, suitable cup shape, and adjustable details.

- **Misunderstanding: A one-piece swimsuit is always more modest than a bikini.**  
  **Better:** Coverage depends on the individual design. A high-cut or cut-out one-piece can reveal more skin than a fuller-coverage bikini or tankini.

- **Misunderstanding: Swimwear only belongs at the beach or pool.**  
  **Better:** Swimwear can also be part of a considered holiday or resort outfit when styled with appropriate layers, such as a shirt, wrap, or [sarong](/en/glossar/sarong/).

## Practical Swimwear Check

Before removing the tags, test swimwear under realistic conditions:

- Lift your arms, bend forward, squat, sit down, and walk a few steps.
- Check that straps, bands, ties, and leg openings stay in place without pinching.
- Confirm that the bust feels supported and that cups do not gape, wrinkle, or cut in.
- Look at the garment in natural light, especially if it is pale, white, or lightly lined.
- Consider how it works with your planned cover-up, sandals, bag, and waterside activities.
- Choose a style that feels like you, rather than one chosen only for a trend or photo.

## Key Line

**Good swimwear combines secure fit, comfortable movement, suitable coverage, and a style that lets you enjoy time by the water with ease.**
