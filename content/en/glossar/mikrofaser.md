## Definition

**Microfiber** is a textile material made from exceptionally fine fibers. It is most often produced from synthetic fibers such as polyester and polyamide, although microfiber is not a single fixed material composition.

Because the fibers are so fine, microfiber fabrics can feel smooth, soft, light, dense, or suede-like. Their exact look and performance depend on the fiber blend, fabric construction, surface finish, and purpose of the garment.

## What does microfiber feel like?

In everyday clothing, microfiber often has a clean, smooth surface and a close, refined appearance. It can be used for activewear, lightweight outerwear, linings, hosiery, underwear, cleaning textiles, and accessories.

Microfiber may feel soft against the skin while still being durable. Some versions are thin and fluid, while others are compact, warm, or lightly brushed. This is why the word microfiber alone does not tell you whether a garment will be breathable, stretchy, warm, or high quality.

For a useful wardrobe decision, look beyond the label. Check the [fabric density](/en/glossar/dichte/), the [handfeel](/en/glossar/griff/), the weight, the inside finish, and how the garment moves when worn.

## Why microfiber matters in clothing

Microfiber can offer practical benefits for daily wear:

- **Low weight:** Fine fibers can create light fabrics that are easy to wear and pack.
- **Smooth appearance:** Many microfiber fabrics look neat and relatively refined, which can suit streamlined everyday outfits.
- **Durability:** Synthetic microfiber can be resistant to abrasion, depending on its construction.
- **Quick drying:** Many microfiber fabrics absorb little moisture and dry faster than many natural-fiber fabrics.
- **Functional use:** It is common in performance clothing and weather-oriented garments where ease of care and lightness matter.

At the same time, microfiber is not automatically the best choice for every wardrobe need. Some fabrics can retain odor, build static, feel less breathable in warm conditions, or show heat damage if cared for incorrectly.

## Microfiber, polyester, and polyamide

Microfiber is often confused with [polyester](/en/glossar/polyester/) or [polyamide](/en/glossar/polyamid/). The terms describe different things.

- **Microfiber** refers to exceptionally fine fibers.
- **Polyester** is a synthetic fiber type.
- **Polyamide** is another synthetic fiber type, often valued for strength and elasticity.

A microfiber garment may be made from polyester, polyamide, or a blend. The composition label tells you what the fibers are, while microfiber helps describe their fineness.

## How to assess microfiber quality

A microfiber item should be judged by its real performance, not by the material name alone. A good practical check includes:

- Feel the fabric for smoothness, density, and whether it seems overly thin or fragile.
- Hold it up to the light to assess [opacity](/en/glossar/opazitaet/), especially in trousers, skirts, and fitted tops.
- Check whether it attracts lint, develops static, or creases noticeably.
- Look at seams, lining, and finishing, since [craftsmanship](/en/glossar/verarbeitung/) strongly affects how a garment wears over time.
- Move, sit, and raise your arms to test comfort and [fit](/en/glossar/passform/).
- Consider whether the fabric suits your climate, activity level, and care routine.

A smooth, dense microfiber can look polished in a minimal outfit. A very thin or shiny version may look more technical or casual. The desired effect should guide the choice.

## Care for microfiber clothing

Always follow the garment's [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/). In general, microfiber benefits from gentle washing and moderate temperatures, as excessive heat can affect synthetic fibers.

Avoid judging care solely by fiber content. Coatings, membranes, elastic fibers, linings, and trims can change what a garment needs. If microfiber is used in functional clothing, fabric softener may interfere with properties such as moisture management or water repellency.

## Common misunderstandings

- **Microfiber is always polyester.**  
  Not necessarily. Polyester is common, but microfiber can also include polyamide or fiber blends.

- **Microfiber is always breathable.**  
  No. Breathability depends on fabric structure, thickness, finishes, and garment design. Learn more about [breathability](/en/glossar/atmungsaktivitaet/).

- **Microfiber always means poor quality.**  
  No. Material quality depends on construction, finishing, fit, durability, and how appropriate the fabric is for its purpose.

- **Microfiber is only for sportswear.**  
  No. It appears in activewear, outerwear, linings, hosiery, underwear, bags, and many everyday garments.

## ESKYNA Key Line

Microfiber is defined by exceptionally fine fibers, but its real value lies in how the finished fabric performs in your everyday wardrobe.

## Advisory view

Choose microfiber when its practical properties support the way you live, move, and care for clothing.
