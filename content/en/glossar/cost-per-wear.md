## Definition

**Cost per Wear**, often abbreviated as CPW, measures what an item effectively costs each time you wear it.

The basic formula is:

> **Purchase price ÷ number of wears = Cost per Wear**

For example, a 120 euro blazer worn 60 times has a Cost per Wear of 2 euros. The figure does not determine whether a garment is objectively good or bad. It gives you a practical way to judge whether an item earns a useful place in your wardrobe.

## Cost per Wear explained simply

Cost per Wear shifts attention away from the price tag alone. Instead of asking, “Is this cheap?” ask, “Will I actually wear this often enough to make it useful?”

A low priced top that only works with one pair of trousers, feels uncomfortable, or needs special care can remain unworn. Its Cost per Wear stays high. By contrast, a more expensive coat, bag, or pair of shoes may become economical over time if it suits your life, combines easily, and remains in good condition.

This makes Cost per Wear especially valuable when shopping for [basics](/en/glossar/basic/), [classic pieces](/en/glossar/klassiker/), outerwear, shoes, and other items you expect to reach for repeatedly.

## How to calculate Cost per Wear

Use the purchase price and your realistic wear count.

| Item          | Purchase price | Times worn | Cost per Wear |
| ------------- | -------------: | ---------: | ------------: |
| White shirt   |       80 euros |   40 times |       2 euros |
| Evening dress |      180 euros |    3 times |      60 euros |
| Wool coat     |      350 euros |  175 times |       2 euros |

The calculation is simple, but the estimate should be honest. Do not count imagined future outfits as wears. Instead, look at your actual routine, climate, work, social life, and existing wardrobe.

For a purchase you have not made yet, try a projected calculation: estimate how many times you are likely to wear the piece over a realistic period. Then compare that result with an alternative you already own or another item you are considering.

## What influences Cost per Wear?

Wear frequency is not only about taste. Several practical factors determine whether an item becomes a favorite or stays in the closet.

- **Fit and comfort:** A garment that restricts movement, pulls, slips, or needs constant adjustment is less likely to be worn. Good [fit](/en/glossar/passform/) supports repetition.
- **Versatility:** The more naturally an item works with your existing colors, shoes, layers, and occasions, the more wear opportunities it has. This is the core of [versatility](/en/glossar/kombinierbarkeit/).
- **Lifestyle:** A sequined skirt may be perfect for one person’s event calendar and impractical for another person’s daily life.
- **Care requirements:** Delicate pieces, dry clean only garments, and materials that crease easily may be worn less often if their care does not fit your routine. Consider [care](/en/glossar/pflege/) before buying.
- **Quality and longevity:** Construction, material, and repairability affect how long an item remains wearable. Price alone is not proof of [quality](/en/glossar/qualitaet/).
- **Season and climate:** A heavy coat may be worn constantly in a cold climate and rarely in a warm one.
- **Personal style:** An item should feel like you. If it does not support your [personal style](/en/glossar/personal-style/), it is unlikely to become part of your regular rotation.

## Why Cost per Wear matters when shopping

Cost per Wear helps you separate an attractive purchase from a useful one. It can reduce impulse buying because it asks how the item will function after the excitement of the fitting room has passed.

It is particularly helpful for building a [capsule wardrobe](/en/glossar/capsule-wardrobe/) or improving your [wardrobe logic](/en/glossar/garderobenlogik/). In both cases, the goal is not to own as little as possible. The goal is to own clothes that suit your needs and work together.

A strong Cost per Wear decision considers:

1. whether the item fills a real wardrobe need
2. whether it works with several pieces you already own
3. whether the cut, color, and material suit your everyday life
4. whether you can care for and wear it comfortably
5. whether you would choose it again after the first styling idea

## Cost per Wear is not only about buying less

Cost per Wear can encourage more thoughtful consumption, but it should not become a rigid rule that removes joy from clothing.

Some garments are valuable because they mark an important moment, express creativity, or meet a specific dress code. A wedding guest outfit, formal dress, or statement piece may not achieve a low figure, yet still be a considered and worthwhile choice.

The important question is not whether every item must be worn constantly. It is whether you understand its purpose before you buy it. For rarely worn pieces, [rental fashion](/en/glossar/mietmode-fashion-rental/), [secondhand](/en/glossar/secondhand/), borrowing, or choosing a more adaptable option can be useful alternatives.

## Common misconceptions

**Misconception: The cheapest item always has the best value.**  
**Better:** Value depends on use. A cheap piece worn once can have a higher Cost per Wear than a better made piece worn for years.

**Misconception: A high Cost per Wear means a purchase was a mistake.**  
**Better:** Not necessarily. Occasion, emotional value, and a specific need matter. The calculation is information, not a verdict.

**Misconception: You should only buy neutral basics.**  
**Better:** Colorful or distinctive pieces can have excellent Cost per Wear when they fit your style and combine well. A [signature color](/en/glossar/signature-farbe/) may be worn more often than an uninspiring neutral.

**Misconception: Cost per Wear can be calculated exactly before purchase.**  
**Better:** Before buying, it is an estimate. Real value becomes clearer through actual wear, care, and longevity.

## A practical Cost per Wear check before purchase

Before taking an item home, ask yourself:

- Can I name at least three outfits with pieces I already own?
- Would I wear it in my real weekly routine, not only in an idealized version of it?
- Does the item feel comfortable enough for several hours?
- Does it support my preferred colors, proportions, and style direction?
- Is the quality appropriate for the price and expected frequency of wear?
- Can I care for it realistically?
- Would a similar item already in my wardrobe do the same job?

If the answers are unclear, put the item on a [wish list](/en/glossar/wunschliste/) and revisit the decision later. Time often reveals whether a want is a genuine wardrobe need.

## Mini case: a lower price is not always lower cost

Imagine choosing between two black trousers. Pair A costs 45 euros but feels uncomfortable after sitting, attracts lint, and only works with one top. You wear it twice.

Pair B costs 140 euros, fits well, works for the office and weekends, and is worn once a week for two years. Pair B costs more at checkout, but its Cost per Wear can become much lower because it serves a clear role repeatedly.

The lesson is not that the higher priced option is always better. It is that the best choice is the one that fits your budget and has a realistic, useful place in your wardrobe.

## ESKYNA key line

**Cost per Wear is not about making every purchase cheap. It is about making each purchase more intentional, wearable, and relevant to your real life.**

## Advisory view

A useful wardrobe is built through better filters, not perfection. Cost per Wear works best alongside clear [buying criteria](/en/glossar/kaufkriterien/), an honest understanding of your daily life, and regular attention to what you already wear.

If you repeatedly buy beautiful pieces that remain unused, the issue may not be your taste. It may be a gap between the item, your lifestyle, and the rest of your wardrobe. A structured wardrobe review can make that gap visible and turn future shopping into clearer decisions.

## Real consulting situation

With **Cost per Wear**, the practical question is rarely only “Is this item worth its price?” More often, it is: “Will this item solve a real wardrobe need?”

In a consultation, this means looking beyond a single garment. We consider existing combinations, your roles and occasions, the desired [impact](/en/glossar/wirkung/), budget, care routine, and the reasons similar purchases may have remained unworn.

## Decision filter before your next purchase

- Does this piece fill a genuine gap rather than duplicate an existing item?
- Can I create at least three realistic outfits from my current wardrobe?
- Does it suit my daily life, fit requirements, and care routine?
- Is the quality appropriate for its expected use?
- Would I still choose it if it were not new or discounted?
- Does it support the style message I want to communicate?
