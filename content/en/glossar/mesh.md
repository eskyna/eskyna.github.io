## Definition

**Mesh** is a textile with an open, net-like structure. Its visible spaces between yarns create a lightweight surface that can be airy, transparent, textured, or supportive, depending on the construction and fiber content.

Mesh is used in fashion, activewear, shoes, bags, and accessories. It may be made from natural, synthetic, or blended fibers, although many technical mesh fabrics are made with [polyester](/en/glossar/polyester/) or [polyamide](/en/glossar/polyamid/).

## What Does Mesh Fabric Look and Feel Like?

Mesh is recognizable by its perforated or grid-like surface. Some versions are soft, fine, and almost sheer, while others are firm, structured, and more visibly sporty. The openings may be tiny and subtle or larger and clearly decorative.

In clothing, mesh can create [transparency](/en/glossar/transparenz/), reveal a layer underneath, or add contrast to an otherwise opaque garment. A fine mesh top can feel light and sensual, while a dense athletic mesh panel can support ventilation without exposing much skin.

The effect always depends on several factors:

- The size and regularity of the openings
- The fiber content and surface finish
- Whether the mesh is stretchy or stable
- The color of the fabric and what is worn underneath
- The garment's cut, placement, and intended use

## Mesh in Fashion and Everyday Clothing

Mesh is often used as an insert at the neckline, sleeves, back, side seams, or legs. It can make a garment appear lighter and more detailed without adding much volume. It is also common in [sheer dressing](/en/glossar/sheer-dressing/), where transparent materials are deliberately styled as part of the outfit.

A mesh garment can be worn in many ways:

- A fine mesh long-sleeve top layered under a blazer, dress, or knit
- A mesh bodysuit under high-waisted trousers or a skirt
- Mesh panels in activewear for airflow during movement
- Structured mesh in sneakers, bags, or hats
- Mesh overlays that add texture to dresses and occasionwear

For an understated look, choose mesh in a shade close to the base garment or wear it over a simple camisole. For more contrast, combine a visible mesh layer with opaque materials such as denim, leather, tailoring, or knitwear.

## Why Mesh Matters When Choosing Clothing

Mesh affects more than appearance. Its construction can change breathability, comfort, opacity, stretch, and durability. A mesh piece that looks appealing online may feel too delicate, too revealing, or less practical once it is worn for several hours.

Before buying, consider how the mesh is used. A decorative mesh sleeve has different requirements from breathable mesh in running shoes or a tight mesh top intended for layering. The right choice depends on your lifestyle, comfort preferences, and the role the item will play in your wardrobe.

Mesh can be especially useful for creating visual lightness in an outfit. Because the surface is open, it often adds detail without the visual weight of a dense woven fabric. This can support thoughtful [layering](/en/glossar/layering/) and make a look feel more dimensional.

## Mesh, Net, and Sheer Fabric: What Is the Difference?

Mesh, net fabric, and sheer fabric overlap, but they are not identical terms.

- **Mesh** describes an open construction with visible spaces between threads or yarns. It can be fine, sporty, stiff, soft, stretchy, or structured.
- **Net fabric** usually has more noticeable, wider openings and a stronger net-like appearance.
- **Sheer fabric** describes transparency rather than a specific construction. Chiffon, organza, tulle, and fine mesh can all be sheer.

In other words, mesh may be sheer, but not every sheer fabric is mesh. For a fuller understanding of see-through materials in outfits, see [Transparency](/en/glossar/transparenz/).

## How to Style Mesh Without Feeling Overexposed

The key to styling mesh is to decide how much skin, contrast, and texture you want to show. The underlayer matters as much as the mesh itself.

For a balanced everyday outfit, try a fine black mesh top over a simple black bra, bralette, or camisole, paired with straight-leg jeans and a jacket. Matching the underlayer to the mesh creates a calmer effect. A contrasting underlayer makes the transparent quality more visible and turns it into a focal point.

For office or smart casual settings, choose mesh only as a small detail, such as sleeves, a neckline panel, or a layered top under a [blazer](/en/glossar/blazer/). In eveningwear, mesh can work with satin, velvet, or tailored pieces when the overall level of coverage suits the occasion.

## Practical Quality Check for Mesh Clothing

When assessing a mesh garment, look beyond the visual effect.

- **Check the recovery:** Gently stretch the fabric and see whether it returns to shape. Poor recovery can lead to sagging or distortion.
- **Look at seams and edges:** Mesh needs clean finishing at seams, hems, and high-friction areas because open constructions can be more vulnerable to damage.
- **Assess transparency in daylight:** Try the garment near a window and in indoor light. What seems opaque in a fitting room may be more transparent outside.
- **Test movement:** Raise your arms, sit down, and bend naturally. Mesh should not pull uncomfortably or shift away from the intended coverage.
- **Consider snagging risk:** Fine mesh can catch on jewelry, rough bag hardware, or abrasive outerwear.
- **Plan the underlayer:** Make sure you already own suitable bras, camisoles, bodysuits, or slips for the piece.

## Care for Mesh Fabric

Care requirements vary with fiber content and construction. Fine mesh can be delicate, especially when it is lightweight, stretchy, or combined with lace, embroidery, or appliqué.

Check the [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/) before washing. If machine washing is permitted, placing delicate mesh pieces in a laundry bag can reduce contact with zippers, hooks, and rougher textiles. Avoid unnecessary friction, and keep mesh away from sharp accessories that may snag the surface.

## Common Misunderstandings About Mesh

- **Mesh is always sporty.**  
  Mesh is common in sportswear, but it can also look minimal, romantic, sensual, edgy, or formal depending on its scale, color, cut, and styling.

- **Mesh is always see-through.**  
  Dense mesh, layered mesh, and mesh backed with another fabric can provide considerable coverage.

- **Mesh is automatically breathable.**  
  An open structure can promote airflow, but breathability also depends on fiber type, fabric density, layers, and the garment's overall construction.

- **Mesh is low quality.**  
  Mesh is a construction, not a quality grade. Quality depends on the yarns, finishing, seam construction, durability, and suitability for its purpose.

## ESKYNA Takeaway

Mesh is an open textile structure that can add breathability, transparency, texture, or lightweight contrast to an outfit. Choose it according to its construction, coverage, comfort in movement, and how easily it works with your existing layers.

## FAQ

### Is mesh fabric stretchy?

Some mesh fabrics are stretchy, especially those containing elastane or knitted constructions. Others are stable and hold their shape more firmly. Always test the garment rather than assuming that all mesh will stretch.

### What do you wear under a mesh top?

That depends on the desired level of coverage. A camisole, bodysuit, bralette, bandeau, or fitted tank top can work well. Choose a matching underlayer for a subtle effect or a contrasting color for a more visible statement.

### Is mesh suitable for summer?

Mesh can be useful in warm weather because its open construction may allow air to circulate. However, comfort still depends on the fiber, garment fit, number of layers, and how directly the fabric sits against the skin.

### Can mesh look elegant?

Yes. Fine mesh in a controlled color palette can look elegant, especially when paired with tailoring, satin, velvet, or refined accessories. The overall styling and finish matter more than the material label alone.

### Is mesh durable?

Durability varies widely. Structured mesh used in footwear or bags can be robust, while very fine fashion mesh may be prone to snagging. Check the density, seam finishing, and intended use before buying.
