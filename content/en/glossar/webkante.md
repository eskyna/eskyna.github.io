## Definition

**Selvedge**, also spelled **selvage**, is the finished longitudinal edge of a woven fabric. It forms at both sides of the cloth where the weft threads turn back during weaving, helping prevent the fabric from unraveling as easily as a cut edge.

A selvedge runs parallel to the [grainline](/en/glossar/fadenlauf/). It may be narrow and plain, or it may include a colored stripe, printed information, or a denser weave than the main fabric.

## Selvedge explained simply

When fabric is woven, it is produced as a continuous width of cloth. The two long outer edges are the selvedges. Unlike a raw cut edge, they are already secured by the weaving process.

For sewing and garment construction, the selvedge is useful because it helps identify the fabric's straight grain. It is not automatically a ready-made seam allowance or hem. Since it can be tighter than the fabric beside it, sewing it into a seam without checking may cause puckering or distortion.

Selvedge becomes especially visible in [raw denim](/en/glossar/raw-denim/) and [selvedge denim](/en/glossar/selvedge-denim/). A turned-up jeans hem can reveal the fabric edge as a fine, clean line, often with a colored detail. In this context, the selvedge is part of the design rather than a hidden construction feature.

## Why the selvedge matters

Understanding selvedge helps with both sewing and buying fabric:

- It indicates the direction of the [woven fabric](/en/glossar/webstoff/).
- It supports accurate pattern placement along the grainline.
- It helps distinguish the original fabric edge from a cut edge.
- It explains why certain denim cuffs have a distinctive finished look.
- It prevents the mistake of treating every firm fabric edge as suitable for a seam or hem.

The selvedge itself is not a universal sign of fabric quality. Its appearance and usefulness depend on the weave, fiber, weight, and intended use of the material.

## Selvedge in garment design

Most selvedges are hidden inside seams or removed during cutting. They are used visibly when their structure or color adds a purposeful detail.

Visible selvedge works best when it is:

- evenly placed on both sides of a garment
- compatible with the garment's [cut](/en/glossar/schnitt/) and fabric weight
- free from unwanted printing or marks
- not so tight that it pulls the fabric out of shape

For denim trousers, a controlled cuff can show the selvedge while also changing the relationship between the trouser hem and shoe. This is a styling detail, not a requirement for wearing denim well.

## Selvedge, hem, and raw edge: the difference

| Term         | What it is                             | When it is created          |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **Selvedge** | The finished long edge of woven fabric | During weaving              |
| **Raw edge** | A fabric edge created by cutting       | During cutting              |
| **Hem**      | A folded and secured garment edge      | During garment construction |

A hem may be finished with stitching, while a selvedge is already woven into the cloth. Learn more about a [hem](/en/glossar/saum/) and [hem finish](/en/glossar/saumverarbeitung/).

## What to look for when using selvedge

- Align pattern pieces with the selvedge only when the pattern instructions call for a straight grain placement.
- Do not assume the selvedge has the same stretch, thickness, or drape as the main fabric.
- Test whether the edge causes pulling before incorporating it into a seam.
- Avoid using a marked or printed selvedge where it will be visible unless that effect is intentional.
- For visible denim cuffs, keep both cuffs equally wide and neat.

## Common misconceptions

- **Selvedge and hem are the same thing.**  
  They are not. Selvedge belongs to the original fabric, while a hem is made after a garment has been cut.

- **Selvedge can always replace seam finishing.**  
  Not necessarily. Its firmer weave may affect how a seam lies and moves.

- **Every fabric has a selvedge.**  
  Selvedge is primarily a feature of woven fabrics. Knit fabrics have different edge behavior and construction.

- **Selvedge denim is automatically better denim.**  
  Selvedge describes the woven edge and production method, not every aspect of the denim's quality, fit, weight, or finish.
