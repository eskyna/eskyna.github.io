## Definition

A **skort** is a single garment that combines the visual effect of a skirt with the practical coverage of shorts. The name blends the words “skirt” and “shorts.”

A skort can look like a skirt from the front while showing shorts at the back, or it can have a skirt-like outer layer over integrated shorts. It is a practical alternative to a [skirt](/en/glossar/rock/) when you want ease of movement without giving up a feminine, sporty, or polished silhouette.

## How a skort works

The defining feature of a skort is its built-in construction. Unlike wearing separate [biker shorts](/en/glossar/biker-shorts/) beneath a skirt, the shorts and skirt effect belong to the same piece.

Common designs include:

- **Wrap-front skorts**, with an overlapping panel that resembles a wrap skirt
- **Sport skorts**, often made for active movement with close-fitting inner shorts
- **Tailored skorts**, which use woven fabrics, clean pleats, or a structured front panel
- **Casual skorts**, often in denim, cotton, linen, or jersey for warm-weather dressing

Fabric, length, waistband height, and volume determine whether a skort reads as athletic, relaxed, preppy, or more refined.

## Why wear a skort?

A skort gives the leg-baring look of a short skirt while providing the practical security of shorts. It can feel especially useful for cycling, walking, travel, outdoor events, and days with wind or frequent movement.

Compared with [shorts](/en/glossar/shorts/), a skort often creates a softer or more dressed silhouette. Compared with a [mini skirt](/en/glossar/minirock/), it offers more coverage without requiring an additional layer underneath.

## How to choose the right skort

A good skort should be comfortable in motion and look intentional from every angle.

- **Check the inner shorts:** They should not pull, roll, or ride up when walking and sitting.
- **Test the front panel:** On wrap-style designs, make sure the overlap stays closed as you move.
- **Consider the waistband:** A smooth waistband usually creates a calmer line under fitted tops. Elastic or gathered waists can feel more casual and add volume.
- **Look at the length:** The outer layer and inner shorts should feel proportionate to each other and suitable for the setting.
- **Try it seated:** This reveals whether the construction stays comfortable and whether pockets or seams add unwanted bulk.

For a more balanced outfit, coordinate the skort’s length and volume with your top, shoes, and jacket. This is part of creating strong [proportion](/en/glossar/proportion/) in an everyday look.

## Styling a skort

A skort is versatile because it can move between casual, sporty, and smart outfits.

For a relaxed daytime look, combine a cotton or denim skort with a T-shirt, light shirt, and [sneakers](/en/glossar/sneaker/). For a polished version, choose a tailored skort with a blouse, fine knit, or [blazer](/en/glossar/blazer/). Sport skorts pair naturally with functional tops and trainers, while a structured skort can also work within a [smart casual](/en/glossar/smart-casual/) wardrobe.

Keep the overall outfit coherent. A very sporty skort paired with highly formal pieces can look disconnected unless that contrast is intentional.

## Common misconceptions

- **A skort is simply a skirt worn over separate shorts.**  
  Not quite. In a skort, the shorts are integrated into the garment’s construction.

- **Every skort looks sporty.**  
  No. Sports skorts are one category, but tailored, pleated, denim, and wrap-front versions can look casual, classic, or refined.

- **A skort has to look like a skirt from every angle.**  
  Not necessarily. Some designs reveal the shorts at the back, while others maintain a skirt-like effect all around.
