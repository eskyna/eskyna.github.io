## Definition

A **synthetic fiber** is a textile fiber made from chemically manufactured polymers. In clothing, the term commonly includes [polyester](/en/glossar/polyester/), [polyamide](/en/glossar/polyamid/), acrylic, and [elastane](/en/glossar/elasthan/).

Synthetic fibers are used across fashion because they can be developed for specific performance needs. Depending on the fiber and fabric construction, they may provide stretch, durability, low weight, quick drying, or improved shape retention.

## Synthetic Fibers Explained Simply

When a garment label lists polyester, polyamide, acrylic, or elastane, it contains one or more synthetic fibers. These fibers may make up the entire fabric or appear in a blend with materials such as [cotton](/en/glossar/baumwolle/), [wool](/en/glossar/wolle/), or [viscose](/en/glossar/viskose/).

A synthetic fiber is not automatically better or worse than a natural fiber. Its usefulness depends on what the garment needs to do. A fitted pair of jeans may benefit from a small amount of elastane for movement. A lightweight rain jacket may use polyamide or polyester for practical weather performance. A knitwear piece with acrylic may be easy to care for, though its feel and long-term surface appearance can differ from wool.

The finished fabric matters as much as the fiber name. A tightly woven polyester can feel crisp and smooth, while a polyester jersey can feel soft and flexible. Fabric weight, density, surface finish, and [fabric drape](/en/glossar/materialfall/) all influence how clothing looks and feels.

## Common Types of Synthetic Fiber

### Polyester

[Polyester](/en/glossar/polyester/) is widely used in clothing, lining, fleece, sportswear, outerwear, dresses, and blends. It is valued for durability, crease resistance, and easy care. Its comfort can vary considerably according to the fabric's weight, weave or knit, and finish.

### Polyamide

[Polyamide](/en/glossar/polyamid/), often called nylon in some contexts, is a strong and lightweight synthetic fiber. It is common in hosiery, underwear, swimwear, outerwear, bags, and technical clothing. Polyamide can create smooth, resilient fabrics and is often blended with elastane in close-fitting garments.

### Acrylic

Acrylic, also known as [polyacrylic](/en/glossar/polyacryl/), is often used in knitwear and fleece-like fabrics. It can provide warmth with relatively low weight and is frequently used as an alternative to wool in sweaters, hats, and scarves.

### Elastane

[Elastane](/en/glossar/elasthan/), also known as spandex or Lycra in some markets, is added in small amounts to give fabric stretch and recovery. It is common in leggings, jeans, underwear, swimwear, fitted tops, and [shapewear](/en/glossar/shapewear/). Elastane is rarely the main fiber in everyday clothing because it is usually blended with another material.

## How Synthetic Fibers Affect Clothing

Synthetic fibers can change a garment's practical performance and visual effect.

- **Stretch and movement:** Elastane helps close-fitting garments move with the body and return toward their original shape.
- **Durability:** Polyester and polyamide can add strength and help fabrics withstand regular wear.
- **Weight:** Many synthetic fabrics are light, which can be useful for activewear, travel clothing, and outer layers.
- **Creasing:** Polyester-rich fabrics often wrinkle less than many natural fibers.
- **Surface appearance:** Synthetic fibers can create matte, smooth, brushed, glossy, sheer, or technical-looking surfaces.
- **Drying time:** Some synthetic fabrics dry relatively quickly, making them practical for sport, travel, and wet-weather clothing.

For warm conditions, do not judge comfort by fiber content alone. Consider the garment's looseness, lining, fabric density, and [breathability](/en/glossar/atmungsaktivitaet/). A loose, lightweight woven fabric may feel very different from a dense, close-fitting synthetic knit.

## How to Choose Synthetic Fiber Clothing

Choose synthetic fibers according to the garment's purpose rather than treating them as a single category.

For activewear, look for comfortable stretch, a fabric weight suited to your activity, and seams that do not rub. For outerwear, consider whether you need water resistance, wind protection, or a light and packable layer. For everyday clothing, focus on handfeel, fit, opacity, and how easily the piece combines with the rest of your wardrobe.

A [fiber blend](/en/glossar/faser-mix/) can be especially useful when it combines complementary qualities. Cotton with elastane may offer a familiar feel with more flexibility. Wool blended with polyamide may add durability to knitwear. The right blend depends on how and where you plan to wear the garment.

Before buying, check the care label and assess whether the fabric suits your real routine. A beautiful fabric that feels too warm, pills quickly, or requires care you will not provide may become a [bad purchase](/en/glossar/fehlkauf/).

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: All synthetic fibers feel the same.**  
  **Better:** Polyester, polyamide, acrylic, and elastane have different properties. Their feel also changes with fabric construction, finish, and garment design.

- **Misunderstanding: Synthetic means low quality.**  
  **Better:** Synthetic fibers are used in clothing at many price points. Quality depends on the full garment, including material quality, construction, fit, finishing, and intended use.

- **Misunderstanding: Natural fibers are always more comfortable.**  
  **Better:** Comfort is personal and contextual. A fabric's weight, density, fit, breathability, and moisture management can matter as much as its fiber category.

- **Misunderstanding: A small elastane percentage makes every garment stretchy.**  
  **Better:** Stretch depends on both fiber content and fabric structure. A woven garment with elastane may offer only modest flexibility, while a knit can stretch much more.

## Practical Check Before You Buy

- Read the fiber composition on the label and identify whether the fabric is a single fiber or a blend.
- Touch the fabric and assess its [handfeel](/en/glossar/griff/), thickness, smoothness, and likely comfort against the skin.
- Move in the garment by sitting, reaching, and walking to test stretch and recovery.
- Hold lighter fabrics against the light to check [opacity](/en/glossar/opazitaet/), especially in trousers, skirts, and fitted tops.
- Check the care instructions and consider whether they suit your laundry routine.
- Think about the garment's role in your wardrobe, such as sport, travel, work, layering, or occasionwear.

## ESKYNA Key Line

Synthetic fiber is not a quality judgment. It is a material category whose value depends on performance, comfort, construction, and how well the garment serves your everyday life.

## Advisory View

Synthetic fibers are most useful when their properties match the purpose of a garment. Choose them deliberately for stretch, durability, easy care, or technical performance, then balance those strengths with fit, fabric feel, and the conditions in which you will wear the piece.
