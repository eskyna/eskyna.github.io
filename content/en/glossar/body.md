## Definition

**Body** is a fashion term for a close-fitting one-piece garment that covers the torso and usually fastens at the crotch. Often called a **bodysuit**, it combines the look of a tucked-in top with a secure fit that stays smooth under trousers, skirts, or layers.

Depending on context, “body” can also refer to a fabric's substance, firmness, or ability to hold its shape. In clothing and styling, however, it usually means the garment itself.

## What does a body look like?

A body can resemble a T-shirt, tank top, blouse, turtleneck, corset top, or lingerie piece. Most versions have a snap, hook, or similar closure at the crotch. They may be made from stretchy [jersey](/en/glossar/jersey/), rib knit, lace, mesh, or more structured woven materials.

The key feature is that the garment remains anchored under the waistband. This creates a clean line at the waist and avoids the bulk that can occur when a regular top is tucked into a skirt or pair of trousers.

## Why wear a body?

A body is useful when you want your outfit to look polished without repeatedly adjusting your top. It can support a sleek, minimal silhouette, but its effect depends on fabric, neckline, fit, and styling.

It is especially practical with high-waisted jeans, tailored trousers, pencil skirts, or under a [blazer](/en/glossar/blazer/). A simple opaque body can become an everyday basic, while lace, sheer fabrics, or strong cut-outs can move the piece closer to [lingerie dressing](/en/glossar/lingerie-dressing/) or evening styling.

## How to choose the right bodysuit

When shopping for a body, focus on fit before trend details.

- **Check torso length:** The body should not pull across the shoulders or crotch when you stand, sit, or raise your arms.
- **Look at the neckline:** A crew neck feels more covered, while a V-neck, square neck, or plunge neckline changes the emphasis around the face and upper body.
- **Consider opacity:** Light colors, thin jersey, lace, and mesh may require a suitable bra or an additional layer.
- **Assess the closure:** It should lie comfortably and be practical for your everyday routine.
- **Match the fabric to the occasion:** Soft stretch jersey is easy for daily wear, while satin, lace, or mesh creates a more deliberate statement.
- **Choose your usual size thoughtfully:** A body that is too small often creates visible pulling or tension lines. Fit matters more than the number on the label.

A good [fit](/en/glossar/passform/) is essential. The garment should feel secure, but never restrictive.

## Styling a body in everyday outfits

For a simple, balanced outfit, combine a neutral body with straight-leg jeans and a cardigan or blazer. The uninterrupted waistline can make even casual clothing look more intentional.

For office or smart casual settings, choose an opaque body with a clean neckline and wear it with tailored trousers, a midi skirt, or a suit. A structured outer layer keeps the look refined.

For an evening outfit, a body in lace, satin, mesh, or a sculptural cut can serve as the focal point. Balance it with calmer pieces such as wide-leg trousers, a long skirt, or a simple jacket. If the body is visibly lingerie-inspired, [underwear as outerwear](/en/glossar/underwear-as-outerwear/) is the more precise styling context.

## Body, lingerie, and shapewear are not the same

A body may be worn as lingerie, but not every body is lingerie. Many are designed as everyday tops in opaque, durable fabrics.

A body is also not automatically shapewear. Some styles offer smoothing or firm support, but [shapewear](/en/glossar/shapewear/) is specifically designed to shape or compress parts of the body. Check the product description and fabric composition rather than assuming that a close fit provides support.

## Common misconceptions

**Misconception:** A body is only suitable for very slim figures.  
**Better:** Bodies are available in many cuts, materials, and support levels. The most important factor is a comfortable fit through the torso and at the closure.

**Misconception:** A body always looks sensual or lingerie-like.  
**Better:** The styling depends on fabric and design. An opaque cotton or jersey body can look as understated as a fitted T-shirt.

**Misconception:** A body and bodycon mean the same thing.  
**Better:** A body is a one-piece garment construction. [Bodycon](/en/glossar/bodycon/) describes a close-fitting silhouette and can apply to different garment types.

## Quick fit check

Before keeping a body, test it in movement:

- Raise both arms and check whether it pulls uncomfortably.
- Sit down to make sure the closure does not press or pinch.
- Look for horizontal tension lines across the torso or crotch.
- Check whether the neckline stays where you want it.
- Try it with the trousers or skirt you plan to wear most often.

## ESKYNA note

A body is a useful wardrobe piece when it gives you a clean line and feels comfortable enough to forget you are wearing it. Choose it for fit and function first, then let neckline, fabric, and styling define its character.
