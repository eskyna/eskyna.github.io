## Definition

A **Digital Product Passport**, often shortened to DPP, is a digital record linked to a physical product. In fashion, it can provide accessible information about a garment, shoe, or accessory throughout its life cycle.

Depending on the product and the applicable system, the passport may contain details such as material composition, care instructions, repair information, origin, or other relevant product data. It is designed to improve transparency and support more informed decisions around use, resale, repair, and recycling.

## Digital Product Passport explained simply

Think of a Digital Product Passport as an expanded digital label. A conventional [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/) gives essential washing and material information, while a passport can make a wider set of product details available through a digital link.

For example, a QR code on a garment may lead to information that helps you understand what you own, how to care for it correctly, and what options may exist when you no longer wear it. The exact content is not identical for every product, brand, or system.

For wardrobe decisions, this matters because good information can make it easier to assess whether an item fits your standards for quality, care, and long-term use. It supports a more considered approach than relying on appearance, price, or trend appeal alone.

## Why Digital Product Passports matter in fashion

Fashion products often move through several stages after purchase. They are worn, washed, repaired, passed on, resold, donated, or recycled. Clear product information can be useful at each stage.

A Digital Product Passport may help you:

- understand a garment's material composition and care requirements
- make more informed decisions about repair and professional cleaning
- retain useful product details when reselling or passing an item on
- compare products beyond price and visual appeal
- support the principles of [circular fashion](/en/glossar/circular-fashion/), where products and materials are kept in use for longer

It can also support a more intentional wardrobe. When you know what an item is made of and how it should be cared for, you are better placed to judge its realistic maintenance needs and likely role in your everyday life.

## What information can a Digital Product Passport include?

The content varies. A Digital Product Passport may include information such as:

- fiber and material composition
- care and maintenance guidance
- product identification details
- information relevant to repair, reuse, resale, or recycling
- information about the product's supply chain or environmental characteristics, where provided

The presence of a passport does not guarantee that every possible detail is available. It also does not make a product inherently sustainable. What matters is the reliability, relevance, and clarity of the information provided.

## How to use a Digital Product Passport when shopping

A passport is most useful when you treat it as part of a wider purchase decision. Read it alongside the garment's fit, construction, price, and potential place in your wardrobe.

Ask practical questions:

- What is the fiber composition, and does it suit the season and intended use?
- Are the care instructions realistic for my routine?
- Does the item need specialist cleaning or delicate handling?
- Is repair information available if a zipper, button, or seam needs attention?
- Will I wear it often enough to justify its care and cost?
- Does it work with the [material profile](/en/glossar/materialprofil/) and color direction of my existing wardrobe?

This approach can reduce avoidable [impulse purchases](/en/glossar/spontankauf/) and help you choose pieces that are more likely to remain useful.

## Digital Product Passport and secondhand fashion

Digital product information can also be valuable in the secondhand market. A future owner may want to know the material composition, original care guidance, or other verified details before purchasing a pre-owned item.

That can make resale easier to evaluate, especially for pieces with complex fiber blends, specialist care needs, or limited visible labeling. Still, condition remains essential. A passport cannot replace checking for wear, stains, damage, alterations, or missing components when buying [secondhand](/en/glossar/secondhand/).

## Common misunderstandings

- **A Digital Product Passport is the same as a care label.**  
  A care label is part of the garment itself and provides mandatory or practical care information. A passport can provide broader digital product information, but its scope varies.

- **A Digital Product Passport proves a product is sustainable.**  
  It does not. It can improve access to information, but sustainability depends on many factors, including materials, production, use, care, longevity, and end-of-life options.

- **Every fashion item already has a Digital Product Passport.**  
  No. Availability differs by brand, product category, market, and regulatory development.

- **More data always means a better purchase.**  
  Information is useful only when it helps answer practical questions. Focus on the details that affect your use of the item, such as care, composition, repairability, and realistic versatility.

## Practical check

When you encounter a Digital Product Passport, check:

- whether the product details are specific and understandable
- whether fiber content and care instructions match your lifestyle
- whether the information helps you assess quality and expected longevity
- whether repair, resale, or recycling guidance is meaningful and relevant
- whether the piece fits your existing wardrobe and will be worn regularly

## ESKYNA key line

A Digital Product Passport does not make the decision for you. It gives you better information to choose, care for, and keep clothing in use more consciously.

## Advisory view

Use a Digital Product Passport to look beyond the fitting-room moment. The best wardrobe choices combine a convincing fit and visual impact with realistic care, reliable material information, and genuine potential for repeated wear.
