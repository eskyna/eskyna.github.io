## Definition

**Morning dress**, also known as **cutaway dress**, is one of the most formal dress codes for daytime events. Its defining garment is the **morning coat**, a dark coat with fronts that sweep back from the waist into long tails at the rear.

For traditional men's morning dress, the outfit commonly includes a black or grey morning coat, a waistcoat, formal striped trousers, a white shirt, tie, and polished formal shoes. It is worn for highly ceremonial daytime occasions, including traditional weddings, official ceremonies, and certain race meetings.

## Morning Dress Explained Simply

Despite its name, morning dress is not restricted to early hours. It is formal clothing for an event held during the day. The key distinction is timing: morning dress belongs to daytime formality, while [White Tie](/en/glossar/white-tie/) and [Black Tie](/en/glossar/black-tie/) are evening dress codes.

The cutaway coat creates a very specific silhouette. It is fitted through the torso, short at the front, and extends into tails at the back. This immediately separates it from a regular [Suit](/en/glossar/anzug/), a tuxedo, or a tailcoat for evening wear.

For women, the expectation is usually formal daytime dressing rather than one fixed uniform. An elegant day dress, tailored skirt or trouser ensemble, refined shoes, and understated accessories are appropriate starting points. At traditional events, a hat or [Fascinator](/en/glossar/fascinator/) may be part of the expected look.

## When Is Morning Dress Worn?

Morning dress is reserved for occasions where tradition and ceremony matter. Typical settings include:

- Formal daytime weddings
- Royal, diplomatic, civic, or ceremonial occasions
- Traditional race meetings
- Highly formal daytime receptions

Always treat the invitation as the main guide. Hosts may specify details such as a particular coat color, hat requirement, or whether a less traditional interpretation is welcome.

## Key Elements of Traditional Morning Dress

### The morning coat

The morning coat, often called a cutaway, is the central piece. It is traditionally black or grey and has curved front edges that taper away from the waist. Its long rear tails make it unmistakably formal.

### Waistcoat and trousers

A waistcoat adds structure and formality beneath the coat. Trousers are generally grey with stripes or a discreet pattern, rather than matching the coat exactly. This contrast is a classic part of the dress code.

### Shirt, tie, and shoes

A crisp white shirt, conservative tie, and well-polished formal shoes keep the look precise. Details should support the overall formality rather than compete for attention. A clean [Shirt Collar](/en/glossar/hemdkragen/), proper [Fit](/en/glossar/passform/), and correct trouser length matter more than trend-driven styling.

### Formal daytime accessories

For women, a hat or fascinator can complete a traditional morning dress outfit. Accessories should feel considered and occasion-appropriate. A small structured bag, restrained jewelry, and elegant footwear usually work better than overtly evening-focused pieces.

## Morning Dress vs. Other Formal Dress Codes

| Dress code      | Typical time                   | Defining garment                        |
| --------------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| Morning dress   | Formal daytime events          | Cutaway or morning coat                 |
| Black Tie       | Formal evening events          | Tuxedo or dinner jacket                 |
| White Tie       | The most formal evening events | Evening tailcoat                        |
| Business Formal | Professional settings          | Dark business suit or tailored ensemble |

Morning dress is not simply a business suit with a waistcoat. It has its own coat shape, trouser tradition, and ceremonial purpose. It is also different from a [Tuxedo](/en/glossar/smoking/), which is designed for evening occasions.

## What to Pay Attention To

- **Follow the invitation closely.** Formality can vary by country, venue, and event tradition.
- **Prioritize fit.** The coat should sit cleanly at the shoulders, and trousers should fall neatly over the shoes without excessive pooling.
- **Choose quiet, formal materials.** Fine wool and restrained textures support the dress code better than casual or highly shiny fabrics.
- **Keep the look polished.** Morning dress relies on careful grooming, clean shoes, and controlled accessories.
- **Respect the daytime context.** Avoid treating morning dress like an evening gala look.

For a formal wedding, the broader guidance in [Wedding Guest Outfit](/en/glossar/hochzeit-als-gast/) can help with color, restraint, and the role of the guest.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: Morning dress is only worn before noon.**  
  **Better:** It is formal attire for daytime events. The event can continue into the afternoon.

- **Misunderstanding: A dark business suit is an acceptable substitute.**  
  **Better:** A business suit is more appropriate for business formal settings. Morning dress requires the characteristic cutaway coat when explicitly requested.

- **Misunderstanding: Morning dress and White Tie use the same tailcoat.**  
  **Better:** They are different. Morning dress uses a cutaway coat for the day, while White Tie uses an evening tailcoat.

- **Misunderstanding: Women must wear a specific matching equivalent.**  
  **Better:** Expectations are less uniform, but formal daytime elegance and the event's traditions should guide the choice.

## Practical Check Before You Go

- Does the invitation explicitly say morning dress, cutaway, or formal daytime attire?
- Is the event held during the day and rooted in a ceremonial or traditional setting?
- Does the outfit look formal from head to toe, including shoes and accessories?
- Is the fit clean when standing, walking, and sitting?
- Have you checked whether a hat or fascinator is expected?
