## Definition

A **pocket square** is a decorative square of fabric worn in the breast pocket of a [jacket](/en/glossar/sakko/), [blazer](/en/glossar/blazer/), or [tuxedo](/en/glossar/smoking/). It is folded, puffed, or arranged so that part of the fabric remains visible above the pocket edge.

Unlike a handkerchief, a pocket square has a purely visual role. It adds a controlled detail near the face and can bring together the colors, textures, and formality of an outfit.

## Pocket Square Explained Simply

Think of a pocket square as a small styling accent rather than a formal obligation. A plain white linen square can make a suit look sharper and more polished. A silk square with a subtle pattern can make the same jacket feel more personal or festive.

The effect depends on three elements:

- **Material:** Silk appears smooth and refined, linen more crisp and understated, cotton more relaxed, and wool more textured.
- **Color and pattern:** A pocket square may repeat a secondary color from the outfit, introduce a gentle contrast, or add a pattern to a plain jacket.
- **Fold:** A clean straight fold looks formal and calm. A softer puff fold looks more relaxed and expressive.

Because it sits close to the face, the pocket square can support [color harmony](/en/glossar/farbharmonie/) and create a subtle [focal point](/en/glossar/fokuspunkt/) without overwhelming the look.

## How to Wear a Pocket Square

A pocket square should look intentional but not overly perfect. The visible portion is usually modest, and the fabric should sit comfortably in the breast pocket without stretching or bulging it.

For a classic outfit, choose a white linen or cotton pocket square with a simple straight fold. This works especially well with a dark suit, a white shirt, and a [tie](/en/glossar/krawatte/).

For more relaxed tailoring, a softly folded silk, cotton, or wool pocket square can add color and texture to a blazer worn without a tie. In this setting, the accessory can make a casual jacket look more considered without becoming formal.

With a tuxedo, a restrained white pocket square is a classic choice. It supports the polished character of [evening wear](/en/glossar/abendgarderobe/) without competing with the formal elements of the outfit.

## Choosing the Right Pocket Square

### For business and formal occasions

Choose restrained colors, fine materials, and a neat fold. White linen, cream, soft blue, or a quiet geometric pattern can work well, depending on the jacket and shirt.

A pocket square should support the overall [dress code](/en/glossar/dresscode/), not become the main attraction. For very formal occasions, less contrast and less volume usually create the clearest result.

### For festive or creative looks

A stronger color, print, or textured fabric can become a deliberate accent. The pocket square can echo a shirt stripe, the tone of a [belt](/en/glossar/guertel/), or another small accessory.

If the jacket already has a pronounced check, herringbone, or other pattern, a solid pocket square is often easier to combine. If the jacket is plain, a patterned square can add interest.

### For smart casual styling

With a blazer, knitwear, or an open collar shirt, choose a pocket square that feels relaxed rather than ceremonial. Matte cotton, linen, or wool often looks more natural than high shine silk.

## Pocket Square Folds and Their Effect

You do not need many complicated folds. A few reliable options are enough.

- **Straight fold:** A visible horizontal edge. It looks crisp, minimal, and formal.
- **Puff fold:** A soft, rounded shape with a little volume. It feels relaxed and works particularly well with silk.
- **Point fold:** One or several peaks are visible. This creates more visual movement and can suit festive occasions.
- **Casual fold:** The fabric is placed loosely in the pocket. It works best when the material has texture and the overall outfit is not highly formal.

The fold should fit the fabric. Linen holds a sharp line well, while silk naturally creates softer shapes because of its [fabric drape](/en/glossar/materialfall/).

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: A pocket square must exactly match the tie.**  
  **Better:** Let the colors relate without copying each other. Picking up a secondary tone usually looks more natural than wearing a pre-matched set.

- **Misunderstanding: Every suit needs a pocket square.**  
  **Better:** A pocket square is optional. It is useful when you want a more polished, festive, or individual finish.

- **Misunderstanding: A pocket square is the same as a handkerchief.**  
  **Better:** A pocket square is decorative. A practical handkerchief should be kept separately and not worn in the breast pocket.

- **Misunderstanding: Larger and fuller always looks better.**  
  **Better:** Too much fabric can distort the breast pocket and distract from the jacket line. A smaller, controlled amount usually looks more elegant.

## Practical Check Before You Leave

- Does the pocket square suit the formality of the jacket and occasion?
- Does it relate to the outfit colors without matching the tie exactly?
- Does the fold fit the material and stay comfortably in place?
- Is the breast pocket lying flat rather than bulging?
- Does the square add a detail rather than compete with other visible accessories?

## ESKYNA Key Line

> A pocket square does not need to copy the outfit. Its role is to create a small, visible connection between color, texture, and personal style.

## Advisory View

A pocket square is especially useful when an outfit feels correct but slightly unfinished. It can bring clarity to a suit or blazer look, provided its color, material, and fold are chosen in relation to the whole outfit.

For a cohesive result, consider the jacket fabric, shirt, [tie](/en/glossar/krawatte/), and occasion together. The most effective pocket square is often not the boldest one, but the one that makes the entire look feel more complete.
