## Definition

**Interlock jersey** is a double-knit fabric with a smooth surface on both the front and reverse sides. It is generally denser, heavier, and more stable than standard [jersey](/en/glossar/jersey/), which is often made as a lighter single knit.

The fabric has a soft handfeel and comfortable stretch, while its construction gives garments a more substantial, refined appearance. Interlock jersey is commonly used when a piece should feel easy to wear but hold its shape better than a thin T-shirt fabric.

## What Makes Interlock Jersey Different?

The key difference is its construction. Single jersey has one main visible face and a different reverse side, while interlock is made from two connected knit layers. This creates a more balanced fabric with a similar look and feel on both sides.

In practice, interlock jersey often:

- Feels thicker and smoother than lightweight jersey
- Offers better coverage because it is less transparent
- Keeps a cleaner shape in tops, dresses, and skirts
- Curls less at raw edges than single jersey
- Has a more polished, less casual visual effect

Do not assume every interlock fabric feels the same. An interlock made from cotton will behave differently from one made with [modal](/en/glossar/modal/), [viscose](/en/glossar/viskose/), polyester, or elastane. Fiber content, finishing, and [fabric weight](/en/glossar/stoffgewicht/) all influence the final result.

## Effect on Fit, Comfort, and Outfit Impact

Interlock jersey offers a useful middle ground between a very soft knit and a structured woven fabric. It moves with the body, but it usually has enough substance to skim rather than cling tightly.

This makes it a strong choice for simple garments where fabric quality is visible. A plain interlock T-shirt can look more considered than a very thin jersey version, while an interlock dress may feel comfortable without looking overly casual.

Because the fabric is often more opaque, it can be especially practical for light colors, fitted silhouettes, and pieces worn without extra layers. Still, opacity varies by color, stretch, and fabric weight. White or pale interlock garments should always be checked in daylight before wearing.

## How to Wear Interlock Jersey

Interlock jersey works best in clothing with clean lines and uncomplicated styling. Its smooth surface suits minimalist outfits, everyday basics, and comfortable smart-casual looks.

### For everyday basics

Choose interlock T-shirts, long-sleeve tops, or tanks when you want a neater alternative to a lightweight tee. Pair them with [jeans](/en/glossar/jeans/), tailored trousers, or an A-line skirt for an easy outfit with more visual structure.

### For dresses and skirts

Interlock is useful for knit dresses, shift shapes, and softly fitted styles. The fabric can provide comfortable movement while maintaining a clearer silhouette than a thin, drapey knit. A well-fitting interlock dress can be layered with a [blazer](/en/glossar/blazer/) for work or dressed down with sneakers.

### For layering

A smooth interlock top is a practical base layer under knitwear, jackets, and overshirts. Its stable surface tends to sit neatly beneath other pieces, especially when the fit is close but not restrictive.

### For comfort-focused clothing

The soft feel and durable structure of interlock make it popular for underwear, loungewear, sleepwear, and baby clothing. For close-fitting pieces, check the fiber composition and the amount of [stretch](/en/glossar/stretch/), as these determine how the garment recovers after movement and washing.

## Interlock Jersey vs Single Jersey

| Feature         | Interlock Jersey                        | Single Jersey                            |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Construction    | Double-knit structure                   | Single-knit structure                    |
| Surface         | Smooth on both sides                    | Front and reverse usually look different |
| Weight          | Often medium weight or more substantial | Often lighter in weight                  |
| Shape retention | Generally better                        | Can be softer and less stable            |
| Raw edges       | Less likely to curl                     | More likely to curl                      |
| Typical effect  | Smooth, polished, stable                | Soft, casual, light                      |

Neither fabric is automatically better. Single jersey can be ideal for airy T-shirts and relaxed summer layers. Interlock is often the better option when you want more coverage, durability, and shape stability.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Interlock jersey is the same as every other jersey fabric.**  
  Interlock is a specific type of [jersey](/en/glossar/jersey/) construction. Its double-knit structure gives it a different weight, surface, and stability from typical single jersey.

- **Interlock always contains elastane.**  
  It may have some natural knit stretch without elastane. Added elastane can increase stretch and recovery, but it is not required for interlock construction.

- **Thicker always means better quality.**  
  A heavier fabric can feel more substantial, but quality also depends on the fibers, knit density, finishing, sewing, and how well the garment performs after wear and care.

- **Interlock cannot cling to the body.**  
  Interlock is often more forgiving than thin jersey, but a close cut, a very stretchy blend, or a light color can still reveal body lines and underwear edges.

## Practical Check Before Buying

When choosing an interlock jersey garment, look beyond the fabric name.

- Hold it up to the light to assess opacity, especially in white, cream, or pale shades.
- Gently stretch the fabric and release it. It should recover smoothly without looking distorted.
- Check whether the garment keeps its shape around the neckline, hems, knees, and elbows.
- Read the fiber label to understand softness, breathability, and care needs.
- Try it on in motion. Sit, raise your arms, and bend slightly to confirm that the fabric remains comfortable and opaque.
- Examine seams and hems, since good construction supports the fabric's natural stability.

## Care Tips for Interlock Jersey

Follow the garment's [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/), as care needs depend on the fiber blend. In general, washing inside out on a gentle cycle and avoiding excessive heat can help preserve color, softness, and shape.

For garments containing cotton or viscose, high dryer heat may increase the risk of shrinkage. If the item contains elastane, heat can also affect stretch recovery over time. Reshape the garment while damp and dry it according to the label instructions.
