## Definition

**Prêt-à-porter**, often called **ready-to-wear**, is finished clothing produced in standardized sizes and sold without being made specifically for one customer. The French term literally means “ready to wear.”

Ready-to-wear collections are designed, sampled, and manufactured for multiple customers. They can range from accessible everyday basics to designer pieces and luxury fashion. The term describes the production and sales format, not automatically the quality, price, or style of a garment.

## Ready-to-Wear Explained Simply

When you buy a blazer in a shop or online in a standard size, it is ready-to-wear. It was developed for a set of body measurements rather than for your individual proportions.

This distinguishes ready-to-wear from [bespoke or custom-made clothing](/en/glossar/massanfertigung-custom-made/), which is ordered and made or substantially adapted for one person. It also differs from [haute couture](/en/glossar/haute-couture/), where individual craftsmanship and client-specific work are central.

A ready-to-wear piece can still fit exceptionally well, be made from excellent materials, and have refined construction. Conversely, an expensive label does not guarantee that a standard garment suits your body, lifestyle, or wardrobe.

## Ready-to-Wear vs Custom-Made vs Haute Couture

| Type          | How it is made                              | Sizing                  | Typical buying process                                |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Ready-to-wear | Produced as part of a collection            | Standardized sizes      | Bought ready-made in store or online                  |
| Custom-made   | Made or extensively adapted for one person  | Individual measurements | Ordered with personal specifications                  |
| Haute couture | Highly individualized, hand-crafted fashion | Individual measurements | Created through specialist fittings and craftsmanship |

Small alterations do not turn ready-to-wear into custom-made clothing. Shortening trousers, taking in a waist, or adjusting sleeve length simply helps a standard garment work better for your proportions.

## Why Ready-to-Wear Fit Matters

Standardized sizes are useful, but they cannot reflect every variation in shoulder width, torso length, waist position, hip shape, or leg length. That is why the size label is only a starting point.

Focus on the areas that are hardest or most costly to alter:

- **Shoulders:** A jacket shoulder should sit close to your natural shoulder line.
- **Upper body:** Check whether the garment pulls across the bust, back, or armhole.
- **Waist and hip:** Make sure you can sit, walk, and move comfortably.
- **Length:** Consider sleeve, trouser, and skirt length in relation to your shoes and proportions.
- **Movement:** Enough [movement allowance](/en/glossar/bewegungszugabe/) prevents pulling and restrictive tension lines.

Understanding [fit](/en/glossar/passform/) makes ready-to-wear shopping more efficient. You learn which labels, cuts, and size ranges tend to work for you, rather than relying on a number that varies from brand to brand.

## What to Check Before Buying Ready-to-Wear Clothing

A thoughtful purchase looks beyond the label and the changing room mirror.

- Compare the garment’s measurements with a piece you already wear often.
- Check the shoulder line, neckline, rise, and overall length first.
- Look inside for construction details such as lining, seams, hems, and possible seam allowance.
- Consider whether the item works with your existing [wardrobe](/en/glossar/garderobe/) and at least three other pieces.
- Decide whether an alteration would improve the garment enough to justify the additional cost.
- Consider fabric, care requirements, and likely [cost per wear](/en/glossar/cost-per-wear/).

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Ready-to-wear means low quality.**  
  Ready-to-wear can be inexpensive, premium, or luxurious. Quality depends on material, craftsmanship, cut, and finishing, not on whether a garment comes in standard sizes.

- **The size label should determine the purchase.**  
  Sizing differs between brands and even between collections. Actual measurements and comfort are more reliable than the label.

- **A garment must fit perfectly without alterations.**  
  Some minor alterations are normal, especially for trouser length, sleeve length, or waist adjustment. The key is to start with a good fit in the shoulders, upper body, and overall balance.

- **Ready-to-wear and mass production are identical.**  
  Ready-to-wear means that garments are made before a specific customer buys them. Production quantities can vary considerably.

## ESKYNA Key Line

> Ready-to-wear is made for standardized sizes. The right cut, fit, and small alterations make it work for your body and your real life.

## Advisory View

Ready-to-wear becomes far more personal when you understand your preferred [proportions](/en/glossar/proportion/), reliable cuts, and realistic alteration options. This turns shopping from a search for the “correct” size into a more precise decision about fit, comfort, quality, and versatility.
