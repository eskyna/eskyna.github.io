## Definition

A **leg-of-mutton sleeve**, also known as a **gigot sleeve**, is a sleeve shape that is wide and rounded at the shoulder or upper arm, then narrows visibly toward a fitted wrist or cuff. Its profile resembles the curved shape of a leg of mutton, which gives the sleeve its traditional name.

This sleeve creates an expressive upper-body silhouette. It directs attention to the shoulders, adds height and structure, and makes even a simple blouse, dress, or knit look more deliberate.

## How a Leg-of-Mutton Sleeve Looks

The defining feature is the contrast between volume at the top and closeness at the bottom. A typical leg-of-mutton sleeve has:

- fullness created at the shoulder through gathers, pleats, or a shaped sleeve head
- a rounded, sometimes dramatic upper arm
- a sleeve that tapers from the elbow downward
- a narrow cuff, fitted wrist, or close lower sleeve

The amount of volume varies widely. Some designs make a clear historical statement, while others use only a gentle shoulder puff for an everyday interpretation.

## Leg-of-Mutton Sleeve vs. Puff Sleeve

A leg-of-mutton sleeve belongs to the broader family of voluminous sleeves, but it is more specific than a [puff sleeve](/en/glossar/puffaermel/).

A puff sleeve may be full at the shoulder, elbow, or hem, and it often remains relatively loose throughout. A leg-of-mutton sleeve is defined by its strong taper: it begins full and ends narrow. A [bishop sleeve](/en/glossar/bischofsaermel/), by contrast, is usually fuller toward the wrist and gathered into a cuff.

## Effect on Proportion and Silhouette

Because it adds visual width and height at the shoulder, a leg-of-mutton sleeve can noticeably change [proportion](/en/glossar/proportion/) and [shoulder line](/en/glossar/schulterlinie/). It often gives an outfit a romantic, vintage-inspired, theatrical, or fashion-forward character, depending on fabric and styling.

The sleeve can be useful when you want to:

- bring attention upward toward the face and shoulders
- add shape to a simple, straight outfit
- create a more defined shoulder presence
- balance fuller skirts, wide-leg trousers, or visual volume at the hips
- make a minimal outfit feel more expressive without adding many accessories

For a quieter result, pair the sleeve with streamlined pieces such as straight-leg jeans, a pencil skirt, or tailored trousers. The contrast lets the sleeve remain the focal point without making the whole outfit feel overloaded.

## Fabric and Fit Matter

The effect of a leg-of-mutton sleeve depends greatly on material and construction. Crisp cotton poplin, taffeta, organza, or structured woven fabrics can hold pronounced volume. Softer jersey, viscose, or fine knitwear creates a more relaxed version with less dramatic lift.

A good fit is essential. The shoulder seam should sit correctly, and the upper sleeve should have enough room for movement without pulling across the arm. The lower sleeve needs to feel close but not restrictive, especially when bending the elbow or reaching forward. Learn more about [fit](/en/glossar/passform/) and [movement allowance](/en/glossar/bewegungszugabe/) when assessing a sleeve in the fitting room.

## How to Style a Leg-of-Mutton Sleeve

A leg-of-mutton sleeve is easiest to wear when the rest of the outfit has a clear line.

- **For everyday wear:** Choose a blouse with moderate shoulder volume and combine it with jeans and simple loafers or ankle boots.
- **For the office:** Wear a structured sleeve blouse with tailored trousers or a narrow skirt. Keep jewelry and other details restrained.
- **For a romantic look:** Combine a sleeve-heavy dress or blouse with a flowing skirt, delicate shoes, and minimal accessories.
- **For a modern contrast:** Style a dramatic sleeve with clean denim, flat shoes, or a sporty piece. This use of [style contrast](/en/glossar/stilbruch/) can make the silhouette feel current rather than costume-like.
- **For evening:** A leg-of-mutton sleeve in satin, velvet, or taffeta can add impact to a simple dress without relying on a deep neckline or heavy embellishment.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **A leg-of-mutton sleeve is just any puffed sleeve.**  
  Not quite. The narrow lower arm is central to the shape. Volume at the shoulder alone does not automatically make a sleeve leg-of-mutton.

- **The sleeve must look historical or theatrical.**  
  It can, but it does not have to. Small-scale versions are common in contemporary blouses, knitwear, and dresses.

- **More volume always creates a better silhouette.**  
  The right amount of [volume](/en/glossar/volumen/) depends on the garment, fabric, body proportions, and the effect you want. A softly shaped version may be more versatile than an extreme one.

## Practical Check Before Buying

- Raise and bend your arms to check whether the sleeve pulls at the shoulder or elbow.
- Look at the shoulder seam from the front and side. It should support the intended shape rather than collapse or sit too far outward.
- Check whether the narrow lower sleeve is comfortable over your wrist and forearm.
- Consider whether the sleeve works with your usual bottoms, outerwear, and accessories.
- Assess the garment in movement. The sleeve should retain its shape without feeling stiff or impractical.
