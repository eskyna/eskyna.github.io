## Definition

**Knitted fabric** is a textile made from a continuous series of interlocking yarn loops. The looped construction gives the material flexibility and often a degree of natural stretch, making it especially suitable for clothing that needs to move with the body.

Knitted fabric is a construction category, not one single material. It can be made from cotton, wool, silk, viscose, polyester, polyamide, or fiber blends. A fine cotton jersey T shirt, a wool sweater, and stretchy activewear can all be knitted fabrics, yet they feel and perform very differently.

## How Knitted Fabric Differs From Woven Fabric

The key difference lies in the construction.

A [woven fabric](/en/glossar/webstoff/) is made by interlacing lengthwise and crosswise yarns at right angles. This often creates a stable fabric with a clearer structure and less natural give.

Knitted fabric is formed from loops connected to one another. When the fabric is pulled, the loops can shift and expand, which is why many knits feel softer, more flexible, and easier to move in. This does not mean every knit stretches equally in every direction. The construction, yarn tension, and fiber content all matter.

A simple way to recognize a knit is to look closely at the surface or reverse side. You may see small V shaped loops, horizontal rows, or a visibly looped texture. Fine knits can be much less obvious, so the care label and fabric composition can also help.

## Common Types of Knitted Fabric

Knitted fabric appears across everyday clothing, lingerie, sportswear, and occasionwear. Common examples include:

- **[Jersey](/en/glossar/jersey/):** A soft, flexible knit often used for T shirts, dresses, leggings, and underwear.
- **[Interlock](/en/glossar/interlock/):** A more stable, thicker double knit with a smooth surface on both sides.
- **Rib knit:** A knit with raised vertical ribs that creates visible texture and substantial stretch.
- **Fine knit:** A lightweight knit made with finer yarns, often used for refined sweaters, cardigans, and tops.
- **Sweatshirt fabric:** A casual knit fabric that may have looped French terry or brushed fleece on the inside.

The term [knitwear](/en/glossar/strick/) usually refers to finished garments made from knitted materials, especially sweaters, cardigans, knit dresses, and vests. Knitted fabric itself describes the underlying textile structure.

## Effect on Fit, Comfort, and Style

Knitted fabric can make clothing feel more forgiving because it follows movement rather than resisting it. This can be useful in close fitting tops, bodycon dresses, leggings, underwear, and loungewear. The amount of ease still matters: a stretchy fabric can feel restrictive if a garment is cut too small.

The material also affects the visual impression of an outfit:

- Fine, smooth knits can look polished, clean, and understated.
- Chunkier knits add texture, softness, and visual volume.
- Fluid jersey can create a relaxed silhouette or follow the body closely, depending on its weight and cut.
- Dense, stable knits can look more structured than lightweight, drapey knits.

Because knitted fabrics are often flexible, they can emphasize body contours more readily than crisp woven fabrics. Consider the fabric weight, opacity, and recovery before choosing a close fit. A lightweight pale jersey may show underwear lines, while a dense interlock knit can provide more coverage and structure.

## How to Choose Knitted Fabric

When shopping, choose the knit construction according to how and where you want to wear the garment.

For everyday basics, look for a fabric that feels comfortable against the skin and returns to shape after being gently stretched. Good recovery helps necklines, cuffs, waistbands, and knees retain a neat appearance.

For fitted clothing, check opacity and support. Hold the fabric lightly against the hand or try the garment on in daylight. If the knit becomes very sheer when stretched, consider whether it needs a lining, a smooth base layer, or more room through the body.

For warm layers, fiber content and knit density matter as much as thickness. Wool and other animal fibers can provide warmth without extreme bulk, while cotton knits often feel breathable and familiar. Synthetic fibers can add durability, stretch, or quicker drying properties depending on the blend.

For a more tailored look, choose a firmer knit with a smooth finish. For softness and movement, select a lighter knit with more drape. Understanding [fabric drape](/en/glossar/materialfall/) makes it easier to predict whether a knit will skim the body, cling to it, or hold its shape.

## Care Considerations

Knitted fabric can stretch, snag, pill, or lose shape if it is treated roughly. Always follow the garment's [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/), as care needs vary widely by fiber and construction.

A few practical habits help knitted garments last:

- Avoid hanging heavy sweaters, which can stretch the shoulders and lengthen the body.
- Fold knitwear for storage when possible.
- Wash delicate knits gently and reshape them while damp if the care instructions allow.
- Keep rough bags, jewelry, and abrasive surfaces away from fine knits to reduce snagging.
- Address [pilling](/en/glossar/pilling/) carefully with a suitable fabric shaver when needed.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: All knitted fabric is thick and warm.**  
  **Better:** Knits range from airy mesh and lightweight jersey to dense wool sweaters. Construction and fiber determine warmth.

- **Misunderstanding: Knitted fabric always contains elastane.**  
  **Better:** Many knits stretch because of their looped structure alone. Elastane increases stretch and recovery but is not essential to make a fabric a knit.

- **Misunderstanding: Knitted fabric and knitwear mean exactly the same thing.**  
  **Better:** Knitted fabric describes the textile construction. Knitwear usually describes garments made from knitted textiles.

- **Misunderstanding: Stretchy fabric always means a better fit.**  
  **Better:** Stretch can improve comfort, but a garment still needs appropriate proportions, enough room for movement, and good shape recovery.

## Practical Check

Before buying a garment made from knitted fabric, check the following:

- Gently stretch a small area, then release it to see whether the fabric recovers smoothly.
- Look at the fabric against light to assess opacity, especially in pale colors or close fits.
- Check whether seams, hems, and necklines lie flat without pulling or rippling.
- Move your arms, sit down, and bend naturally to test comfort and shape retention.
- Read the fiber content and care instructions before deciding whether the garment suits your routine.
- Consider whether the fabric's texture, weight, and drape support the silhouette you want.

## ESKYNA Key Line

Knitted fabric brings flexibility and comfort through its looped construction, but its quality and visual effect depend on the yarn, density, weight, and recovery.

## Advisory View

Understanding knitted fabric helps you make better choices between soft jersey basics, structured double knits, fine sweaters, and stretch garments. Rather than judging a fabric by stretch alone, assess how it feels, moves, recovers, and works with the intended fit.
