## Definition

A **cuff** is a finished band, folded edge, or separate fabric section at the end of a sleeve or trouser leg. Most often, the term refers to the part of a shirt, blouse, jacket, or knitwear sleeve that sits at the wrist.

Cuffs give an edge structure and help determine how a garment sits on the body. On shirts, they can also provide a closure. On trousers, cuffs are visible folded turn-ups at the hem.

## Cuff Meaning in Clothing

In everyday fashion language, a cuff usually means the wrist section of a long sleeve. It may be made from the same fabric as the garment, reinforced for a firmer shape, finished with elastic, or formed by a folded knit rib.

A cuff can be subtle, as on a fine knit sweater, or a defining design feature, as on a crisp white shirt with wide cuffs. It affects the visual ending point of the sleeve and therefore has a direct influence on [sleeve length](/en/glossar/aermellaenge/), hand visibility, and the balance of an outfit.

On trousers, a cuff is a folded band at the bottom of the leg. This detail adds visual weight near the shoe and creates a clear horizontal line. It is therefore closely connected to the relationship between trouser length, shoes, and overall [proportion](/en/glossar/proportion/).

## Types of Cuffs

### Shirt cuffs

Shirt cuffs are usually structured sections at the wrist that close with buttons or, in some cases, [cufflinks](/en/glossar/manschettenknoepfe/). Common variations include:

- **Button cuffs:** Cuffs that fasten with one or more buttons. They are common on everyday shirts and blouses.
- **Double cuffs:** Longer cuffs folded back onto themselves and usually closed with cufflinks. They are often seen in formal shirts.
- **Convertible cuffs:** Cuffs designed to be worn with either buttons or cufflinks.
- **Barrel cuffs:** Rounded or straight button cuffs that wrap around the wrist.

The cuff construction is an important quality and styling detail on a [shirt](/en/glossar/hemd/). A crisp, well-fitting cuff can make even a simple outfit look more considered.

### Knit cuffs

On sweaters, cardigans, sweatshirts, and some jackets, cuffs are often made from rib knit or elastic material. Their practical role is to hold the sleeve in place, but they also influence the garment’s shape.

A narrow rib cuff gathers volume and creates a more defined sleeve ending. A loose or extended cuff feels softer and more relaxed. When choosing knitwear, check whether the cuff stays in position comfortably or feels too tight at the wrist.

### Trouser cuffs

Trouser cuffs are outward folds at the lower leg. They are sometimes called turn-ups. They can add a classic tailored character to trousers, particularly when paired with [tailored trousers](/en/glossar/stoffhose/) or a suit.

Because they form a visible horizontal line, trouser cuffs can make the lower leg appear visually shorter. This is not inherently good or bad. It simply means the detail should work with the garment length, shoe shape, and the desired balance of the outfit.

## Why Cuffs Matter for Fit and Proportion

Cuffs are small details, but they create clear visual endpoints. A sleeve cuff tells the eye where the arm ends. A trouser cuff marks the transition to the shoe. These lines can change how tidy, relaxed, structured, or formal a look appears.

Consider the following:

- **Width:** A wider cuff becomes more visible and can create a stronger accent at the wrist or ankle.
- **Contrast:** A cuff in a contrasting fabric or color draws attention to that area.
- **Structure:** Firm cuffs appear sharper and more tailored, while soft cuffs feel more casual.
- **Position:** A cuff that ends at the wrist bone usually looks intentional and allows jewellery or a watch to remain visible.
- **Volume:** A narrow cuff on a voluminous sleeve can gather the fabric and create shape. A loose cuff lets the sleeve fall more freely.

These details support the broader [silhouette](/en/glossar/silhouette/) rather than existing in isolation.

## How to Check the Fit of a Sleeve Cuff

A well-fitting sleeve cuff should feel secure without restricting movement.

- It should sit around the wrist without slipping over the hand.
- It should not press into the skin or make bending the wrist uncomfortable.
- The sleeve should not pull across the forearm when you move your arm.
- Button placement should lie flat rather than strain or gape.
- If you wear a watch, consider whether the cuff fits comfortably over or beside it.

For a tailored shirt, the cuff should usually extend slightly beyond the sleeve of a jacket when your arms rest naturally at your sides. The exact amount depends on the jacket and shirt fit, but the result should look deliberate rather than accidental.

## Cuff vs Hem: What Is the Difference?

A [hem](/en/glossar/saum/) is the finished lower edge of a garment, such as the bottom of a skirt, dress, sleeve, or trouser leg. A cuff is a specific kind of finished edge that is folded, attached, or constructed as a distinct band.

Not every hem is a cuff. For example, the lower edge of a T-shirt is typically a hem. A buttoned section around the wrist of a shirt is a cuff. A folded turn-up on trousers is also a cuff.

## Styling Cuffs Intentionally

Cuffs can help refine an outfit without adding extra pieces.

- Roll the cuff of a casual shirt to reveal the wrist and make the look feel more relaxed.
- Keep structured shirt cuffs closed and neat when you want a polished business or formal impression.
- Use cufflinks with a double cuff shirt when the occasion calls for a more dressed-up finish.
- Balance wide sleeve cuffs with cleaner lines elsewhere if you want the detail to remain the focal point.
- Check trouser cuffs with the shoes you plan to wear, because the cuff changes the visual length of the leg.

A cuff is most effective when it supports the garment’s [fit](/en/glossar/passform/), the occasion, and the level of structure you want your outfit to communicate.

## Common Misunderstandings

**Misunderstanding:** A cuff only means the buttoned end of a formal shirt.  
**Better:** Shirt cuffs are the best-known version, but cuffs can also be elastic knit endings, sleeve bands, and folded trouser turn-ups.

**Misunderstanding:** Wider cuffs always look more formal.  
**Better:** Formality depends on fabric, construction, closure, and the rest of the garment. A wide cuff can look formal, dramatic, relaxed, or fashion-focused.

**Misunderstanding:** Trouser cuffs work with every trouser length.  
**Better:** A cuff adds a horizontal visual line and needs enough length to sit cleanly above the shoe. The effect should be assessed with the actual footwear.

## Quick Mirror Check

When wearing cuffs, look at the outfit from a short distance and ask:

- Does the sleeve end at a comfortable, intentional point?
- Does the cuff add useful structure or unwanted bulk?
- Is the cuff width in balance with the garment’s volume?
- Do trouser cuffs sit cleanly above the shoe?
- Does the detail fit the formality of the occasion?
