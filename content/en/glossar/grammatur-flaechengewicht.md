## Definition

**Fabric weight**, often stated as **GSM**, is the weight of a textile measured in grams per square metre. It is also called grammage, area weight, or fabric weight per unit area.

GSM is a practical specification for comparing fabrics. It can help indicate whether a material is likely to feel light and airy, substantial and structured, or dense and durable. However, it is only one part of the picture. The same GSM can feel very different depending on the fiber, weave, knit, finish, and construction.

## What fabric weight tells you

Fabric weight influences how a garment is likely to behave in everyday wear. In general, a lighter fabric may feel cooler and move more freely, while a heavier one may provide more structure, coverage, and resistance to wear.

It can affect:

- **Opacity:** Lighter fabrics can be more transparent, particularly in pale colors or when stretched.
- **Drape:** Lower weight fabrics often fall softly, while more substantial fabrics can hold a defined shape.
- **Warmth:** Heavier fabric may offer more insulation, but warmth also depends strongly on fiber type and construction.
- **Durability:** A higher GSM can be helpful for items exposed to friction, though construction quality remains essential.
- **Silhouette:** Fabric weight affects whether a garment skims the body, flows, stands away from it, or holds crisp lines.
- **Care and comfort:** Weight can influence drying time, crease behavior, and how the garment feels across seasons.

For a fuller assessment, consider fabric weight alongside [fabric density](/en/glossar/dichte/), [opacity](/en/glossar/opazitaet/), [fabric drape](/en/glossar/materialfall/), and [handfeel](/en/glossar/griff/).

## How to use GSM when shopping

GSM is especially useful when comparing similar fabrics online, where you cannot touch the material. It gives more context than a product description such as “lightweight” or “premium,” although it should never be the only reason to buy.

A lighter fabric weight may suit:

- Summer shirts, blouses, and relaxed dresses
- Fine base layers and lightweight knitwear
- Scarves, linings, and flowing skirts
- Warm weather [resortwear](/en/glossar/resortwear/)

A medium fabric weight may suit:

- Everyday T shirts, shirts, trousers, and dresses
- Denim, jersey, and versatile knitwear
- Pieces that need a balance of comfort, coverage, and shape

A heavier fabric weight may suit:

- Structured trousers, jackets, and coats
- Dense sweatshirts and durable workwear
- Upholstery inspired bags or accessories
- Garments where a clear silhouette or added coverage is important

These are broad tendencies, not fixed rules. A lightweight wool can feel warmer than a heavier cotton, while a dense synthetic fabric can feel more substantial than its GSM suggests.

## Why fabric weight matters for fit and style

Fabric weight changes how a cut appears on the body. A softly draping fabric can emphasize movement and fluid lines, while a firm fabric can create cleaner edges and more visible volume.

For example, a wide leg trouser in a light fabric may flow and move easily, whereas the same cut in a heavier fabric can look more architectural. A close fitting top made from a very light jersey may reveal more than expected, while a more substantial knit can create a smoother surface.

This is why fabric weight is useful when considering [fit](/en/glossar/passform/), [silhouette](/en/glossar/silhouette/), and [proportion](/en/glossar/proportion/). It helps you anticipate whether a garment will deliver the look shown in product photos and whether it works for your climate, lifestyle, and preferred level of structure.

## Common misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: Higher GSM always means higher quality.**  
  **Better:** A higher number only means more fabric mass per square metre. Quality also depends on fiber quality, construction, finishing, stitching, and how well the item performs over time.

- **Misunderstanding: GSM tells you the exact thickness of a fabric.**  
  **Better:** Thickness and GSM are related but not identical. A lofty fabric can be thick but light, while a dense fabric can be thin yet comparatively heavy.

- **Misunderstanding: Heavy fabric is always warmer.**  
  **Better:** Warmth depends on fibers, air trapped within the fabric, construction, layering, and wind resistance. Weight alone cannot predict thermal performance.

- **Misunderstanding: A low GSM fabric is automatically poor quality.**  
  **Better:** Fine silk, voile, batiste, and other lightweight textiles are intentionally light. Their quality should be judged by their intended use, construction, feel, and durability.

## Practical check before buying

When fabric weight is listed, use it as part of a quick quality and suitability check:

1. **Check the garment category.** Ask whether the fabric weight suits the purpose, such as a breezy summer blouse or a structured overshirt.
2. **Read the fiber composition.** Compare GSM with the material, such as [linen](/en/glossar/leinen/), [cotton](/en/glossar/baumwolle/), wool, or polyester.
3. **Look for lining details.** A lining can improve comfort and opacity in lightweight skirts, dresses, and trousers.
4. **Review product photos closely.** Look for visible underwear, pulling, shine, or excessive creasing, while remembering that studio lighting can alter the result.
5. **Consider your climate and use.** A substantial fabric may be ideal for cool weather but uncomfortable in heat or for frequent travel.
6. **Assess the whole construction.** Check seams, finishing, stretch, and care requirements rather than relying on GSM alone.

## ESKYNA Key Line

Fabric weight in GSM is a useful clue to how a textile may feel and perform, but the best choice always depends on fiber, construction, fit, and intended use.

## Advisory view

Use GSM to make more informed comparisons, especially when shopping online. It can help you avoid overly sheer summer pieces, overly heavy layers, or garments whose structure does not suit the silhouette you want. For the clearest decision, pair fabric weight with an assessment of [material quality](/en/glossar/materialqualitaet/), drape, opacity, and real wardrobe needs.
