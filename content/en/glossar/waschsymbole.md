## Definition

**Laundry symbols** are the small icons printed on a garment's [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/). They provide instructions for washing, bleaching, drying, ironing, and professional cleaning.

They are practical information, not decorative details. Reading laundry symbols correctly helps preserve a garment's shape, color, surface, and useful life.

## Laundry Symbols Explained Simply

Most clothing labels use five core symbol groups:

- A **wash tub** tells you whether the garment may be machine washed or hand washed, and at what maximum temperature.
- A **triangle** gives bleaching instructions.
- A **square** explains drying, including tumble drying or line drying.
- An **iron** indicates whether the fabric can be ironed and at what heat setting.
- A **circle** refers to professional care, such as [dry cleaning](/en/glossar/chemische-reinigung/).

A symbol crossed out with an X means that treatment is not suitable for the garment. Dots inside the iron or tumble-dry symbol indicate the permitted heat level. One dot means low heat, while more dots allow more heat. Bars below washing or drying symbols signal a gentler process.

## Why Laundry Symbols Matter

Care instructions affect much more than laundry day. Incorrect washing or drying can lead to shrinkage, fading, loss of elasticity, pilling, or changes in texture. This is especially relevant for delicate fabrics, knitwear, structured tailoring, coated surfaces, and garments with embellishments.

Knowing how to read care labels also makes shopping more deliberate. A beautiful piece that needs professional cleaning after every wear may be perfect for an occasional event, but less practical for a busy everyday wardrobe. Care requirements are therefore part of a garment's real [quality](/en/glossar/qualitaet/), cost, and long-term usefulness.

## The Main Care Symbol Groups

### Washing Symbols

The wash tub shows whether a garment may be washed in water.

A number inside the tub indicates the maximum washing temperature. A hand inside the tub means hand wash only. One or two bars underneath indicate a gentle or very gentle cycle. If the tub is crossed out, do not wash the item in water.

When in doubt, choose a cooler, gentler cycle and avoid overfilling the machine. This can be particularly useful for garments prone to [shrinkage](/en/glossar/einlaufen/) or changes in shape.

### Bleaching Symbols

The triangle refers to bleach.

An empty triangle permits bleaching. A crossed-out triangle means do not bleach. If a garment has strong color, a delicate fiber, or a special finish, bleaching can alter its appearance even when the item otherwise seems robust.

### Drying Symbols

The square is the drying symbol. A circle inside the square refers specifically to tumble drying.

Dots inside this symbol indicate heat. A crossed-out square with a circle means do not tumble dry. Other versions can show line drying, drip drying, or flat drying.

Drying is important for maintaining the [fit](/en/glossar/passform/) of knitwear and delicate garments. Heavy wet knits can stretch when hung, while excessive dryer heat can shrink fibers or weaken elastane.

### Ironing Symbols

The iron symbol shows whether ironing is permitted.

Dots indicate the maximum heat level. A crossed-out iron means do not iron. Steam may also be restricted for certain materials, coatings, prints, and synthetic fibers. In these cases, [steaming](/en/glossar/dampfen/) at an appropriate distance can sometimes be a gentler alternative, provided the care label allows it.

### Professional Cleaning Symbols

The circle indicates professional textile care.

A plain circle generally means professional cleaning is possible, while letters and lines can provide more specific instructions for the cleaning professional. A crossed-out circle means do not dry clean.

Professional care can be appropriate for tailored pieces, structured coats, delicate eveningwear, and fabrics that may be damaged by water or agitation.

## What to Check Before You Buy

Laundry symbols are useful buying criteria, especially when you want a wardrobe that works in real life.

- Check whether the care routine suits how often you expect to wear the piece.
- Consider whether the garment requires hand washing, flat drying, or professional cleaning.
- Look at the fiber composition alongside the care instructions. A [fiber blend](/en/glossar/faser-mix/) may need different treatment than a pure natural fiber.
- Be cautious with pieces that require high maintenance if you are looking for reliable everyday clothing.
- Keep the label visible until you know the garment's care needs.

## Common Misunderstandings

**Misunderstanding:** A higher washing temperature cleans every garment better.  
**Better:** Higher heat can damage fibers, set stains, fade color, or cause shrinkage. Follow the maximum temperature shown on the label.

**Misunderstanding:** “Do not tumble dry” means the item cannot be dried at home.  
**Better:** It usually means the garment should air dry, often on a hanger, a line, or laid flat depending on the symbol.

**Misunderstanding:** Care labels only matter for expensive clothing.  
**Better:** Every garment can lose shape, color, or comfort through unsuitable care. Correct laundering protects both budget finds and investment pieces.

**Misunderstanding:** Dry cleaning is always the safest option.  
**Better:** Professional cleaning is only appropriate when the care label permits it. Some materials and finishes should not be dry cleaned.

## Practical Care Routine

1. Read the care label before the first wash, not after a problem occurs.
2. Separate garments by color, fabric sensitivity, and recommended wash temperature.
3. Turn printed, dark, delicate, or embellished pieces inside out where appropriate.
4. Use a gentle cycle and low spin for delicate fabrics.
5. Check drying instructions before placing anything in the tumble dryer.
6. Store care information for special pieces that you do not wash often.

Laundry symbols turn garment care into an informed wardrobe decision. When you understand them, clothes are more likely to keep their fit, color, texture, and value over time.
