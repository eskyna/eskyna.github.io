## Definition

A **French cut** is a very high, V-shaped leg opening used in underwear and swimwear. The line rises sharply from the crotch toward the hip bones, leaving more of the upper thigh and hip area visible than a standard brief or bikini bottom.

This cut creates an open, elongated lower-body line. It is often chosen for its leg-lengthening visual effect, but it can also simply be a preferred fit for someone who likes less fabric across the hips.

## What Does a French Cut Look Like?

The defining feature is the angle of the leg opening. Instead of curving gently around the top of the thigh, the fabric rises high at the sides toward the waist or upper hip.

A French cut may appear on:

- High-cut briefs and lingerie bottoms
- Bikini bottoms
- One-piece swimsuits
- Bodysuits
- Retro-inspired underwear styles

The cut is related to a [High-Leg Bikini Cut](/en/glossar/high-leg-beinausschnitt/), although French cut is also widely used for everyday underwear and not only swimwear.

## Effect on Proportion and Silhouette

A high French-cut leg opening creates a strong upward diagonal at each hip. This can make the legs appear visually longer because the eye follows the raised line upward rather than stopping at a lower, more horizontal opening.

The effect depends on several details:

- **Height of the cut:** A higher opening reveals more of the hip and upper thigh.
- **Angle of the line:** A steep V shape looks more dramatic than a softly curved high leg.
- **Waist position:** A [high-waist](/en/glossar/high-waist/) French-cut bottom creates a different effect from a low-rise version.
- **Color and pattern:** A solid color often keeps the line clear, while prints can soften or emphasize the cut depending on their scale and placement.
- **Fabric:** Stretch recovery helps the leg opening sit smoothly instead of shifting, digging in, or rolling.

Because it reveals more skin around the hips, this style can feel more minimal than a standard brief. It does not need to be revealing overall, however. A high-waisted French-cut design can still offer substantial front and back coverage.

## How to Choose a French Cut

Choose a French cut based on comfort, desired coverage, and the occasion rather than trying to follow a fixed body-shape rule.

For underwear, look for soft elastic that lies flat without pressing into the skin. The fabric should return to shape after movement, especially in close-fitting briefs or bodysuits. A smooth French-cut brief can also work well under skirts, dresses, and trousers when the fabric is not bulky.

For swimwear, consider both the leg opening and the waist height. A [High-Waist Bikini](/en/glossar/high-waist-bikini/) with a French-cut leg can provide more coverage through the midsection while keeping the hip line open. A lower-rise version usually feels more minimal and exposes more of the hip area.

If you are comparing swimwear styles, a [Bikini Proportion Check](/en/glossar/bikini-proportionen-check/) can help you assess coverage, strap placement, pattern scale, and how the cut feels when standing, walking, and sitting.

## How to Wear a French Cut

A French cut is primarily a fit choice, but it can influence how clothing layers over it.

- Choose a seamless or smooth version under fitted skirts, dresses, and lightweight trousers.
- Select a wider gusset and stable leg elastic if you want a secure everyday fit.
- Wear high-waisted French-cut swimwear with a shirt, sarong, or lightweight cover-up for adjustable coverage.
- Pair a French-cut bodysuit with jeans, tailored trousers, or a skirt when you want a clean, uninterrupted tucked-in line.
- Check that the leg opening remains comfortable when you sit, bend, and walk.

The best version is one that stays in place without constant adjustment. A very high cut should feel intentional, not restrictive.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: A French cut is always a thong.**  
  **Better:** A French cut describes the high leg opening, not the amount of back coverage. It may have full, moderate, or minimal coverage at the back.

- **Misunderstanding: French cut only means vintage underwear.**  
  **Better:** The cut appears in many styles, from retro-inspired briefs to modern swimwear and streamlined bodysuits.

- **Misunderstanding: A high leg cut is only meant to make legs look longer.**  
  **Better:** Visual elongation can be one effect, but many people choose the cut simply because it offers a less restrictive, more open feel around the upper thigh.

- **Misunderstanding: The highest cut is always the most flattering.**  
  **Better:** The most successful height is the one that suits your comfort level, coverage preference, and how securely the garment fits in motion.

## Practical Check Before Buying

- Stand, sit, walk, and bend to make sure the leg opening does not pull or shift.
- Check whether the elastic lies flat without pinching at the hip crease.
- Look at the front and back coverage separately, as a high leg does not automatically mean minimal coverage.
- Choose fabric with enough stretch and recovery for a close, stable fit.
- For swimwear, assess the cut in natural light and test whether it stays comfortable when moving.

## Key Line

A French cut is a high, hip-rising leg opening in underwear or swimwear that creates a more open hip line and a visually longer-looking leg shape.
