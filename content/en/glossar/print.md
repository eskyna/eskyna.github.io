## Definition

**Print** is a design applied to the surface of fabric or clothing. In fashion, prints include motifs such as flowers, stripes, dots, graphics, animal designs, checks, lettering, or abstract artwork.

A print changes how a garment reads from a distance. It can create movement, add softness or energy, direct the eye, and become the focal point of an outfit. Print is closely related to [pattern](/en/glossar/muster/), but the terms are not identical: a pattern may also be woven, knitted, or created through texture rather than printed onto fabric.

## Print explained simply

A plain navy blouse and a navy blouse with a cream floral print may have the same cut and fabric, yet they create very different impressions. The plain blouse feels quieter and more flexible. The printed blouse is more expressive and usually becomes a stronger visual element in the outfit.

The effect depends on more than the motif itself. Important factors include:

- **Scale:** Small motifs often appear finer or more detailed, while large motifs make a stronger statement.
- **Contrast:** High contrast prints are more visible and energetic. Low contrast or tonal prints appear calmer.
- **Color:** The colors within a print influence its harmony with your complexion, wardrobe, and accessories.
- **Placement:** A [placed print](/en/glossar/platzierter-print/) can deliberately draw attention to one area, while an [allover print](/en/glossar/allover-print/) covers the full garment.
- **Fabric and drape:** The same print can look crisp on cotton, fluid on viscose, or more luxurious on satin.

## Why print matters in outfits

Print is one of the clearest tools for shaping an outfit’s mood. A subtle stripe can bring structure to an everyday look. A floral print can feel romantic, fresh, or dramatic depending on color and scale. A graphic print can add a modern or urban note.

Because prints are visually active, they influence [proportion](/en/glossar/proportion/), focus, and balance. A large, high-contrast motif naturally attracts attention. Use this intentionally if you want to highlight a part of the outfit. If you prefer a calmer overall effect, choose smaller motifs, lower contrast, or colors that sit close together.

A printed piece can also make a wardrobe more versatile when its colors connect with several solid basics. For example, a print containing navy, cream, and burgundy can be combined with each of those colors separately.

## Types of print in fashion

Common fashion prints include:

- **Floral print:** Flowers, leaves, and botanical motifs. See [Floral Print](/en/glossar/blumenmuster/).
- **Animal print:** Motifs inspired by animal markings, such as leopard or zebra.
- **Geometric print:** Repeated shapes, lines, grids, or abstract forms.
- **Graphic print:** Illustration, lettering, logos, photographs, or artwork.
- **Striped and dotted print:** Regular lines or dots that create rhythm and direction.
- **Abstract print:** Non-figurative motifs with a painterly, expressive, or modern effect.

A [check pattern](/en/glossar/karomuster/) may be printed, but it can also be woven into the fabric. Looking closely at the material helps clarify whether you are seeing a print or a construction-based pattern.

## How to style printed clothing

Start with one printed piece and let its colors guide the rest of the outfit. This is often the easiest way to create a coherent look.

- Pick one color from the print for trousers, a skirt, shoes, or a bag.
- Combine a bold print with solid-colored pieces to give the eye a resting point.
- Mix prints only when they share a color, a similar visual mood, or a clearly different scale.
- Use accessories with restraint when the print is already the focal point.
- Consider the occasion. Small-scale or tonal prints often feel more understated, while larger and more contrasting designs are more expressive.

A print does not have to be loud to be interesting. Tonal prints, where motif and background are close in color, can add depth while maintaining a quiet, polished look.

## Print, quality, and care

A print alone does not define quality. Assess the garment as a whole: the fabric, cut, seams, fit, and finishing matter as much as the motif.

When shopping, look for clear edges, even color coverage, and a print that sits appropriately on the garment. On garments with seams, pockets, or a button placket, the motif may not align perfectly. Whether that matters depends on the design and price level, but it is worth checking.

Care also affects how a print lasts. Follow the [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/), turn printed garments inside out when appropriate, and avoid unnecessarily harsh washing or heat. This can help preserve color and surface appearance. [Color fastness](/en/glossar/farbechtheit/) is especially relevant when bright or dark printed fabrics are washed.

## Common misconceptions

**Misconception: Every print is a pattern.**  
**Better:** Print is one type of pattern. A pattern can also be woven, knitted, embroidered, pleated, or otherwise created through the material itself.

**Misconception: Small prints are always subtle.**  
**Better:** A small motif can still look visually strong when the color contrast is high or the repeat is dense.

**Misconception: Prints cannot be versatile.**  
**Better:** A well-chosen print can connect several wardrobe colors and create many combinations with solid basics.

**Misconception: Print placement does not matter.**  
**Better:** Motif size and placement can direct attention and noticeably change the perceived balance of a garment.

## Practical check before buying a printed garment

- View the print from close up and from a few steps away.
- Check whether the colors work with pieces you already wear.
- Notice whether the scale feels balanced with your preferred silhouette and proportions.
- Look at the print around seams, pockets, zippers, and button plackets.
- Consider whether the motif suits the occasions where you would realistically wear it.
- Review the care instructions before deciding.

## ESKYNA note

A print works best when it supports the message of the outfit. Choose it for its color, scale, and mood, not only because the motif catches your eye in the moment.
