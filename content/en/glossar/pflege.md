## Definition

**Care** refers to the cleaning, drying, ironing, storage, and maintenance of clothing. In wardrobe planning, good garment care helps preserve a piece's fit, color, texture, and overall appearance so it can be worn confidently over time.

Care is not limited to laundry. It also includes reading the [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/), treating stains promptly, storing garments appropriately, and choosing professional cleaning when a material or construction requires it.

## Care explained simply

Every garment has practical needs. A cotton T-shirt may tolerate regular machine washing, while a wool knit, silk blouse, or structured jacket can require a gentler approach. The right method depends on the fabric, dye, lining, embellishments, and garment construction.

Care matters because clothing changes through wear and washing. Heat, friction, moisture, detergent, and incorrect storage can affect a garment's surface and shape. Pilling, fading, shrinkage, stretched knitwear, or damaged finishes often result from care that does not suit the item.

The care label gives the clearest starting point. Its [laundry symbols](/en/glossar/waschsymbole/) indicate whether an item can be machine washed, should be [hand washed](/en/glossar/handwaesche/), needs [dry cleaning](/en/glossar/chemische-reinigung/), or can be ironed safely.

## Why clothing care matters

Thoughtful care is part of building a useful, long-lasting wardrobe. A piece that stays in good condition is easier to combine, feels more reliable to wear, and can deliver better value across repeated use.

It also supports a more considered approach to shopping. Before buying, it is worth asking whether you are willing and able to care for the item properly. A beautiful fabric that does not suit your daily routine may remain unworn, regardless of how good it looked in the fitting room.

Care therefore connects directly to [longevity](/en/glossar/langlebigkeit/), [quality](/en/glossar/qualitaet/), and a [sustainable wardrobe](/en/glossar/nachhaltige-garderobe/).

## Practical clothing care checklist

- Read the care label before the first wash, not after a problem appears.
- Sort laundry by care requirements, color, and fabric weight where appropriate.
- Use the gentlest effective washing method for the garment.
- Avoid unnecessary heat when washing, drying, or ironing.
- Check delicate materials, trims, and prints before using a dryer or iron.
- Fold knits when suitable to help prevent stretching, and hang structured garments on supportive hangers.
- Address stains quickly, but test unfamiliar stain treatments on an inconspicuous area first.
- Use [steaming](/en/glossar/dampfen/) or [ironing](/en/glossar/buegeln/) according to the fabric's needs to maintain a polished appearance.

## Common misconceptions

**Misconception:** Care only means washing clothes.  
**Better:** Care includes the full life of a garment, from cleaning and drying to pressing, storing, repairing, and handling it with attention.

**Misconception:** Delicate-looking clothing always needs dry cleaning.  
**Better:** The correct method depends on the specific care label. Some delicate items can be hand washed, while some structured or lined garments require professional care.

**Misconception:** Higher temperatures clean every garment better.  
**Better:** Excess heat can shrink fibers, dull colors, damage elastic components, or affect finishes. Use the recommended temperature and method.
