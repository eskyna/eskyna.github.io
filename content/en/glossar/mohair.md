## Definition

**Mohair** is a natural animal fiber made from the hair of the Angora goat. It is valued in fashion for its natural sheen, springy structure, light warmth, and ability to create either a soft halo or a crisp, polished surface.

Mohair appears in fluffy sweaters, cardigans, scarves, coats, and tailored fabrics. It is often blended with other fibers, such as [wool](/en/glossar/wolle/), [silk](/en/glossar/seide/), or synthetic fibers, to balance softness, durability, warmth, and care requirements.

## What does mohair feel like?

Mohair can feel very different from one garment to another. A brushed mohair sweater often looks airy and cloud-like, with fine fibers standing away from the knit. This creates visual softness and depth, but may feel prickly to sensitive skin.

In woven fabrics, especially for tailoring, mohair creates a cleaner and firmer effect. The fabric can appear subtly glossy, hold a sharp line, and recover well after movement. That is why mohair blends are often used where a polished surface and a lively, structured drape are desired.

The [handfeel](/en/glossar/griff/) matters more than the fiber name alone. A fine, carefully made mohair knit can feel very different from a coarse or heavily brushed one.

## Mohair in knitwear and tailoring

Mohair is particularly recognizable in the following forms:

- **Fluffy knitwear:** Brushed yarns create a soft, hazy surface that makes a simple sweater look more tactile and expressive.
- **Fine knitwear:** A smaller amount of mohair can add lightness and a refined sheen without creating a strong pile.
- **Coats and outerwear:** Mohair may contribute warmth, texture, and a slightly elevated surface.
- **Suiting:** Mohair blends can give suiting fabrics bounce, crease resistance, and a crisp visual finish.

Because mohair has a strong visual presence, it can make even a minimal outfit feel more intentional. A mohair sweater with straight jeans, tailored trousers, or a [satin skirt](/en/glossar/satinrock/) brings texture into an otherwise simple look.

## Mohair vs. Angora, wool, and cashmere

Mohair is often confused with Angora, but they are different fibers:

- **Mohair** comes from the Angora goat.
- **Angora** usually refers to hair from the Angora rabbit.
- **Wool** is most commonly associated with sheep and ranges greatly in softness, weight, and texture.
- **Cashmere** comes from cashmere goats and is generally known for a softer, finer feel.
- **Alpaca** is valued for its soft handle, warmth, and fluid drape.

A garment labeled as a [fiber blend](/en/glossar/faser-mix/) may contain only a small percentage of mohair. Even then, it can noticeably change the fabric's sheen, texture, warmth, and shape.

## What to pay attention to when buying mohair

Mohair can be a beautiful investment, but it is worth assessing the individual garment rather than buying by fiber content alone.

- **Touch it first:** Hold the fabric against the neck or inner arm if you are sensitive to texture.
- **Check the blend:** Look at the label to see how much mohair is included and which fibers accompany it.
- **Look at fiber fly:** Fluffy mohair can shed loose fibers, which may be noticeable on darker garments worn underneath.
- **Assess the knit or weave:** A loose, airy knit looks softer and more casual. A dense weave appears cleaner and more structured.
- **Consider care:** Follow the [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/) closely. Brushing, washing, and storage can change the surface over time.

## Common misconceptions

- **Misconception: Mohair comes from the Angora rabbit.**  
  **Better:** Mohair comes from the Angora goat. Angora and mohair are separate textile fibers.

- **Misconception: Every mohair garment is very soft.**  
  **Better:** Mohair can be soft, but it can also feel textured or prickly. Fiber quality, finishing, and the overall blend determine comfort.

- **Misconception: Mohair is only for winter.**  
  **Better:** Mohair is warm, but its lightness can also make it useful in fine, airy layers for transitional weather.

## Practical check

Before choosing a mohair garment, ask:

- Does the texture feel comfortable directly on my skin?
- Is the fluffy surface right for my desired level of visual softness?
- Does the color show loose fibers easily?
- Can I realistically follow the required [care](/en/glossar/pflege/) routine?
- Does the garment work with the rest of my wardrobe, or is its texture too specific for frequent wear?
