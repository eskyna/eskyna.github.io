## Definition

**Shrinkage** is the reduction in a garment's length, width, or overall dimensions after washing, drying, steaming, or other finishing processes. In clothing, shrinkage matters because even a small change can affect [fit](/en/glossar/passform/), comfort, proportions, and whether a piece still works as intended.

It is most commonly noticed after laundering. A sweater may become shorter, trousers may lose length, or a cotton shirt may feel tighter across the body after its first wash.

## What Causes Clothing to Shrink?

Fabric shrinkage is not caused by one factor alone. It can result from the interaction of fiber, fabric construction, finishing, and care.

Common influences include:

- **Moisture:** Water can allow fibers and yarns to relax or shift.
- **Heat:** Hot washing and tumble drying can intensify shrinkage.
- **Agitation:** Mechanical movement in a washing machine can affect delicate fibers and knitted structures.
- **Fiber type:** Natural fibers such as [cotton](/en/glossar/baumwolle/) and [wool](/en/glossar/wolle/) may react differently from synthetic fibers.
- **Fabric construction:** Woven fabrics and knitwear do not behave in the same way.
- **Finishing:** Some fabrics are treated during production to limit later shrinkage, but the outcome still depends on care.

Shrinkage may happen evenly across a garment, but it can also affect one direction more strongly. For example, a top may become noticeably shorter while its width changes only slightly.

## Why Shrinkage Matters for Fit

Shrinkage can turn a garment that initially fits well into one that feels restrictive, too short, or visually unbalanced. This is especially relevant for fitted shirts, tailored trousers, knitwear, dresses, and pieces with precise sleeve or hem lengths.

A reduced length can alter the relationship between top and bottom, while a tighter body can create pulling or discomfort in movement. In trousers, shrinkage may change the intended [hem length](/en/glossar/saumlange/) and the way the fabric meets the shoe.

For wardrobe planning, this means a new garment should not be judged only by its fit in the fitting room. Consider how it is likely to perform after its first care cycle and whether the care routine is realistic for your everyday life.

## Shrinkage and Fabric Type

### Cotton

Cotton is comfortable, breathable, and widely used, but it can shrink when washed or dried under unsuitable conditions. Read the [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/) before washing, especially with denim, jersey, and untreated cotton items.

### Wool

Wool requires particular care. Heat, moisture, and agitation can cause wool fibers to felt together, leading to substantial and often irreversible shrinkage. Follow the garment's instructions closely and use a suitable wool program or hand wash method when recommended.

### Knitwear

Knitwear can change shape as well as size. A knitted garment may contract, stretch, or distort depending on the fiber blend, stitch structure, washing method, and drying position. Drying knitwear flat is often important for maintaining its intended shape.

### Synthetic Fibers and Blends

Synthetic fibers may be more dimensionally stable in some cases, but this is not a universal rule. In a fiber blend, each component can respond differently to heat and moisture. Always assess the full material composition and care instructions rather than relying on one fiber alone.

## How to Reduce Shrinkage When Washing Clothes

Care cannot eliminate every possible change, but it can reduce avoidable shrinkage and help clothing retain its fit for longer.

- Check the [laundry symbols](/en/glossar/waschsymbole/) and follow the stated washing temperature.
- Avoid hotter water than the care label permits.
- Use a gentle cycle when the garment calls for it.
- Treat wool and delicate knitwear with special care.
- Do not tumble dry unless the label explicitly allows it.
- Reshape garments carefully after washing when appropriate.
- Dry knitwear flat when recommended to protect both size and form.

The best care method is the one that protects the garment while fitting your actual routine. A beautiful piece that needs care you cannot realistically provide may have limited long term usefulness.

## Shrinkage as a Buying Consideration

Before purchasing a garment, consider not only its current appearance but also its likely performance after repeated wear and care. Shrinkage is particularly relevant when buying close fitting items, full length trousers, cropped jackets, wool knitwear, or pieces where a few centimetres make a visible difference.

Ask yourself:

- Does the garment have enough ease for comfortable movement?
- Is the length likely to remain suitable after washing?
- Can I follow the required care instructions consistently?
- Is the material composition clear and appropriate for my routine?
- Will the garment still work with my existing wardrobe if it changes slightly?

This perspective supports better [buying criteria](/en/glossar/kaufkriterien/) and can help prevent a promising piece from becoming a bad purchase after one laundry cycle.

## Common Misunderstandings About Shrinkage

- **Misunderstanding: All shrinkage means poor quality.**  
  **Better:** Shrinkage can occur in well made clothing, particularly with natural fibers. Clear care instructions, appropriate finishing, and sensible material choices are more useful indicators than expecting zero change in every garment.

- **Misunderstanding: A larger size always solves the problem.**  
  **Better:** Sizing up may create poor proportions before washing and still does not guarantee the right fit afterward. Consider the cut, fabric, intended ease, and care requirements together.

- **Misunderstanding: Tumble drying is harmless if the garment seems sturdy.**  
  **Better:** Heat can affect fibers, fabric structure, and size. The care label, not the garment's apparent weight or thickness, should guide the decision.

## Practical Check Before Purchase

- Read the fiber composition and the [care label](/en/glossar/pflegeetikett/).
- Check whether the garment already feels close fitting in areas that need movement.
- Pay attention to sleeve, body, and trouser length.
- Consider whether you would be comfortable if the garment became slightly smaller.
- Choose care requirements that genuinely suit your lifestyle.
- If possible, check reviews or ask the retailer whether the fabric is known to shrink.

Shrinkage is a material behavior that can change a garment's fit after care. Understanding it helps you choose clothing that remains comfortable, proportionate, and wearable beyond the first try on.
