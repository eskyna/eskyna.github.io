## Definition

**Burgundy** is a deep, dark red color with muted purple, brown, or blue influences. Also called **bordeaux** or wine red in many fashion contexts, it is inspired by the visual character of red wine and creates a rich, composed effect in clothing and accessories.

Burgundy is darker and less vivid than a clear [red](/en/glossar/rot/). Its depth makes it feel more restrained than bright red while still bringing warmth, color, and presence to an outfit.

## What color is burgundy?

Burgundy sits between red, purple, and brown. There is no single universal burgundy shade: one version may appear warmer and more brown-red, while another looks cooler, deeper, and closer to plum or [aubergine](/en/glossar/aubergine/).

In practice, burgundy is usually:

- Deep rather than light
- Muted rather than bright
- Red-based, often with a purple or brown cast
- Elegant, grounded, and visually rich

Because it has lower brightness and saturation than a vivid primary red, burgundy can often function almost like a dark neutral. It is a useful alternative to black, navy, or dark brown when you want color without a loud effect.

## How burgundy affects an outfit

Burgundy gives an outfit depth, warmth, and a polished sense of weight. It can make simple pieces such as a sweater, blazer, loafer, leather bag, or nail color feel more intentional.

The final effect depends strongly on the material. Burgundy in matte wool, suede, or corduroy often looks soft and grounded. In velvet, satin, leather, or glossy lipstick, the same color can appear more dramatic, sensual, or festive.

Its visual impact also changes through [color contrast](/en/glossar/kontrast/). Paired with light cream, burgundy becomes more pronounced. Combined with dark navy or charcoal, it creates a quieter and more sophisticated look.

## How to combine burgundy

Burgundy is highly versatile because it works with both warm and cool color directions. The best combination depends on whether your burgundy leans brown-red, purple-red, or blue-red.

### Classic burgundy color combinations

- **Burgundy and cream:** Soft, elegant, and warm without the starkness of pure white.
- **Burgundy and camel:** Rich, classic, and especially harmonious with warmer burgundy shades.
- **Burgundy and navy:** Deep, refined, and low contrast. Add a lighter top or accessory if the outfit needs more clarity.
- **Burgundy and gray:** Modern and composed. Light gray creates more contrast, while charcoal keeps the look subtle.
- **Burgundy and denim:** An easy everyday pairing that balances the richness of burgundy with casual texture.
- **Burgundy and chocolate brown:** A deep tonal combination with a warm, luxurious feel.
- **Burgundy and soft pink:** A gentle red-family harmony that can look romantic or modern depending on the materials.
- **Burgundy and muted green:** Olive, moss, and sage can create an earthy, autumnal color palette.

For a more expressive approach, burgundy can also work with dusty blue, lilac, or a carefully chosen [berry tone](/en/glossar/beerentoene/). Keep one color dominant so the outfit remains coherent.

## Is burgundy warm or cool?

Burgundy does not belong to only one temperature category. It can be warm, cool, or close to neutral.

A warm burgundy often contains more brown, rust, or brick-red influence. A cool burgundy usually appears more blue-red, purple-red, or plum-like. This is why two garments labelled burgundy may look very different beside the face.

Use [color temperature](/en/glossar/farbtemperatur/) and your personal [undertone](/en/glossar/unterton/) as practical guides, not strict rules. Hold the garment near your face in daylight and notice whether your skin looks clearer and more even, or tired and shadowed.

## Who does burgundy suit?

Burgundy can suit many people, but the most flattering version varies.

- People who suit warm, muted colors may prefer brown-red, oxblood, or rust-leaning burgundy.
- People who suit cool, deep colors may prefer blue-red, plum, or violet-leaning burgundy.
- People with lower overall contrast often find softened, smoky burgundy easier than a very dark, sharply saturated wine red.
- People with high contrast may wear deep burgundy particularly well alongside black, crisp cream, or strong dark neutrals.

If you are unsure, start with burgundy away from the face. A bag, belt, shoe, or skirt lets you test the color without making it the main focus of your complexion.

## Burgundy in a practical wardrobe

Burgundy is particularly useful when you want a wardrobe to feel more individual than black, gray, and navy alone, while remaining easy to combine. It works across casual, business, and evening contexts.

Useful burgundy pieces include:

- A fine knit sweater or cardigan
- Tailored trousers
- A blazer or coat
- Leather ankle boots, loafers, or pumps
- A handbag or belt
- A scarf, lipstick, or nail color as a controlled accent

For a compact wardrobe, choose one burgundy item that connects with at least three existing colors. For example, burgundy boots work well when you regularly wear denim, cream, camel, navy, or gray.

## Common misconceptions

**Misconception: Burgundy is simply dark red.**  
**Better:** Burgundy is red-based, but it usually has a muted wine-like quality created by purple, brown, or blue influences. A bright, clear red is not usually described as burgundy.

**Misconception: Burgundy is only for autumn and winter.**  
**Better:** Its depth naturally suits cooler seasons, but burgundy also works year-round in lighter fabrics, accessories, floral prints, and combinations with cream, blush, or light blue.

**Misconception: Burgundy only suits formal outfits.**  
**Better:** Material and styling determine formality. Burgundy denim, sneakers, knitwear, and casual bags can feel relaxed, while burgundy velvet or satin can read as eveningwear.

## Quick mirror check

When trying burgundy near your face, look for three things:

- Does your complexion appear clear and even?
- Does the shade support your natural contrast rather than overpower it?
- Does it connect with the other colors, textures, and level of formality in your outfit?

If the color feels too dark or heavy, add cream, soft gray, light denim, or a lighter accessory near the face. If it looks too muted, introduce a clearer accent through jewelry, lipstick, or a lighter top.
