## Definition

A **keyhole neckline** is a neckline detail with a small opening below the neck, most often at the center front or center back of a top, blouse, or dress. The opening can be circular, oval, teardrop-shaped, or narrow and vertical, resembling the outline of an old-fashioned keyhole.

Unlike a deep [V Neckline](/en/glossar/v-ausschnitt/) or a [Plunge Neckline](/en/glossar/plunge-neckline/), a keyhole neckline does not necessarily expose a large area of skin. Its effect comes from a controlled interruption in an otherwise closed or high neckline.

## How a Keyhole Neckline Works

A keyhole opening can change the mood of a garment with very little fabric removed. A small opening at the throat may feel delicate and polished, while a larger opening lower on the chest creates a more noticeable focal point.

Keyhole necklines are often finished with a button, hook, tie, or loop at the top. On back keyhole styles, this closure can be functional, allowing the garment to open wide enough to pull on comfortably. On front keyhole designs, the fastening may also be decorative.

The visual effect depends on several details:

- **Placement:** A high opening near the collarbone is usually more discreet than one placed lower on the chest.
- **Size and shape:** A narrow slit reads differently from a wide teardrop opening.
- **Fabric:** Stable woven fabrics hold a clean shape, while soft jersey or draped fabrics can make the opening appear more fluid.
- **Fit:** Tension across the bust, shoulders, or neckline can pull the opening wider than intended.
- **Surrounding neckline:** A keyhole can sit within a crew neck, mock neck, halterneck, or collar design.

## Keyhole Neckline Styling and Proportion

A keyhole neckline draws the eye toward the center of the upper body. It can therefore act as a subtle [Focal Point](/en/glossar/fokuspunkt/) without needing jewelry, print, or strong color contrast.

For a calm, balanced outfit, let the neckline remain the main detail and choose simple accessories. Small earrings, a fine necklace that sits above or below the opening, or no necklace at all often works well. A necklace that falls directly across the opening can look visually busy, although this is ultimately a personal styling choice.

The opening also influences [Neckline Effect](/en/glossar/ausschnittwirkung/). A vertical keyhole can introduce a gentle lengthwise line, while a rounded opening creates a softer, more decorative impression. There is no universally flattering neckline. What matters is whether the line, visibility level, and overall [Proportion](/en/glossar/proportion/) feel right for you and the occasion.

## When Is a Keyhole Neckline Appropriate?

A keyhole neckline can work across many settings because its level of visibility varies widely.

- **Everyday outfits:** Small front or back keyholes add interest to T shirts, knitwear, blouses, and simple dresses without making the look overly dressy.
- **Workwear:** A high, modest keyhole can suit some workplaces, especially when the garment is opaque and fits securely. Consider your specific office culture and [Dress Code](/en/glossar/dresscode/).
- **Eveningwear:** Larger keyholes, glossy fabrics, cutouts, and open backs create a more expressive effect for dinner, parties, or [Evening Wear](/en/glossar/abendgarderobe/).
- **Warm weather:** Keyhole details can provide visual lightness without the broader exposure of a strapless or deeply cut top.

A keyhole neckline is not automatically revealing, formal, or casual. Scale, construction, material, and styling determine its message.

## Fit, Comfort, and Underwear Considerations

Before buying a keyhole neckline, move in it. Raise your arms, sit down, turn your shoulders, and look at the garment from more than one angle. The opening should retain its intended shape rather than pulling, gaping, twisting, or requiring repeated adjustment.

Pay close attention to the bust and shoulder area. If the garment is too tight, the keyhole may stretch open or sit too low. If it is too loose, the neckline may shift and expose more than expected. Good [Fit](/en/glossar/passform/) is especially important when the opening is at the front.

Underwear depends on the design:

- A small high keyhole usually works with a standard, well-fitting bra.
- A lower or wider opening may reveal the bra bridge or cup edge.
- Back keyholes can conflict with bra straps or closures.
- Dresses with very open backs or complex cutouts may require a strapless, backless, or integrated support option.

Try the exact underwear you expect to wear with the garment. This is more reliable than judging the neckline on a hanger or in a changing room without proper support.

## Keyhole Neckline vs Similar Details

### Keyhole Neckline vs Cutout

A [Cut-out](/en/glossar/cut-out/) is a broader term for an intentional opening in a garment. A keyhole is a particular type of cutout, usually small and positioned near a neckline or upper back.

### Keyhole Neckline vs Plunge Neckline

A [Plunge Neckline](/en/glossar/plunge-neckline/) is defined by a neckline that extends deeply downward, often in a V shape. A keyhole neckline keeps fabric around the opening, so it can show a smaller and more contained area of skin.

### Keyhole Neckline vs Open Back

An [Open Back Neckline](/en/glossar/rueckenausschnitt/) exposes a larger section of the back. A back keyhole is typically a smaller, enclosed opening that may also function as a closure detail.

## Practical Check Before Purchase

- Check whether the opening stays in place when you move normally.
- Look for pulling or horizontal tension around the bust, shoulders, and neck.
- Assess whether the visible skin level suits your real settings, not only a mirror pose.
- Test the garment with the bra, camisole, or support solution you would actually wear.
- Consider whether the neckline works with your existing jackets, cardigans, and accessories.
- Review fabric opacity, especially with light colors or [Transparency](/en/glossar/transparenz/) in daylight.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **A keyhole neckline is always revealing.**  
  Not necessarily. A small opening near the collarbone can be more covered than many standard necklines.

- **Every keyhole is a front neckline detail.**  
  No. Keyholes are also common at the back of blouses and dresses, where they often incorporate a button closure.

- **The opening should sit the same on every body.**  
  The appearance changes with garment fit, bust shape, posture, and movement. The intended design matters more than a fixed rule.

## ESKYNA Takeaway

A keyhole neckline is a small opening that adds focus, lightness, or sensuality to a garment without defining the entire look. Choose the size, placement, and construction that feel secure, comfortable, and appropriate for how you actually dress.
