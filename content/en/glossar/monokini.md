## Definition

A **monokini** is a one-piece swimsuit with intentional open sections, usually at the sides, waist, back, or abdomen. It creates the impression of a bikini while retaining a connected swimsuit structure.

Modern monokinis range from subtly shaped one-pieces with small waist cut-outs to dramatic designs with deep side openings, asymmetrical straps, or exposed backs. The exact amount of coverage depends on the design.

## What Does a Monokini Look Like?

The defining feature of a monokini is the connection between its upper and lower sections. This connection may be broad and supportive, narrow and decorative, or placed only at the front, sides, or center of the torso.

Many designs use [cut-outs](/en/glossar/cut-out/) to reveal the waist and create a more sculpted, graphic silhouette. Others borrow details from a [bandeau bikini](/en/glossar/bandeau-bikini/), such as a strapless-looking bust line, or use an asymmetrical upper section similar to a [one-shoulder neckline](/en/glossar/one-shoulder-ausschnitt/).

A monokini is swimwear, not simply a [body](/en/glossar/body/). While both are one-piece garments that follow the torso, a monokini is made for beach, pool, or resort settings and should be chosen with water comfort, lining, and secure movement in mind.

## Effect and Styling Impact

A monokini creates a confident, fashion-focused swimwear look. The continuous structure can make the outfit feel more polished than a bikini, while the openings add skin exposure and visual tension.

The placement of the cut-outs matters:

- **Side cut-outs** draw attention to the waist and create a strong hourglass-inspired outline.
- **Front cut-outs** can create a longer central line or a more daring, body-aware effect.
- **Back cut-outs** keep the front more covered while adding interest from behind.
- **Asymmetrical cut-outs** create a modern, directional look and make the swimsuit itself the focal point.

Because its silhouette is already expressive, a monokini often needs little additional styling. A simple cover-up, sandals, sunglasses, and one considered accessory can be enough. For a more elevated resort look, add a lightweight shirt, a sarong, or a flowing skirt after swimming.

## How to Choose a Monokini

Choose a monokini according to the setting, the desired level of coverage, and how actively you plan to move.

For swimming, look for a secure bust area, stable straps, and fabric that stays comfortably in place around every cut-out. Try raising your arms, bending, sitting, and walking in the fitting room. A design can look secure while standing still but shift once you move.

For sunbathing or a poolside event, you may prefer a more dramatic design with deep openings, decorative rings, thin straps, or a statement neckline. Keep in mind that cut-outs also create distinct tan lines.

Color and surface can change the impact significantly. Solid dark colors often make the cut-out shape look graphic and refined, while bright colors, metallic finishes, or prints make the design more visible. If you want a calmer result, choose a monochrome shade and clean construction.

## Monokini vs. Bikini vs. One-Piece Swimsuit

A bikini has two separate pieces: a top and briefs or bottoms. A classic one-piece swimsuit covers more of the torso with a continuous fabric panel. A monokini sits between these two ideas.

It remains a one-piece garment, but its cut-outs interrupt the fabric and expose selected areas of skin. This gives it the openness of a bikini without fully separating the top and bottom.

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: Every monokini has the same amount of coverage.**  
  **Better:** Monokinis vary widely. Some have modest waist openings, while others expose much of the sides, back, or midriff.

- **Misunderstanding: A monokini is only for posing or lounging.**  
  **Better:** Some styles work well for swimming, but fit and construction are essential. Check that the bust, straps, and leg openings remain stable during movement.

- **Misunderstanding: A monokini is the same as a bodysuit.**  
  **Better:** A monokini is designed as swimwear. A bodysuit is generally an everyday or lingerie-inspired garment and may not be suitable for water.

- **Misunderstanding: Cut-outs always make a swimsuit less supportive.**  
  **Better:** Cut-outs can reduce coverage, but support depends on the overall construction, including fabric recovery, lining, straps, and the fit around the bust and torso.

## Practical Check Before Buying

- Check whether the bust area feels secure without digging in or gaping.
- Move your arms, bend, sit, and walk to see whether cut-out edges stay in place.
- Look at the swimsuit from the front, side, and back to assess coverage from every angle.
- Make sure straps, rings, ties, and closures feel comfortable against the skin.
- Consider where you will wear it: active swimming, a hotel pool, a beach holiday, or a resort event.
- Check the care label, especially for swimsuits with hardware, delicate mesh, or textured fabric.

## ESKYNA Key Line

A monokini combines the structure of a one-piece swimsuit with the bold, skin-revealing effect of a bikini through intentional cut-outs.

## Advisory View

A monokini works best when its level of exposure feels aligned with your comfort, the setting, and your intended style message. Choose the cut-out placement for the effect you want, then prioritize secure fit and easy movement over a dramatic design that does not stay comfortable.
