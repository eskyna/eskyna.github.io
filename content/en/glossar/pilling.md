## Definition

**Pilling** is the formation of small balls of tangled fibers on the surface of a textile. These pills develop when loose fibers are pulled out by friction, gather on the fabric surface, and become entangled.

Pilling is especially common on knitwear, jerseys, and garments exposed to repeated rubbing. It can appear under the arms, at side seams, around the waistband, on sleeves, or where a shoulder bag rests against the body.

## What causes pilling?

Pilling is usually caused by a combination of fabric construction, fiber properties, friction, and care. It is not limited to one specific material or price level.

Common causes include:

- Friction from wearing, sitting, layering, seat belts, or bags
- Machine washing, particularly when garments rub against rougher textiles
- Loose or short fibers reaching the surface of the fabric
- Soft, brushed, or loosely constructed materials
- Repeated contact in high-stress areas of a garment

The [fiber blend](/en/glossar/faser-mix/), yarn quality, and fabric density all influence how easily pills form and how noticeable they become.

## Does pilling mean poor quality?

Pilling does not automatically mean that a garment is low quality. Even fine wool, cashmere, and soft knitwear can pill because fibers naturally move and rub during wear.

Still, pilling can be useful information when assessing [material quality](/en/glossar/materialqualitaet/). If a garment pills heavily after only a few wears, consider whether its material, construction, or intended use fits your everyday life. A delicate sweater worn under a rough coat or with a frequently carried bag may show surface wear sooner than the same sweater worn with less friction.

The key question is not simply whether pilling occurs, but how quickly, how extensively, and whether the garment remains attractive and wearable after care.

## How to remove pilling from clothing

A [fabric shaver](/en/glossar/fusselrasierer/) is usually the most practical tool for removing pills from sturdy knitwear, jersey, and similar fabrics.

1. Lay the garment flat on a smooth surface.
2. Make sure the fabric is clean and dry.
3. Pull the area gently taut without stretching it.
4. Move the fabric shaver lightly across the surface.
5. Check the result regularly and avoid staying in one spot for too long.

Be particularly careful with loose knits, lace, sheer fabrics, and delicate surfaces. If unsure, test the tool on an inconspicuous area first.

## How to prevent pilling

You cannot eliminate all pilling, but thoughtful [care](/en/glossar/pflege/) can reduce it.

- Turn garments inside out before washing.
- Choose a gentle wash cycle and avoid excessive spinning.
- Wash soft knits separately from rough fabrics such as towels or heavy denim.
- Avoid overfilling the washing machine, as crowded garments rub more strongly.
- Follow the care label, especially for wool, cashmere, and delicate blends.
- Give garments a rest between wears when possible.
- Notice where bags, belts, and outerwear create repeated friction.

For frequently worn pieces, [abrasion resistance](/en/glossar/abriebfestigkeit/) is an important practical quality factor. A fabric may feel soft and look refined, yet still be less suitable for high-friction daily use.

## Why pilling matters for your wardrobe

Pilling changes a garment's surface and can make an otherwise polished outfit look tired. This is particularly noticeable near the face, on dark solid colors, and on clean, minimal silhouettes where texture is highly visible.

Understanding pilling supports better buying decisions. Before purchasing a sweater, cardigan, or jersey piece, consider how often you will wear it, what you will layer it with, and how much care you are willing to give it. This makes it easier to judge whether a garment is a realistic addition to your wardrobe rather than a potential [bad purchase](/en/glossar/fehlkauf/).

## Common misconceptions

**Misconception:** Only inexpensive clothing pills.  
**Better:** Pilling can occur at every price point. Fiber length, yarn twist, fabric construction, friction, and care all play a role.

**Misconception:** Synthetic fibers are always the cause.  
**Better:** Natural, regenerated, and synthetic fibers can all pill. In some synthetic blends, pills may remain attached to the surface longer because the fibers are strong and do not break away easily.

**Misconception:** Removing pills damages every garment.  
**Better:** A suitable fabric shaver used gently can refresh many garments. Delicate, open, or fragile textiles need extra caution.

## Practical check when shopping

Before buying a knit or soft jersey garment, look for:

- A fabric surface that appears even rather than excessively fuzzy
- Seams and high-friction areas that feel stable
- A fiber blend suited to the garment's intended use
- Care instructions you can realistically follow
- A fabric weight and structure that match how often you plan to wear it

## ESKYNA note

Pilling is normal wear, not a personal failure or an automatic sign of poor quality. Knowing how it develops helps you choose materials more consciously, care for them well, and keep favorite pieces looking polished for longer.
