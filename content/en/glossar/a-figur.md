## Definition

An **A-figure body shape**, also called a **triangle silhouette**, describes proportions in which the hips appear wider than the shoulders. The waist is often visibly defined, creating an outline that broadens gradually from the upper body toward the hips, similar to the letter A.

This is a description of visual proportion, not a size category or a styling limitation. The apparent shape can vary with posture, garment cut, fabric, and the way an outfit is put together.

## What Does an A-Figure Look Like?

With an A-figure, the shoulder line is usually visually narrower than the hips. The waist may stand out clearly between them, although this is not necessary for every person with this proportion pattern.

The effect is easiest to see in fitted or lightly structured clothing. A top that follows the shoulder line and a skirt or pair of trousers with room through the hip can make the A-shaped outline more visible. In contrast, an oversized jacket, a wide neckline, or strong shoulder detail can shift the visual balance.

For a broader explanation of this proportion, see [Triangle Silhouette](/en/glossar/dreieck-silhouette/).

## How Clothing Changes the A-Figure Effect

Clothing does not change the body, but it can change the proportions an outfit highlights. The main factors are shoulder structure, waist placement, hip ease, volume, and visual contrast.

### To create a more balanced shoulder and hip line

If you want the upper body to appear more present, look for details that draw the eye upward:

- Necklines that frame the shoulders, such as a boat neck, square neckline, or wider collar
- Sleeves with gentle volume or shape at the shoulder
- Jackets with a clear shoulder seam and a precise fit through the upper body
- Color, print, texture, jewelry, or scarves placed near the face and shoulders
- A lighter or brighter top with a darker, calmer bottom

These choices can support [Shoulder-Hip Balance](/en/glossar/schulter-hueft-balance/) without making the outfit feel stiff or overly constructed.

### To emphasize the waist

A defined waist can be highlighted with a shaped jacket, wrap top, belt, or a high-waisted skirt or trouser. The best option is one that feels comfortable when sitting and moving, rather than one that only looks good when standing still.

Useful techniques include:

- Choosing dresses with shaping seams, darts, or a wrap construction
- Wearing a belt at a comfortable natural waist position
- Using a cropped jacket over a dress or high-waisted bottom
- Tucking or partially tucking a top when the fabric is not too bulky
- Selecting pieces with intentional [Waist Emphasis](/en/glossar/taillenbetonung/)

### To keep the hip area comfortable and clean-lined

Fit through the hips matters more than any particular garment type. Trousers, skirts, and dresses should allow movement without pulling, gaping, or creating tension folds.

Softly flared skirts, straight-leg trousers, wide-leg trousers, and dresses with an [A-Line](/en/glossar/a-linie/) shape can offer comfortable room through the lower body. A more fitted pencil skirt can work as well when the fabric has adequate ease and the waistband sits securely.

## Styling Ideas for an A-Figure Body Shape

There is no single best wardrobe for an A-figure. The most useful approach is to decide what you want your outfit to communicate: balance, waist definition, softness, structure, ease, or a stronger fashion statement.

### Everyday outfit formula

Try a well-fitting top with a considered neckline, straight-leg jeans or tailored trousers, and shoes that suit the overall level of visual weight. A tucked-in or French-tucked top can create a clear waistline if that feels right for you.

### Smart casual outfit formula

Pair a blouse or fine knit with a tailored blazer that fits cleanly across the shoulders, then add wide-leg trousers or an A-line skirt. This combination creates structure above the waist while allowing ease below it.

### Dress outfit formula

A wrap dress, fit-and-flare dress, or A-line dress can create a naturally balanced line. Choose the neckline, sleeve length, and fabric based on the occasion and your preferred level of coverage.

For more guidance on the relationship between garment shape and the body, explore [Body Shape](/en/glossar/koerperform/), [Proportion](/en/glossar/proportion/), and [Fit](/en/glossar/passform/).

## Common Misunderstandings

- **Misunderstanding: An A-figure must hide the hips.**  
  **Better:** Hips do not need to be disguised. The goal is a comfortable fit and an outfit that reflects your preferred style.

- **Misunderstanding: Every A-figure has a small waist.**  
  **Better:** A visibly defined waist is common in this proportion pattern, but individual bodies vary. The shoulder-to-hip relationship is the key characteristic.

- **Misunderstanding: Only flared skirts suit an A-figure.**  
  **Better:** Straight skirts, trousers, jeans, fitted dresses, and relaxed silhouettes can all work when their cut, fabric, and fit are right.

- **Misunderstanding: Balance means making both halves of the body look identical.**  
  **Better:** Visual balance is a styling option. You may prefer to emphasize the waist, shoulders, hips, or a favorite feature instead.

## Practical Check When Shopping

Use these questions when trying on clothes for an A-figure body shape:

- Does the garment sit smoothly across the shoulders without slipping or pulling?
- Is there enough room through the hips and seat for walking, sitting, and bending?
- Does the waistband remain comfortable rather than cutting in or sliding down?
- Do the garment’s seams, pockets, prints, or details fall where you want attention to go?
- Does the outfit create the level of shoulder, waist, and hip definition you actually like?
- Can you wear it with shoes and layers you already own?

A quick movement check is especially useful. Walk, sit down, raise your arms, and look at the garment from the side as well as the front. Good [Fit](/en/glossar/passform/) should support both comfort and the intended silhouette.

## Key Line

An A-figure body shape has hips that appear wider than the shoulders, often with a defined waist. Styling can balance these proportions, emphasize them, or simply prioritize comfort and personal expression.
